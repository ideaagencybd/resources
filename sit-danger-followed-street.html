Physical Danger & Threat

You are being followed on the street

high The Exit Mapsafetystreetnight
1

What is happening

You notice the same footsteps behind you through two turns. It is dark, the street is thinning out, and the distance between you has not changed. Your stomach knows before your mind agrees.

2

What is actually going on

  • Your instinct has already done the maths. People routinely override it because being wrong feels embarrassing, and embarrassment is not a real cost compared to the alternative. Act on the read and be wrong cheerfully.
  • You do not need to determine intent. You need to change three variables: whether you are alone, whether you are predictable, and whether you are observed. Any one of them dismantles most of the risk.
  • The goal is not confrontation and it is not proof. It is a boring outcome where nothing happens.
3

Do this, in this order

  1. Confirm cheaply, without stoppingCross the road, or turn a corner you did not need. If they mirror two deliberate changes, you have your answer.
  2. Break the pattern immediatelyDo not continue toward home. Never let a follower learn where you sleep.
  3. Move toward light, noise and peopleA petrol station, a bus with a driver, a pub, a hotel lobby, a taxi rank. Occupied and lit beats short and quiet, always.
  4. Get visibly on a live callRing an actual person and state your street name out loud. Being witnessed is the cheapest deterrent available.
  5. Go inside somewhere staffed and say so plainlyNot a doorway or an alley — a business with employees. Tell staff you think you are being followed and ask to wait.
  6. Call emergency services if closed in onDo not wait for something to happen first. "I am being followed" is a valid call.
4

Words you can borrow

On the phone, loudly
"I'm on Mill Street heading to the Shell garage. I think someone's following me. Stay on the line."
To staff inside
"I think I'm being followed. Can I stand here for a few minutes and can someone walk me to a taxi?"
If they speak to you
"No." Keep walking. You owe strangers nothing, least of all politeness.
If they close distance
Loud, flat, and public: "Stop following me." Volume recruits witnesses and most followers leave the moment they are named.
5

What makes it worse

  • Walking home to check whether you are imagining it. That is the one place you must not lead them.
  • Headphones in, or eyes on your phone screen. Both remove the sense you most need.
  • Turning to confront in an empty street. You are trading your best asset, distance, for certainty you do not need.
  • Cutting through the park because it is faster. Speed is worth nothing next to visibility.
  • Waiting until you are sure. Certainty arrives too late to be useful.
6

Once the pressure drops

  • Tell someone what happened tonight, not next week. Saying it out loud stops it becoming a private thing you doubt.
  • Report it, including descriptions and times. Patterns in an area are built from reports and affect patrols.
  • Walk the route again in daylight and pick your lit options and staffed buildings in advance. Pre-loaded exits work under adrenaline; improvisation does not.
  • Expect to be jumpy for a few days. That is a normal recalibration, not damage.
Where this stops being yours to handle

If you are cornered, grabbed, or a weapon appears, the situation is no longer about avoidance. Property is replaceable — hand over the bag. Make noise, create distance, and run toward people. Your only objective is leaving.

A note on this library

These are general principles written to be useful under pressure, not legal, medical, or financial advice. Laws and emergency procedures differ by country. If life is at risk, contact your emergency services first and read this afterwards.
