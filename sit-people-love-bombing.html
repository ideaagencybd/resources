Manipulation & Bad Actors

Someone new is moving unnaturally fast

medium The Pace Testrelationshiplove-bombingred flagscoercion
1

What is happening

Three weeks in and it is intense: constant contact, enormous declarations, talk of moving in, gifts beyond the stage of the relationship, and mild irritation whenever you are unavailable. It feels wonderful and something in you is uneasy.

2

What is actually going on

  • Intensity is not intimacy. Intimacy is built from accumulated small reliability; intensity can be manufactured in a fortnight by anyone willing to.
  • The pattern to watch is not the flattery — it is the reaction to a small, ordinary boundary. That single test carries most of the diagnostic weight.
  • Fast escalation serves a function: it invests you heavily before you have seen how they behave under mild frustration.
  • Early over-disclosure of trauma creates false closeness and makes later withdrawal feel like cruelty on your part. That effect is sometimes engineered and sometimes just sad.
3

Do this, in this order

  1. Slow it down on purpose and observeNot a rejection — a test. "I want to take this a bit slower" is the cheapest diagnostic available and it costs you nothing with a good person.
  2. Keep your own life runningFriends, gym, family, routine, your own place. The first casualty of this pattern is always your independent life, and it goes quietly.
  3. Decline one small thing earlySay no to a single evening. Watch the response carefully. Fine and warm, or wounded and punishing?
  4. Refuse financial entanglement at this stageNo joint accounts, no lending, no co-signing, no cards. Whatever the story, the timeline is the problem.
  5. Talk to one friend honestly, including the uneasy partSay the doubt out loud. Isolation from candid friends is what allows these patterns to run.
  6. Write down the specific uneaseVague feelings get argued away. A dated note of the exact behaviour does not.
4

Words you can borrow

Setting pace
"I really like this. I want to go slower — I do things better when they build."
The boundary test
"I am seeing friends on Thursday and I am keeping that."
If pushed on commitment
"I am not ready to promise that yet. Not because of you — that is just my timeline."
Naming a bad reaction
"You seem angry that I had one evening to myself. That worries me more than the evening does."
5

What makes it worse

  • Interpreting jealousy as depth of feeling. It is a control signal, not a compliment.
  • Giving up your own home, job, or friendships inside the first months.
  • Explaining away a frightening reaction because the apology afterwards was enormous. The cycle is the pattern.
  • Keeping the doubts from everyone who knows you, so nobody can tell you what they see.
6

Once the pressure drops

  • If they responded to the slow-down warmly, you have learned something genuinely good. Proceed.
  • If they punished it, believe the punishment rather than the apology. The apology is the part that is repeatable at will.
  • Keep one account, one document folder, and one friendship that are entirely yours, permanently. Good relationships are undamaged by this.
  • Re-read your dated notes at three months. It is the clearest picture you will get.
Where this stops being yours to handle

If leaving feels frightening, if your phone or money is monitored, or if you have been cut off from friends and family, that is coercive control. Contact a domestic abuse helpline before you announce any decision — the period around leaving is statistically the most dangerous.

A note on this library

These are general principles written to be useful under pressure, not legal, medical, or financial advice. Laws and emergency procedures differ by country. If life is at risk, contact your emergency services first and read this afterwards.
