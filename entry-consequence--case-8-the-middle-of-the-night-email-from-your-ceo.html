Case 8 — The Middle-of-the-Night Email from Your CEO

486 words

The Situation

It is 11:47 pm. You receive an email from your CEO — not in your normal chain — asking you a specific question about a competitor's move that you saw in the news that afternoon. The email tone suggests she wants a real answer, tonight.

Your Move — Decide Before Reading On

Option A

Reply immediately with your best guess.

What likely happens:

You send an answer that's confident-sounding but has holes. Tomorrow morning at 8 am, when the CEO shares your answer in a strategy meeting, someone in the room asks a follow-up you didn't anticipate — and the holes are exposed. You have delivered fast but unreliable.

What you gained:

Speed. Visibility. The impression of being always on.

What you missed / what could go wrong:

Accuracy. The reputation for reliability that is more valuable than the reputation for speed.

What you should have noticed:

CEOs value reliability over speed on consequential questions. Speed on trivial ones. Do not conflate.

Option B

Reply briefly: 'Good question. Let me spend 30 minutes on it in the morning and reply properly by 7:30 am.'

What likely happens:

The CEO gets a quality answer at 7:30 am with time to absorb before the 8 am meeting. She notes that you responded quickly to her overnight message AND took the time to answer properly. This is a small, memorable positive.

What you gained:

Time to think. Quality answer. Reputation for both responsiveness and rigor.

What you missed / what could go wrong:

Minimal. The CEO can absorb waiting five hours for a proper answer.

What you should have noticed:

'I'll get back to you properly' is a professional response, not a delay. Rushing signals inexperience.

Option C

Do not respond until morning at all. Emails after 10 pm are noise.

What likely happens:

The CEO does not know if you received the message, are on it, or are asleep. At 8 am she asks you in person: 'Did you see my email?' You explain your policy. Depending on the CEO's disposition, this either impresses her (healthy) or annoys her (some CEOs).

What you gained:

Your sleep. Your after-hours boundaries.

What you missed / what could go wrong:

Some CEOs will interpret silence as unavailability rather than as boundary. Know your specific manager.

What you should have noticed:

There is a difference between protecting your evening for real rest and being unreachable. A one-line 'I'll reply properly at 7:30' preserves both.

The Wrap

B is the correct answer for almost every CEO. The one-line acknowledgment costs nothing and buys you the time to answer well. A is the mistake of most people, particularly early-career professionals, who confuse speed with competence. C works in a healthy culture and with a healthy CEO — but 'healthy' is more variable than we like to admit. When in doubt, B is safe. It preserves both your evening and your professional signal.
