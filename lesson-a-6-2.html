AI as a Collaborator

From asking to working with

26 minThink-Ask-Review-Improve-Verify-Use2,820 words22 sections
Skill
Use AI as a partner, not an oracle
Tool
Think-Ask-Review-Improve-Verify-Use
Result
You get useful work out of a model without surrendering judgment
In one line
The model proposes. You dispose.
The idea

The Hook

The first time a model answers you in complete sentences, something primitive in you relaxes.

It sounds like a person who has already done the reading. It is patient. It is never insulted. For a tired human, that is a kind of love. It is also how you get hurt.

A fluent intern who never says "I don't know" is not a sage. It is a risk with a friendly voice. If you treat it as an oracle — ask, receive, paste, done — you have not gained a collaborator. You have gained a way to be wrong at scale, in your own tone.

The resourceful move is unglamorous. You think first. You ask with a job. You review like a skeptic. You improve the brief. You verify the load-bearing parts. Then you use what survived.

The model can be a tutor, a critic, a red team, a simulator, an editor, an analyst. It cannot be the person who is responsible.

The room

The Scenario

Monday, 8:41 a.m. Mara has a ten-page vendor packet and a 9:30 with Elena. She pastes the PDF into an assistant: "summarize this and tell me if we should sign."

Ninety seconds later she has a confident brief: "strong fit," "competitive" pricing, and a number — $47,000 annual — that looks speakable. She does not open the PDF again. She changes the font and walks in.

Elena asks, "Where does the 47 come from?"

The brief does not say. The model had sounded sure. What do you do if you are Mara at 9:31?

Your move

Your Move

Write the sentence you would say to Elena, given that you did not check the number.

Common move. Defend the brief. "The AI pulled it from the packet." You have just told a manager that your judgment is a paste action.

Better move. Stall. "Let me double-check." You still burned the first minute of a decision meeting on a number you cannot source.

Expert move. Separate the draft from the claim. "This is a first-pass brief I have not verified. I should not have put an unsourced number in front of you. Fifteen minutes with the packet." The hit now is cheaper than after a signature.

Resourceful move. You never enter the room that way. Before 9:30 you ran the loop: thought about the actual question, asked for a structured extract, reviewed it against the PDF, sent the model back at the weak spots, verified every figure, then used the brief. The model was a collaborator. You were still the analyst.

Why the common move fails: fluency is not a source. Elena is asking who will be wrong with her if the number is wrong.

The idea

The Big Idea

AI is a collaborator with no skin in the game.

Treat it like a fast intern who will invent a citation rather than sit with uncertainty. Useful — if you keep the loop humans have always used with interns: think, assign, review, send back, check, then ship.

Think → Ask → Review → Improve → Verify → Use.

Skip Think, and you ask the wrong job. Skip Review, and you are hypnotized. Skip Verify, and you put a fluent lie in a room that can hurt someone.

Explained

The Simple Explanation

A language model predicts text that looks right. "Looks right" is not "is true." That is why it can draft a superb outline and invent a statute. Both are fluent continuations.

Think. What is the job? What does good look like? What would a wrong answer cost? What must not be pasted?

Ask. One job per ask. Assign a role.

Review. Read it as if a stranger wrote it to impress you. Highlight every number, name, date, recommendation.

Improve. The second prompt is usually where the work starts. "Attack this. What would a hostile reader say?"

Verify. Every load-bearing claim. 6.4 is the craft of this step.

Use. Only what survived. Using is a human act. It has your name on it.

RoleHired forYou still own
TutorExplain until you can explain it backWhether you understand
CriticFind holes in your draftWhich holes are real
Red teamArgue the oppositeWhether to change your mind
SimulatorPlay the other personWhat you will actually say
EditorTighten, cutThe meaning
AnalystStructure messy inputsThe figures and the conclusion

Not on the list: oracle, lawyer, doctor, ghostwriter of a citation you did not look up.

What's changing. Models as of 2026 can often search, run code, read files, sometimes browse. That reduces some hallucinations and creates new ones (a wrong page, confidently summarized). "Agent" features will keep arriving. A collaborator with tools is still a collaborator. You still verify. 6.8 is where a human stays on the judgment step of anything that can click.

The tool

The Practical Tool

Name: Think-Ask-Review-Improve-Verify-Use

  1. Think. Job in one sentence. Cost of a bad answer. What must not be pasted (secrets, other people's data, credentials, health).
  2. Ask. One job. A role from the table. Audience. Format. "List what you do not know."
  3. Review. Once for usefulness, once for danger. If the highlights are dense, you have a minefield, not a brief.
  4. Improve. Send the highlights back. "Challenge my assumptions. Two alternatives. What would make this wrong?"
  5. Verify. For each highlight: a source you trust, or delete. No orphans.
  6. Use. Paste only the surviving parts, in your voice, with your name.

If a step feels skippable, that is the step that will make the story later.

Fast path

The Fastest Reliable Method

Five minutes, model already open. Keyboard down for thirty seconds. Then:

"Role: critic. Job: [one sentence]. Audience: [who]. Format: [bullets / 150 words]. Flag anything you are not sure of. Do not invent a number. If you don't know, write DON'T KNOW."

Delete every line you cannot check. Use what remains. For anything that can spend money, harm a person, or go on letterhead, you do not skip Verify.

The hack

The Hack

Never ask a model to decide. Ask it to prepare a decision.

"Should we sign?" is how Mara got a $47,000 fiction. "Extract every dollar figure with the page it came from, then list what is missing" is a collaborator brief. Models have no values and no name. They will still give you a verdict, because verdicts are a common shape of text.

AI move

The AI Move

Use AI for: the six roles; a pre-mortem on your draft; turning notes into a structure you then fill with checked facts.

Do not use AI for: a conclusion you will not verify; firing, breaking up, or diagnosing; licensed work without a licensed human in the loop.

Prompt (copy, then fill):

Context: Collaborator, not oracle. I keep judgment.
Role: [tutor / critic / red team / simulator / editor / analyst]
Job: [one sentence]
Audience: [who will see this]
Material:
"""
[paste only what is allowed to leave your machine]
"""
What I already think: [three bullets]
What a wrong answer would cost: [one sentence]

Do the job in the role. Then a section UNCERTAIN: what you do not know, what I should verify, two ways this could be wrong.
Do not invent numbers, citations, or quotes. If a figure is not in the material, write NOT IN SOURCE.
Do not tell me what to decide. Prepare the decision.
Output: [format]. Short. No cheerleading.
Then ask me three questions that would change the output.

What to verify: every figure, every "study shows," every name of a law, every recommendation that sounds like a decision.

Do not paste: passwords, health records, HR files, customer lists, children's data, unpublished financials, other people's messages.

Tech

The Technology Move

The job is "get a useful first pass without surrendering the wheel."

Category: general assistants. Current examples, which will change: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok, Copilot, search-backed tools such as Perplexity, plus whatever is bundled into your office suite next quarter. Some are better at long documents, some at code, some at sitting inside email. None of that crowns a winner. Run 6.1's Job Test.

When not to use the category: when you need a primary source, a legal opinion, a medical plan, or a number that will be spoken in a room. Use the model to prepare. Use a human and an official document to close.

What's changing. Tool-using models and agents blur draft and action — sending the email, editing the sheet, clicking the site. Verify becomes more important, not less. An intern who can talk is a risk. An intern who can click is a larger one.

Mistake

The Common Mistake

People skip Think and Review because the output was fast and polite. A bad intern who takes three days gives you time to notice. A model that answers in nine seconds arrives before skepticism has its coat on. The Pause applies to paste. Hands off the keyboard. Then read.

Second mistake: one role for everything. Hire one role per ask.

Recovery: if you already pasted an unverified brief, do not "fix it" with a second unverified brief. Open the source. "I sent a first pass I had not checked. Here is the corrected version, with sources" is a professional sentence. "The AI said" is not.

Defense

The Defense

The loop fails when you have no source to verify against (then the output is hypothesis, not a brief); when you are using the model as a companion because you are lonely (it will be warm; it is not a clinician or a friend — for crisis in the U.S., 988); when the model is wrong in a way that matches what you wanted to hear. That last one is the expensive error. Red-team on purpose.

This will not make every model reliable. It will make you a person who can use an unreliable one without becoming one.

Council

The Masters' Table

Alan Turing. A machine that talks like a person is demonstrating a performance. Enjoy it. Do not confuse it with a mind that can be responsible.

Richard Feynman. You must not fool yourself, and you are the easiest person to fool. A model that agrees with you is a flattery engine until proven otherwise.

Claude Shannon. More fluent bits are not more information. If the output does not reduce your uncertainty about a checkable fact, it was entertainment.

Grace Hopper. A computer does what you tell it, not what you meant. A lazy ask is a fake collaboration.

Don Norman. A tool that feels like a human will be used like a human. That is a usability trap. Design your own friction — Review — because the product will not.

Synthesis. Talk is cheap. Responsibility is not. A collaborator you cannot fire from the judgment seat is a boss you invented.

Where the council could be wrong. Some tasks are low-stakes enough that a sloppy loop is rational — a joke, a first outline you will rewrite. Do not let that exception colonize the rest of your work. As tools improve, some verification can be delegated to other tools (search, code). Delegating is not disappearing.

Best move

The Best-Possible Response

Back to 9:31.

Calm. You do not blame the model in front of Elena. You also do not defend the number.

Observe. Elena asked a source question. That is a gift. The room is still recoverable.

Define. Stop a bad number from becoming the official number.

Verify. You cannot. So you say so.

Prioritize. Integrity of the figure over dignity of the paste.

Find. The original packet. Not another model as a "source."

Options. Bluff. Blame AI. Or retract and return.

Risks. Retracting costs status this morning. Bluffing costs the contract and your name.

Choose. Retract.

Communicate. "I should not have put that figure in front of you. I used a first-pass extract I had not checked. I do not know if 47 is real. I will come back at 10:15 with every dollar figure, page-cited, and a recommendation that is mine."

Act. Open the packet. Extract numbers with the model in analyst role, then tick each against the page. Improve the qualitative claims. Then Use.

Document. Figure, page, what it includes, what it doesn't.

Adapt. If $47,000 is not in the packet, write that down. Invented numbers are data about your process.

Reflect. Where did fluency feel like knowledge?

Practice

Practice

Fifteen minutes. Take a piece of real work under two pages.

  1. Think: job, cost of being wrong, what you will not paste.
  2. Ask: the prompt, role = critic or editor.
  3. Review: highlight every claim you would not want Elena to question.
  4. Improve: send the highlights back. Demand UNCERTAIN.
  5. Verify: check or delete.
  6. Use: the version you would actually send.
  7. Save both versions. One line: what did the loop catch?
Real life

Real-Life Challenge

Work — the packet. Situation: a long document, a short meeting, a model that will summarize. What most people do: "summarize and recommend." Why it fails: recommendations hide missing numbers. Resourceful response: analyst role, extract claims with locations, you decide. Words to use: "NOT IN SOURCE." Words to avoid: "The AI thinks we should." Tool: the loop, then CSDMH (6.4). What could go wrong: the model cites a page that does not contain the claim. You still open the page.

Family — the medical rabbit hole. Situation: a relative forwards a confident chat about a supplement or a symptom. What most people do: argue, or obey. Why it fails: the model is not a clinician. Resourceful response: "This is a draft of questions for a professional, not an answer." Official guidance and a licensed human. Words to use: "Let's write the questions for the appointment." Words to avoid: "I asked the internet's brain." What could go wrong: delaying real care because a chat was soothing.

Digital — the polite leak. Situation: you want help rewriting a thread that includes a customer's name, a salary, or a child's school. What most people do: paste the whole thread because context helps. Why it fails: context is how secrets travel. Resourceful response: strip identifiers first. The collaborator does not need the real name to fix the tone. What could go wrong: a "temporary" paste into a tool whose retention you have not read.

Bad room

Bad-Room Version

Elena is already in the room with the customer. She reads Mara's 47 out loud as if it were yours. The customer says, "We'll need that in the SOW."

You cannot rewind the sentence. You can still stop it from hardening.

"I want that figure exact before it hits a SOW. I will confirm it from the packet and send a cited line by [time]. If it is different, I will say so immediately rather than lock a wrong number because it was spoken."

If the number was invented: "That figure was a draft error; here is the actual range from pages 4 and 9." You do not say "the model hallucinated" to a customer unless you enjoy watching trust die in a specific way. The customer hired a human. Be the human.

Reflect

Reflection

  • Which role do you actually hire, and which do you pretend you are hiring?
  • Where in the loop do you get lazy — Think, Review, or Verify?
  • What have you pasted in the last month that you would not put on a postcard?
  • When the model agrees with you, do you feel relief or suspicion?
  • Whose name is on the output when it leaves the chat window?
Remember

Remember This

The model proposes. You dispose. If you cannot verify it, you do not get to use it.

Deep dive

Optional Deep Dive

A note on "collaboration" that is not sentimental.

Human collaboration works because the other person can suffer consequences, hold a grudge, and be asked to show their work. A model can do none of that. Anthropomorphizing it is a convenience of language — "ask," "it thinks" — and a cognitive hazard. Use the verbs. Do not grant it a character.

Emily Bender, Timnit Gebru, and colleagues' 2021 paper "On the Dangers of Stochastic Parrots" is often flattened into a culture-war object. The operational point: large language models can look like they understand while they are modeling linguistic form. Fluency is not comprehension. A parrot that can also search the web is still a parrot until you check.

Turing's 1950 question was about indistinguishability in a game, not about whether you should let the game file your taxes. Hopper: the machine is relentlessly literal. Your sloppy ask is a bug you wrote.

Harder drill: take one completed piece of your work this week and run the red-team role: "Argue that shipping this would be negligent." Then decide, as a human, which parts of the attack are real.

Mission

Field Mission

In the next 24 hours, run the full loop once on work that will leave your machine.

Write six words on a card, one for each step. Cross each off. If you skip one, write why. "I was late" is allowed. "I trusted the tone" is the lesson.
