Your landlord tells you to leave

high The Process Checkhousingtenancyrightseviction
1

What is happening

A text, a letter, or a knock: you need to be out by the end of the month. Maybe there are arrears, maybe there was an argument, maybe they want to sell. There is a deadline and it sounds final.

2

What is actually going on

  • In most jurisdictions a landlord cannot simply remove a tenant. There is a required notice, a required form, a court order, and only then enforcement — and each stage takes time.
  • Eviction without that process is illegal eviction, which is a criminal offence in many places, not a civil disagreement.
  • Notice being invalid is extremely common: wrong form, wrong period, missing deposit protection, missing safety certificates. Defects buy you months.
  • Leaving voluntarily because you were told to can remove your rights to help with rehousing, because you may be treated as having made yourself homeless.
3

Do this, in this order

  1. Do not leave and do not hand back the keys yetNot until you understand the process stage. Handing keys back ends your tenancy voluntarily.
  2. Get the notice in writing and photograph itThe document type and date determine everything that follows.
  3. Get specialist housing advice this weekA housing charity or the council's housing options team. This is a technical area where advice changes outcomes dramatically.
  4. Check the notice for validityCorrect form, correct notice period, deposit in a protection scheme, gas safety and energy certificates provided, licence where required.
  5. Keep paying rent if you canArrears strengthen their case and weaken yours. If you cannot pay, get advice on that separately.
  6. Photograph the property condition nowEvery room, dated. Deposit disputes are decided almost entirely on before-and-after evidence.
4

Words you can borrow

To the landlord
"I am taking advice on the notice. I am not going to leave without the proper process, and I will keep paying rent in the meantime."
If they threaten to change the locks
"Changing the locks or removing my belongings would be an illegal eviction. I would report it and I am telling you now so there is no confusion."
To the council
"I have received a notice and I want to register as at risk of homelessness so I do not lose my rehousing rights."
On repairs raised in retaliation
"I reported the damp in March, in writing. I would like it addressed regardless of the notice."
5

What makes it worse

  • Leaving on the day stated in a notice, believing it is a deadline. Usually it is only the earliest date they can begin court action.
  • Withholding rent as leverage. It is the fastest route to a mandatory possession ground.
  • Verbal agreements about leaving early, deposits, or rent reductions. Everything in writing.
  • Missing a court date. Attend; adjournments and defences exist for people who show up.
  • Removing or damaging fixtures on the way out.
6

Once the pressure drops

  • Request the deposit through the protection scheme rather than asking the landlord directly.
  • Get a reference in writing before relations deteriorate further, if the relationship allows.
  • If it was an illegal eviction or harassment, there may be compensation. Get advice; these claims succeed.
  • Keep the whole file for a year: notices, receipts, photos, correspondence.
Where this stops being yours to handle

If locks are changed, utilities cut off, belongings removed, or you are physically prevented from entering, that is illegal eviction. Call the council's tenancy relations or housing enforcement team and the police, the same day.

A note on this library

These are general principles written to be useful under pressure, not legal, medical, or financial advice. Laws and emergency procedures differ by country. If life is at risk, contact your emergency services first and read this afterwards.
