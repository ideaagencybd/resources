Arguments, Not Vibes

Claim, evidence, warrant

24 minThe Three-Part Test610 words22 sections
Skill
Test whether a statement can survive contact
Tool
The Three-Part Test
Result
You can take apart a confident paragraph
In one line
A vibe with a footnote is still a vibe if the warrant is missing.
The idea

The Hook

Someone writes three hundred confident words. There is a chart. There is a percentage. There is the word "clearly." You feel yourself nodding because nodding is cheaper than thinking.

Then you ask, like a mechanic, not like a prosecutor: What is the claim. What is the evidence. Why would that evidence make the claim true.

The third question is the one that makes you useful. It is the warrant — the often-invisible rule that connects the two. Stephen Toulmin made a career of this in argument theory. You need the pocket version.

The room

The Scenario

"Engagement is up 40%, so the new onboarding is a success and we should roll it to all regions Friday."

Claim? Evidence? Warrant? What's missing? (Denominator, comparison window, what "engagement" counts, whether 40% of a tiny base is a sneeze, whether success was defined as engagement.)

Your move

Your Move

Common: "Love this." Better: "What's the n?" Expert: Three-part test, spoken in human: "The 40% is evidence of something. What definition of success did we agree, and why does this number mean we should roll Friday?" Resourceful: Expert plus a reversible rollout (7.2).

The idea

The Big Idea

Most workplace "strategy" is a claim glued to a number with hope.

Explained

The Simple Explanation

Claim — what they want you to believe or do. Evidence — the data, quote, example. Warrant — why the evidence is supposed to license the claim.

Missing denominators, missing comparisons, missing time windows, missing alternative causes — these are warrant holes. Correlation presented as causation is a warrant crime (6.6).

Steelman first (2.7): put the argument in its best form, then test it. Otherwise you are fighting a scarecrow and calling it rigor.

The tool

The Practical Tool

Three lines in the margin of any memo: C / E / W. If W is "because I said so" or "clearly," you do not have an argument yet.

Fast path

The Fastest Reliable Method

Ask: Compared to what?

The hack

The Hack

Circle the verb that jumps from number to decision ("so we should"). That jump is the warrant. Make it walk through security.

Tech

The Technology Move

The number in a sheet you can filter, not a screenshot of a dashboard. Capability: inspectable evidence.

Mistake

The Common Mistake

Being the person who only destroys. After the test, offer a better claim or a test.

Defense

The Defense

Not every human moment is an argument. "I'm scared" does not owe you a warrant. Do not three-part-test a eulogy.

Council

The Masters' Table

Toulmin. The structure of arguments. Feynman. Compare to experiment. Kahneman. The easy story substitutes for the hard warrant. Where wrong. Pedantry as dominance.

Best move

The Best-Possible Response

You say: "40% of what, versus what window, toward what definition of success? If success was 'more people finish setup,' show me that number. Friday all-regions is a one-way door until we have it."

Practice

Practice

Rewrite the onboarding paragraph as an honest argument, or refuse the Friday.

Real life

Real-Life Challenge

News. A "study shows." Claim, sample, warrant. Family. "You don't care." Evidence? Warrant from evidence to character? Work. Any "so we should" in this week's deck.

Bad room

Bad-Room Version

The number is in a CEO slide. You cannot give a seminar. One question: "What would we need to see to not roll Friday?" That is the warrant, inverted.

Reflect

Reflection

  • Where do you let "clearly" do the work?
Remember

Remember This

If you cannot say why the evidence licenses the claim, you do not have a reason. You have a mood with a chart.

Deep dive

Optional Deep Dive

Toulmin's full model has backing, qualifier, rebuttal. Pocket version is enough. Qualifiers ("sometimes," "in this sample") are not weakness. They are manners.

Mission

Field Mission

Take apart one real paragraph today. Send a better version or a question, not a dunk.
