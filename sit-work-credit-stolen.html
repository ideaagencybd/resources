Someone takes credit for your work

medium The Paper Trailreputationpromotionpolitics
1

What is happening

Your manager forwards a summary of the project to leadership. The framing is "the work I led," the analysis is yours, the deck is yours, and your name appears nowhere. Two people reply with congratulations. To them.

2

What is actually going on

  • Credit is the currency that converts into promotion and pay. Losing it once is annoying. Losing it as a pattern is the mechanism by which competent people stay stuck for years while their work travels upward under someone else's name.
  • You cannot generally reclaim credit for a finished piece of work without looking small. What you can do is change the visibility architecture so the next piece cannot be silently reassigned.
  • There is a real difference between a manager who legitimately represents the team's output upward and one who systematically erases contributors. Before you act, decide honestly which you are in.
3

Do this, in this order

  1. Do not reply to the thread with a correctionCorrecting attribution in a leadership thread reads as status anxiety, regardless of how right you are.
  2. Add value where you are visibleReply to the thread with something substantive only you could add — a next step, a risk, a number. Presence beats protest.
  3. Raise it once, directly, without accusationGive them one clean chance to fix it. Many people do this thoughtlessly and are embarrassed when named.
  4. Change how future work is structuredSend your own updates. Present your own findings. Put your name in the document. Copy people. Do this as normal practice, not as protest.
  5. Build a record for review seasonA running file of what you did, when, with links. Reviews are won by whoever has evidence in the room, and memory is not evidence.
4

Words you can borrow

The direct raise
"When the analysis went up it was framed as your work. I did that analysis. I want to assume that was an oversight — can future summaries name who did what?"
Making it about the work
"It helps me to be visible to leadership for my own development. Can I present this part myself next time?"
If it happens again
"This is the second time. I need us to agree now how contribution gets named, because it affects my review."
Claiming space in the moment
"I can take that section — it was my model."
5

What makes it worse

  • Sulking while hoping someone notices the injustice. Nobody is tracking your contribution as carefully as you are.
  • The bitter joke in the group chat. It reaches them, without your reasoning attached.
  • Escalating to their boss on the first occurrence. You spend a lot of capital and are labelled difficult.
  • Quietly reducing your effort as revenge. The only career it damages is yours.
  • Refusing to share work in future. Hoarding gets you managed out.
6

Once the pressure drops

  • Watch what happens after you raise it once. The response tells you whether this is a person problem or a place problem.
  • If the pattern continues after being named, treat it as settled information: this manager will not sponsor you. Plan around that fact rather than relitigating it.
  • Cultivate one relationship outside your reporting line who sees your work directly. Single points of failure for your reputation are dangerous.
  • Keep the evidence file permanently. It is also the raw material for your next CV.
