Boss Fight: The Broken Machine

The process is on fire and everyone is improvising

40 minMap + Checklist + Top 33,763 words23 sections
Skill
Stabilize a failing system
Tool
Map + Checklist + Top 3
Result
You restore a working path and prevent the next fire
In one line
Stabilize the path. Then fix the machine.
The idea

The Hook

The machine was always this brittle. Today you can see it.

The person who "just knew how" is out. The unofficial path is the only path, and it lived in their head. Three people are inventing three workarounds that will not fit together on Monday. A customer is using capital letters. A manager wants a name. A hero is already volunteering to work the weekend, which will teach the machine that fire is a staffing plan.

This is not a thinking puzzle. It is not a people puzzle, not first. It is Level 5's exam: can you execute when the process is on fire — without becoming the new single point of failure?

You have the tools. Top 3. Two-minute start. Checklist. Process map. Five Whys with limits. Owner, outcome, deadline. Upward brief. Definition of done. You will not use them as a museum. You will use them in order: stabilize a path, then fix a machine.

The room

The Scenario

Friday, 1:40 p.m. Intake is on fire.

Priya — the only person who reliably ran quality and the only one who understood the "real" spreadsheet, the one that was not the official one — is out. Family emergency. Back maybe next Thursday. Maybe.

The shared inbox has 41 unread. The form dumped 12 overnight. Sales DMed four "quick ones" this morning, one of them a 24-hour promise to a logo client. Jonah is rewriting the form in a side doc because "the process is stupid." Mara is triaging in a personal spreadsheet she started at 11:00. Sam is answering customers from Slack with guesses. Elena pings you — you are the person who can still see the whole mess — at 1:41:

"I need this stable by end of day. Client X is hot. Don't tell me it's a process problem. I know. Fix it."

Your calendar: a 2:00 you can skip, a 3:30 you cannot, Elena's boss possibly joining a 4:45 "quick pulse." You are not Priya. You do not have her spreadsheet password. You do have the last four weeks of this level in your hands.

The Room, unofficial, is already forming in a thread titled "war room???"

What do you do with the next 90 minutes?

Your move

Your Move

Do not keep reading. Pick A, B, or C as if Elena were waiting. Write the first sentence you would send. Then read the consequences.

Option A — Hero weekend. You take Priya's job. You skip the 3:30. You ask Jonah to stop rewriting the form and "just help you grind the pile." You tell Elena: "I've got it. I'll be in the inbox all weekend." Sam keeps improvising; you will "catch anything wrong" later. No map. No card. You are the path.

Option B — Alignment meeting. You call a 90-minute all-hands at 2:00. Agenda: "get on the same page." You invite sales, Jonah, Mara, Sam, Elena. You try to Five Why the last complaint on a whiteboard until the root cause is "communication." You promise a new process next week. Client X waits. The pile grows during the meeting.

Option C — Stabilize, then fix. You refuse the war-room theater. Ninety minutes: one visible queue, one path, one short checklist, a Top 3 for the fire, an upward brief. Improvisations freeze. Jonah's rewrite parks. Sam gets a script. Client X gets a true date. Priya's absence is treated as a system fact, not a moral failure.

Pick. Then look.

Consequences

Consequence Engine

If you picked A — what happened.

You are a hero until Sunday night. You knock the pile to 12. You also invent a fourth workaround in your own head. Monday, Mara does not know what you did. Sam's guesses have created two commitments you did not see. Jonah is angry you killed the form rewrite without a path. Elena's boss heard "we've got it" and told Client X "24 hours," which you never said. Priya's spreadsheet is still locked. You are now the bottleneck, and you are tired. The machine learned: when it breaks, you will catch it with your body.

Retry from 1:40. A is how 5.B happens again in six weeks. You need a path that survives you going home.

If you picked B — what happened.

The meeting is 110 minutes. Sales defends the 24-hour promise. Jonah lectures. Mara is quiet and furious. You produce a "root cause: communication" and a parking lot of initiatives. Client X emails during minute 80. Nobody is on the inbox. Elena's boss joins the 4:45 and asks for a status. You have a photo of a whiteboard and a larger pile. Five Whys, used as theater, manufactured a slogan.

Retry from 1:40. You may have a meeting later. You may not have it while the building is open to the weather.

If you picked C — what happened.

You are not done. You are in a better game. The rest of this lesson is how to run C without turning it into a quieter A.

If you picked A or B, you are not behind. You found the default. Now run C on purpose.

The idea

The Big Idea

In a fire, the first product is a path, not a diagnosis and not a hero.

Stabilize: one way work gets from in to out, visible, owned, with a floor that prevents the dumbest misses. Then, and only then, map, why/system/incentive, and a change that would survive Priya being out again. If you reverse the order, you will have a beautiful autopsy and a still-burning inbox.

Explained

The Simple Explanation

A broken machine has a pattern.

A hidden path (Priya's spreadsheet, Mara's head, a side DM). A key-person dependency. Improvisation that creates incompatible truths. A promise the machine cannot keep. A manager who needs a name or a feeling of control. A hero offering their weekend.

Deming: the system produced this. Priya out is a special event; a machine that cannot survive Priya out is a common-cause design. Five Whys that end on "Priya was out" have failed 5.5 in public.

Goldratt: the bottleneck just moved. It might be quality (nobody else can check). It might be "who can see the pile." Do not flood a new hero. Protect a constraint you can staff.

Grove: your output is the team's output under this shock. Leverage is a queue, a card, a brief — not your typing speed in an inbox.

The order of operations for this boss fight:

  1. Pause and State Check. Hot rooms make stupid maps.
  2. Top 3 for the fire (not for your career, not for Jonah's form).
  3. One map, crude, current — front doors, pile, missing box (Priya).
  4. One path with Owner, Outcome, Deadline.
  5. One checklist so Sam does not demo-staging the equivalent.
  6. Upward brief so Elena can help in the 4:45.
  7. Ship a Definition of Done for "stable" before you go home.
  8. Why / System / Incentive after the path exists, or as a short note, not as a meeting.

You will use later levels: two-way doors (7.2) for reversible patches; AI (Level 6) for draft SOPs and meeting notes you verify; the Figure-It-Out Loop (3.1) if a step is actually unknown.

The tool

The Practical Tool

Name: Stabilize the Path (Map + Checklist + Top 3, with 5 Whys-with-limits in the second hour)

Hour 1 — Stop the weather.

  1. Pause. You do not type "I've got it."
  2. Day Top 3 for the fire. Example: (1) one visible queue, (2) Client X true date and owner, (3) freeze improvisation with a script. Week Top 3 (later today): backup for Priya's check, one front door, promise rule with sales.
  3. Napkin map. Arrive → one queue → do → check (who, now that Priya is out?) → send. Mark UNKNOWN on the spreadsheet password. Do not wait for the password to start the queue.
  4. Freeze extra paths. Message: "Until Monday 10:00, no side DMs, no personal sheets, no form rewrites. Everything goes here. Jonah, park the rewrite — I need you on the queue, OOD in this thread."
  5. Checklist, five lines, for anyone touching a customer. Identity of the request, what we will not promise, where it lives, who can say a date, what "sent" means. Sam uses it as read-do.

Hour 2 — Make it survive the weekend.

  1. Cover the Priya box. Who can do a minimum check? If nobody, the DoD for the weekend is "we send only X type of work; the rest waits with a dated apology." That is a hard, honest floor. Hero-checking everything is Option A in a mask.
  2. Upward brief to Elena before 4:45. Status, ask, risk, options. Ask: retract or reframe the 24-hour promise. Risk: her boss is about to repeat it. Default: you send Client X a true date with Elena CC'd at 4:30 if silent.
  3. Why / System / Incentive, one page, after the path exists. Cartoon root to reject: "Priya was out." System: quality and the real tracker lived in one head. Incentive: sales is still rewarded for 24-hour language. Changes that survive recasting: backup checker, one queue, promise rule.
  4. Definition of Done for "stable" today: queue visible, Client X dated, improvisation frozen in writing, Elena briefed, weekend floor named, trail left for Monday and for Priya.
Fast path

The Fastest Reliable Method

If you only have 25 minutes before the 3:30:

  1. Create one list. Dump inbox + form + known DMs into it. Oldest at top.
  2. Assign Client X an owner (maybe you) and a true date.
  3. Send the freeze message and a five-line card.
  4. Six-line brief to Elena.

Map prettier after 3:30. Diagnosis after the path. Not instead.

The hack

The Hack

Name "stable" as a product.

If "stable" is a feeling, you will hero until you drop. If "stable" is a DoD, you can go home. Going home is part of the fix. A machine that requires your Saturday is still broken. You are not abandoning people. You are refusing to train the fire.

AI move

The AI Move

AI can draft the freeze message, the five-line checklist, the upward brief, a first-pass map from your walkthrough, and a Monday SOP. It cannot see the inbox. It will invent a tidy process if you let it.

Use AI for:

  • Drafts of the freeze, the script for Sam, the Client X note, the Elena brief
  • Turning a napkin map into a one-page current-state
  • Meeting notes if you must have a 20-minute stand-up (not a 90-minute war room)

Do not use AI for:

  • Promises to Client X
  • The official root cause
  • Anything from Priya's locked sheet you do not actually have

Prompt (copy, then fill):

WHEN TO USE: A process is on fire, a key person is out, I need stabilize-then-fix artifacts.

Facts:
- Key person out until: [date]
- Front doors: [list]
- Pile: [what we know]
- Dangerous promise: [exact words if known]
- Who is improvising: [roles]

Goal:
1) A Day Top 3 for the fire and a Week Top 3 for the machine.
2) A five-line customer-touch checklist.
3) A freeze message (no extra paths).
4) An Upward Brief (status, ask, risk, options, default).
5) A Why/System/Incentive note that rejects "key person was out" as the root.
Constraints: No hero-weekend plan. No 90-minute meeting. Label UNKNOWN. Do not invent client commitments.

Verify every date and name. Do not paste client contents, Priya's personal situation, passwords.

Tech

The Technology Move

The job is "one visible queue and a freeze the tools cannot violate easily."

Category: a single list people can see on their phones, plus a channel topic that states the path. Current examples: a board, a shared sheet with age, an inbox rule that forwards form mail into it. Do not migrate platforms on Friday at 2:00. Do not let Jonah's form rewrite become a stealth migration.

When not to use technology: a new tool as a substitute for a freeze. Tools do not stop DMs. A sentence from Elena does.

Mistake

The Common Mistake

Fixing the machine in theory while the pile eats the customer.

It happens because mapping feels like control, and inboxes feel like shame. Recovery: Top 3 includes one customer-facing close. The map is in service of the path, not a museum of the fire.

The other mistake: becoming Priya. Recovery: if you took the box, name the successor and the date you leave the box. Write it in the brief.

Defense

The Defense

Stabilize-the-path fails when:

  • The work is unsafe to send with a minimum check (legal, medical, money that cannot be reversed). Then the honest floor is "we stop sending that type until a checker exists," not "we ship hope."
  • Power demands a villain by 4:45. You still do not offer Priya or Mara. You offer a role and a path (5.5 Bad Room).
  • You freeze so hard that a real emergency cannot reach you. Include "if Client X-level, ping me" on the card.
  • You skip the Pause and write a cruel freeze. People in improvisation were trying to help. Freeze the paths, not their dignity.

Limitation: you will not repair four weeks of design in 90 minutes. You will make a path that Monday can improve. That is a win.

Council

The Masters' Table

Deming. Do not hunt a worker. Priya's absence revealed the system. Design a machine that can survive a human life.

Grove. Leverage under shock: queue, checklist, brief. Your weekend typing is low leverage.

Goldratt. Do not flood the new constraint. Limit WIP. Oldest first, or Client-X-first if that is the true business rule — name the rule.

Drucker. Contribution today is a stable path, not a perfect process deck.

James Clear. The system you run this afternoon is what the team will copy in the next fire. Heroics are a system. So is a card.

Ohno / Five Whys, with limits. After the path exists, dig. Reject the single cartoon root. System + incentive.

Synthesis. Stabilize, then fix. Path, then cause. People last as villains, first as operators of a visible machine.

Where the council could be wrong. Production metaphors can ignore that Client X is a relationship, not a widget. A true date with a human sentence may matter more than a perfectly oldest-first queue. Do both: a path, and a voice that does not sound like a factory to a frightened customer.

Best move

The Best-Possible Response

Calm. Pause. You do not enter the "war room???" thread with heat. State Check: you can skip the 2:00; you cannot skip your brain.

Observe. Three front doors. No Priya. Four improvisations. One illegal promise. Elena wants stable-by-EOD and is allergic to the word process, which you will translate into path.

Define. Stable = DoD above. Not "inbox zero." Not "Jonah's new form."

Verify. You do not have Priya's password. UNKNOWN. You will not wait.

Prioritize. Client X and one queue beat the form rewrite and the 90-minute meeting.

Find resources / people. Mara (triage skill), Jonah (capacity, if pointed), Sam (scripted), Elena (sales and her boss), whoever can do a minimum check.

Options. A, B, C. You already saw A and B fail. C.

Risks. Elena hears "process" and punishes. So you speak path, date, freeze, ask. Jonah mutinies in public. You take him to OOD in a private line, not a duel in the thread.

Choose. C.

Communicate. Freeze message. Client X note (true date). Upward brief. OOD to Jonah and Mara. Script + checklist to Sam.

Act. Build the list. Move the known items. Two-minute start on Client X, not on a manifesto.

Document. Photo of the napkin map. DoD for stable. Trail for Monday and Priya: what you did, what you did not, where the list lives.

Adapt. If the 4:45 demands a name, you give a role: "Queue owner this weekend is X; quality floor is Y; I own the path until Monday 10:00, then we review." Not a sacrifice.

Reflect. Did you try to become Priya? Did you try to hold a seminar?

That is a resourceful human in a broken machine. Not a hero. A restorer of a path.

Practice

Practice

Twenty minutes. Use the scenario, or a real fire if you have one.

  1. Write the fire Top 3.
  2. Draw the five-box napkin map. Mark UNKNOWN.
  3. Write the five-line checklist.
  4. Write the freeze message (≤8 lines).
  5. Write Elena's Upward Brief.
  6. Write Why / System / Incentive in six lines, rejecting "Priya was out."
  7. Write DoD for "stable by EOD."

If you picked A or B earlier, do not rewrite history. Note what those options optimized (identity, relief) and what they cost (Monday).

Real life

Real-Life Challenge

Work — key person out. Situation: the unofficial owner of a process is gone; people are improvising. What most people do: hero, or a long meeting. Why it fails: the machine learns the wrong lesson. Resourceful response: Stabilize the Path; brief up; honest floor. Words to use: "One queue. One path. Here is what we will not promise." Words to avoid: "I've got it," "root cause is Priya," "war room." Tool: Map + Checklist + Top 3, then 5 Whys-with-limits. AI assist: drafts; you verify dates. What could go wrong: a freeze nobody with power enforces. Your ask to Elena is the enforcement.

Family — the household keystone is down. Situation: the person who "just does" school, food, or bills is sick or away. What most people do: chaos, then blame. Why it fails: the path still lives in one head. Resourceful response: a one-page map of the next 72 hours, a door checklist, a Top 3 for the house, one owner per outcome. Words to use: the three lines of OOD. Words to avoid: "How did we not know?" as a first move (save it for after the path). Tool: same kit, gentler voice. What could go wrong: a meeting about fairness while the child is unfed. Path first. Fairness is the after-action.

Travel — the itinerary breaks. Situation: a cancellation, a lost connection, a group looking at you. What most people do: five people, five apps, five bookings. Why it fails: incompatible improvisations. Resourceful response: one owner, one next flight/train, a five-line card (tickets, passports, meeting point), a brief to the group. Words to use: "One path. Don't book until I say. Meet here at T." Words to avoid: "Figure it out and tell me." Tool: Top 3 + freeze + checklist. What could go wrong: you freeze too long. Time-box the decide.

Bad room

Bad-Room Version

Worse than 1:40.

Elena's boss is already on the 4:45. Client X has threatened the contract (you have seen this heat since 1.1). Jonah posts: "This company is a joke." Sales wants you on a call right now to "reassure." You still do not have the spreadsheet password. Sam just promised something in Slack you cannot see yet.

Pause. Shoulders. You will not fight Jonah in public. You will not join the sales call unprepared.

Sequence under worse:

  1. Holding sentence to Elena: "I'll have a path and a Client X date by 4:30, in six lines you can use upstairs. I will not be on the sales reassurance call until we have a date that is true."
  2. Someone (Mara) hunts Sam's Slack promise as a Top 3 fuse.
  3. You still do C, smaller: queue, freeze, checklist, brief. Jonah gets a private OOD, not a debate on the company.
  4. If the contract is truly on the table, the brief says so as risk, with options, not as panic. Facts vs Stories: what did Client X actually write?

The Pause is still the first move of a Bad Room. The path is the second. Your dignity is not a third Top 3 item. It is the way you do the first two.

Reflect

Reflection

  • Which option did you actually want — A, B, or C — and what identity was it serving?
  • Where are you currently Priya for someone else's machine?
  • What would "stable" mean, in ticks, in your real job?
  • Who improvises in your shop when the official path fails?
  • If you disappeared on a Friday, what would catch fire, and is that on a map?
Remember

Remember This

Stabilize the path. Then fix the machine. Do not become the new hidden spreadsheet.

Deep dive

Optional Deep Dive

Fires teach. They teach the wrong thing unless you pick.

A hero weekend teaches: the official path is optional, and one body is the backup plan. A war room teaches: when we are scared, we talk. A freeze-plus-queue teaches: work has one door, even when we are scared. Next Friday's people will copy today's lesson. That is why going home after a real DoD of "stable" is not laziness. It is instruction.

Incident command in other fields (emergency response, aviation, some hospitals) separates command of the scene from investigation and from the daily org chart. You can steal that without a vest: one path-owner for the fire, a short operational period (until Monday 10:00), then a review. Do not let the fire-owner become the permanent king. Write the end date in the brief.

On Five Whys in incidents: do them after people have a path, or you will harvest stories under threat. Psychological safety is not a poster here. It is whether Jonah can say "I rewrote the form because I could not see the work" without being the villain. That sentence is a system clue.

You will take this into Level 6 when the "machine" is digital, Level 7 when the patch is a decision you cannot reverse, Level 8 when the useful move is to stop carrying a machine that should not be yours. For tonight, the exam is smaller and harder: a queue, a card, three true priorities, a brief, a floor called done.

If you want a harder variation, run a premortem (7.4, early): "It is Monday 10:00 and this fire is worse. Why?" Write five because-sentences. Most of them will be "we were heroes" or "we held a meeting" or "we never froze the DMs." That list is your checklist.

Mission

Field Mission

In the next 48 hours, pick a real brittle process — work or home — and write a one-page "if the key person is out" note: map, five-line checklist, fire Top 3, who owns the path, DoD for stable.

Send it to one other person who lives in that machine. If you are the key person, that is the point. Get out of being the hidden spreadsheet before the family emergency does it for you.

Proof, four lines:

  1. The machine
  2. The hidden path you named
  3. Who else can run the floor
  4. What you will not promise when it breaks
