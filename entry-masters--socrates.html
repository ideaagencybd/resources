30 Thinkers Worth Sitting With · Ancient Wisdom

Socrates

207 words

5th century BCE, Athens

The question is more powerful than the answer.

What They Contributed

Socrates left no writings. What we know comes primarily from Plato and Xenophon. His method — the elenchus — was to ask questions that led interlocutors to expose the incoherence of their own confident beliefs. He reportedly said (via Plato) that 'the only true wisdom is in knowing you know nothing.' The specific attribution is disputed, but the practice of intellectual humility as the beginning of learning is unmistakably his.

How To Apply It Now

In any professional context where someone speaks with confidence, ask the Socratic question. 'How would you know if you were wrong?' 'What definition of X are you using?' 'What are the assumptions underneath that conclusion?' This is not aggression — it is the mildest, most effective form of quality control ever developed. Used well, it makes rooms smarter.

Where Their Thinking Breaks

The Socratic method, deployed without warmth, is annoying. Used to score points, it destroys trust. Used against a superior in the wrong context, it is career-limiting. Match the technique to the culture and the moment.

One Book Or Source

Plato, Apology and Meno — the two most direct sources for the Socratic method in action.
