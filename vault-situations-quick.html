50 Real-Life Situations, Quick Reference

50 Real-Life Situations

1,258 words From book

The specific moments the book was designed for.

Below: fifty situations you will meet in a working life, with the first useful move to make in each one. Not the full playbook — the first move. When the moment arrives, the first move is what matters.

#SITUATIONFIRST MOVE
1Starting a new jobFirst 30 days: listen more than you speak. Ask each person the same four questions (Level 4, Final Unknown). Do not commit to any 'improvements' until day 30.
2A new managerAsk directly: 'What does success look like from your seat? What would make me easy to work with? What would make me hard?'
3Being promoted to manage former peersHave a 1:1 with each within week 1. Acknowledge the awkwardness openly. Ask what they need from you now that the relationship has changed.
4An impossible deadlineNever accept silently. 'To hit this, I would need to deprioritize X. Is that acceptable?' Trade scope, timeline, or resources — not quality.
5Unclear instructionsReverse-brief before starting. 'To make sure we're aligned, here's what I heard: [X]. Is that right?'
6A difficult customer or clientLMR (Level 4). Do not defend. Ask them to walk through the specific issue. Only then respond.
7Public criticismDo not respond in the moment. 'Thank you — that's specific enough for me to work with. Let me sit with it and come back to you.'
8Office politicsRead informal power (Level 4.6). Route through allies for sensitive proposals. Never write in email what you'd say in private.
9A colleague taking credit for your workDo not confront in public. In private, direct: 'I noticed yesterday you presented X. I want to make sure our shared manager knows the specific parts I owned.'
10Being blamed for something you didn't causeDo not defend by counter-blaming. Own the piece that's genuinely yours. Ask calmly for the specific facts. Then propose the next constructive action.
11A visible mistakeOwn it in the first sentence. Not the second. Propose the corrective action. Then follow through.
12Presenting to executivesPREP structure. Point in the first 15 seconds. Anticipate the three sharpest questions.
13Salary negotiationNever name your number first. Do the five-column prep. Silence after your ask.
14Asking for a promotionDo not ask casually. Prepare a written case: what you delivered, what impact it had, what the next level looks like, why now.
15Managing a micromanagerOver-communicate. Send proactive updates before they're asked for. Slowly, trust will build — or the pattern will reveal itself as unchangeable.
16Managing an absent managerAsk for the minimum scaffolding: one 30-minute weekly 1:1, a decision-authority document, a monthly written check-in.
17Handling a public failureOwn it fast. Post-mortem publicly. What we learned, what we're changing, when we'll show progress. Then execute.
18A job interviewResearch the company for 30 minutes. Prepare three stories that answer 'tell me about a time when.' Have three intelligent questions ready.
19A job rejectionSend a genuine thank-you note. Ask for one piece of specific feedback. Follow up in six months if the role was a good fit — companies re-open.
20Sudden job lossGive yourself 48 hours before making any decision. Then: list all severance/benefits carefully. Reach out to 20 people in your network in week 1. Don't hide it — most help comes from people who know you're looking.
21Entering a foreign countryOffline maps downloaded. Emergency numbers saved. Passport copy in encrypted cloud AND printed. Local currency for first 24 hours.
22Getting lostAsk three people, not one. If two of the three agree, that's your direction.
23Missed flightGo to a physical desk while calling the airline simultaneously. The first to succeed wins. Ask about the next flight AND the flight after — sometimes the second is cheaper to change to.
24Language barrierLearn 40 phrases before travel. Download offline translation. Show, don't tell — pictures and gestures work when words don't.
25Phone battery dyingScreenshot your address, tickets, and directions immediately. Power down non-essential apps. Ask hospitality staff for a charge.
26Lost documentsPassport: local embassy, immediately. Card: bank's international line. ID: police report first (needed for replacements).
27Suspicious offerIf it feels too good, it is. Never send money. Never share credentials. Call the institution directly — using the number on their official site, not the one in the offer.
28Financial emergency abroadWire from trusted person is fastest. Bank's fraud line is second. Never use unofficial money-changers in stressful moments.
29Difficult family discussionNot over text. Not on holidays. Book time. Open with 'I want to talk about X because our relationship matters to me.'
30Disagreement with a friendAddress within 72 hours. Direct, not through a third party. Own your part first.
31Setting boundariesState the boundary once, clearly, without justification. 'I'm not able to X.' Then hold. Repetition, not explanation.
32Making an apologyName the specific behavior. Do not add 'but.' Do not explain why. Ask what would help.
33Being rejected48 hours of grief. Then: what did I learn, and what would I do differently? Then: move.
34Major purchaseWait 24 hours. Compare three options. Read one thoughtful review. Then decide.
35Difficult financial decisionDecision 7. Sleep on it. If irreversible, get one independent opinion before deciding.
36A financial scam attemptNever act on urgency. Verify by calling the institution directly. Report to your country's fraud authority.
37Confusing contractNever sign under time pressure. Read it aloud once. Highlight anything you don't understand. For large commitments, one hour with a lawyer or advisor is cheap.
38Phishing attempt5-second pause. Verify sender. Do not click. Go to the site directly.
39Hacked accountChange email first (it resets everything). Enable 2FA everywhere. Check security logs. Notify your bank and IT if relevant.
40Suspicious attachmentDo not open. Verify with sender through a different channel (call, text). If work: forward to IT with subject 'suspected phishing.'
41Data breach at a service you useChange password there and anywhere you reused it. Enable 2FA if not already. Monitor accounts for unusual activity.
42AI hallucination in your reportOwn it fast with the client. Send correction. Update your protocol so it doesn't recur.
43Broken software mid-taskReproduce the exact steps. Document what happens. Try one workaround. If not fixed: escalate with the reproduction steps, not the frustration.
44Messy spreadsheet inheritedDo not try to understand it all. Copy raw data into new file. Answer ONE question at a time. Document as you go.
45Broken processMap, measure, move one step (Level 5). Do not redesign the whole thing.
46Recurring error5 whys with evidence. Stop at the factor you can change.
47No documentationAsk three people. Combine what they say. Write the documentation. Share it. You are now the person who documented it.
48Contradictory evidenceWhich source has the strongest track record? What are the incentives of each? Where do they actually disagree — vs. seem to?
49Everyone agrees too quickly'What are we missing?' Ask it once. Then be silent for 7 seconds. Someone will fill it.
50A crisis where you're the least experiencedAsk questions instead of proposing answers. Observation from a new pair of eyes is often what the experienced team is missing.

Remember This
