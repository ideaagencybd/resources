Physical Danger & Threat

You are cornered by a group

critical The De-escalation Ladderviolencerobberycritical
1

What is happening

Three or four of them, spread out around you, one doing the talking while the others move to your sides. There is a question being asked — the time, a cigarette, where you are from — and the question is not the point.

2

What is actually going on

  • The spreading out is the tell. It is positioning, and it means the encounter has already been decided as an event rather than a conversation.
  • You cannot win against a group and should not try. Everything you carry is worth less than the next ten minutes of your physical integrity.
  • Groups behave according to an audience dynamic — the talker is performing for the others. Removing yourself as entertainment is more useful than any answer you could give.
3

Do this, in this order

  1. Do not let the circle closeMove immediately toward the widest gap and toward light. Position is the only variable that still favours you.
  2. Give up property instantly and visiblyPhone, wallet, watch, bag. Throw it slightly to one side rather than handing it over — attention follows the object and it creates a moment of space.
  3. Keep your hands open and your voice flatNo sudden reaches, including toward your own pocket. Announce any movement before you make it.
  4. Do not answer the identity questionsWhere you are from, who you know, what team you support. Every answer is a hook. Stay bland and keep moving.
  5. Make noise once you have any spaceShout, set off an alarm, hit a car. Groups depend on privacy and disperse under attention.
  6. Run toward people, not toward homeDistance and witnesses. Do not lead them anywhere that matters to you.
4

Words you can borrow

Compliance
"Take it, it's yours." Flat, no eye contact held, no negotiation.
Announcing movement
"Phone's in my right pocket. I'm getting it now."
Refusing the hook
"I'm not from anywhere, mate, I'm just going home." Then move.
Recruiting attention
Anything loud and specific: "Call the police!" — directed at a window, a car, a doorway.
5

What makes it worse

  • Reaching for anything without narrating it. Sudden movements toward pockets get you hurt.
  • Fighting for a phone. Replaceable. Teeth, eyes and skulls are not.
  • Backing into a doorway or an alcove. That completes their work for them.
  • Any insult, even under your breath, and any parting comment as you leave.
  • Standing still to appear unafraid. Stillness inside a closing circle is the worst option available.
6

Once the pressure drops

  • Get somewhere staffed and call the police. Provide clothing, numbers, direction of travel — not faces, which memory reconstructs badly under stress.
  • Cancel cards, remote-wipe and lock the phone, change the passwords for anything that auto-logs-in on it. Do this within the hour; the device is a key to your accounts.
  • Tell someone tonight. Do not go home and process a robbery alone.
  • Expect anger at yourself for complying. Complying is what worked — you are reading this, and that was the objective.
Where this stops being yours to handle

If you are being physically attacked and cannot escape, protect your head, stay off the ground if at all possible, make continuous noise, and move toward any light or crowd at the first gap. Get medical attention afterwards even if you feel fine — head injuries and internal injuries are routinely missed in the adrenaline window.

A note on this library

These are general principles written to be useful under pressure, not legal, medical, or financial advice. Laws and emergency procedures differ by country. If life is at risk, contact your emergency services first and read this afterwards.
