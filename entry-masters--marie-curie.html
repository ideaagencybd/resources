Marie Curie

163 words

1867–1934, Poland / France

Careful measurement, patient work, and evidence over reputation.

What They Contributed

The only person to win Nobel Prizes in two different sciences (physics and chemistry), Curie's work on radioactivity was performed under conditions of poverty, sexism, and eventual health destruction (radiation exposure). Her method was extraordinary care in measurement, patient repetition, and complete indifference to received wisdom that did not survive the evidence.

How To Apply It Now

For research work (Level 3.2) and any evidence-based professional practice: Curie's discipline is the model. Do not accept authoritative claims that don't survive measurement. Repeat experiments until the result is stable. Publish carefully. Do not overclaim.

Where Their Thinking Breaks

Curie's era's radiation exposure was catastrophic. 'Fearless investigation' has costs; some evidence-hunts should include safety precautions the original researchers didn't have. Match the tolerance for risk to the modern understanding, not the historical example.

One Book Or Source

Marie Curie: A Biography by Barbara Goldsmith (2005) or Curie's own Autobiographical Notes.
