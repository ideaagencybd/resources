  • Check the source, not just the wording.
  • Check the date/version for changing information.

Spreadsheet & Data Field Guide

You do not need to become a data scientist to become useful with data. You need enough fluency to inspect, calculate, compare, summarize, visualize, and question the result.

Digital Self-Defense Field Guide

Security is not a specialist-only concern. NIST CSF 2.0 frames cybersecurity as a risk-management discipline, and CISA emphasizes strong passwords, MFA, and phishing awareness. Prefer phishing-resistant MFA when available and appropriate.

Account Security

Use unique strong credentials, a password manager, MFA, and a recovery method you have tested.

Phishing

Treat unexpected urgency, unusual payment requests, credential prompts, and suspicious links as signals to slow down and verify.

Device Security

Keep software updated, use screen lock, encrypt when available, and know what to do if a device is lost.

Backups

Important files should have a recovery path. A backup you have never tested is an assumption, not a guarantee.

Privacy

Before sharing data with a tool, ask what the data contains, who can access it, how long it may be stored, and whether you actually need to share it.

AI Security

Prompt injection can cause an AI system to follow malicious or unintended instructions, particularly when external content is ingested or tools are connected. Treat AI outputs and retrieved content as untrusted inputs until verified.

Negotiation Field Guide

Negotiation is not verbal combat. It is structured problem-solving between people who have different interests, constraints, and alternatives. BATNA means Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement: what you will do if no deal is reached.

Interests

Ask what each side actually needs, fears, values, or is trying to avoid.

Positions

A position is what someone says they want. It is the opening surface, not the full problem.

BATNA

Know your alternative before you negotiate. A weak alternative makes you more dependent on agreement.

Trade-offs

Trade things that differ in value to each side rather than giving away value for free.

Questions

Use questions to diagnose before proposing. Good questions reduce unnecessary conflict.

Silence

Silence creates space. Do not fill every pause just because you feel uncomfortable.

Documentation

Important agreements should be written in plain language: owner, deliverable, price, date, exceptions, and next step.

Resourceful Human Cheat Sheet

01. Pause before you prove you are right. — Use the 10-Second Pause.

02. Turn vague work into the next physical action. — Ask: “What can I literally do next?”

03. Never research without a stopping rule. — Define what evidence would be enough to act.

04. When stuck, find someone who has already solved it. — Use the Expert Request.

05. When everyone agrees, search for the missing downside. — Run a mini-premortem.

06. When AI answers confidently, verify the important parts. — Claim → Source → Date → Cross-check.

07. When a process fails repeatedly, stop blaming people first. — Map the system and incentives.

08. When a conversation gets hot, slow the tempo. — Name the issue before solving it.

09. In negotiation, protect the relationship and your alternative. — Know your BATNA.

10. Make important work visible. — Document decisions, owners, dates, and assumptions.

11. Make good behavior easy and bad behavior harder. — Change the environment, not just your willpower.

12. Do the smallest useful version first. — Create a safe test before a big commitment.

13. Separate what is true from what is useful. — Evidence first; interpretation second.

14. Separate a bad outcome from a bad decision. — Judge process with the information available then.

15. The best tool is the one you can verify and control. — Capability > brand.

16. Never automate an unclear process. — Simplify before automating.

17. If the downside is catastrophic, add a guardrail. — Use approval, backup, or human review.

18. Ask “and then what?” before acting on attractive plans. — Second-order thinking.

19. When confused, write down what you know. — Then list the unknowns.

20. When overwhelmed, choose the next useful thing. — Shrink the horizon.

21. When criticized, extract the signal before defending yourself. — Ask: “What part is useful?”

22. When you need certainty, check whether certainty is actually available. — Use ranges and scenarios.

23. When a mistake happens, close the loop. — Fix, document, notify, prevent recurrence.

24. When two people disagree, ask what each sees that the other misses. — Use disagreement as information.

25. When you have influence, increase clarity and reduce fear. — People execute better when they understand the why and the next step.

26. Do not confuse complexity with sophistication. — Prefer one good model over five mediocre ones.

27. The goal is not to avoid all failure. — It is to fail cheaply and learn quickly where possible.

28. Use AI to expand thinking, not outsource judgment. — Ask it to challenge, compare, and expose uncertainty.

29. Protect your recovery systems. — Back up accounts, documents, contacts, and critical information.

30. A calm person can create options. — Regulation is operational capacity.

31. Wisdom begins where optimization stops. — Ask what actually matters.
