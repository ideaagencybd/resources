Solve What Keeps Breaking

20 min drill1,160 words22 sections
The room

The Hook

A recurring error is rarely solved by saying “be more careful.” Look at handoffs, incentives, ambiguity, tools, and workload.

Your move

Your Move

Before reading the answer, write what you would do. The objective is not to guess the instructor’s preferred words. The objective is to expose your current default behavior.

The idea

The Big Idea

Find systemic causes is a capability you can train. The useful distinction is between knowing the concept and being able to deploy it under pressure. This lesson gives you one primary tool — 5 Whys + Process View — because a single reliable action pattern is easier to remember and more likely to survive a stressful day.

The tool

The Practical Tool

  • Describe the failure.
  • Ask why without blaming a person.
  • Trace the process and handoffs.
  • Look for multiple contributing causes.
  • Fix the strongest leverage point.
Fast path

The Fastest Reliable Method

When time is short, do only this: use 5 Whys + Process View once on one real problem. Do not practice on an imaginary problem if a safe real problem is available. The fastest route to competence is a small loop that creates feedback.

Note

BEST SHORTCUT Map the system and incentives.

The hack

The Hack / Shortcut

Protect your recovery systems. Back up accounts, documents, contacts, and critical information.

AI move

The AI Move

Use AI as a collaborator, not an authority. A strong pattern is: “Here is the context. Here is the goal. Here are the constraints. Here is an example. Give me a practical first pass. Then identify assumptions, uncertainty, and what I should verify.” For high-stakes outputs, check primary sources or qualified professionals where appropriate. Current AI products can support explanation, drafting, reasoning and web-supported work, but capabilities and availability change over time.

Note

AI PROMPT Act as a practical coach for the skill “Solve What Keeps Breaking”. Context: [paste my situation]. Goal: [desired result]. Constraints: [time, money, people, rules]. Examples: [paste one good example if available]. Output: first give the best next actions in plain language; then show one alternative; then list assumptions, uncertainties, and exactly what I should verify. Do not invent facts. Ask only the questions that materially change the answer.

Tech

The Technology Move

Choose technology by capability, not brand. Ask what job it does, what data it touches, what permissions it needs, what happens if it fails, and how you can export or replace it. A tool is valuable when it reduces friction without weakening verification, privacy, or human control.

Mistake

Common Mistake

The most common failure is treating the tool as a substitute for attention. People often reach for a framework before defining the actual problem, or reach for AI before checking whether the task is high-stakes. The resourceful response is to shrink the problem, identify what must be true, and then choose the simplest tool that improves the result.

Defense

Defense / Limitation

Every tool has failure modes. Overconfidence, context loss, poor incentives, stale information, tool mismatch, privacy leakage, and automation errors can all turn a useful method into a liability. Good practice includes an explicit verification step. For AI systems, prompt injection and malicious external instructions are recognized risks, especially when AI can interact with external content or tools.

Council

Great Minds Intelligence Council — the Masters’ Table

  • Leonardo da Vinci: observe closely and stay curious about the mechanism.
  • Herbert Simon: work within bounded rationality and use satisficing when appropriate.
  • Deming: look at the system, not only the individual.

Where could the Council be wrong? No framework is a substitute for context. Philosophical lenses are tools for questioning, not evidence about a modern event. Use them to generate better questions, then test the questions against reality.

Simulation

The Scenario

You are dealing with a realistic find systemic causes problem. A colleague, client, manager, family member, or system has introduced uncertainty. Time is limited. You do not have every fact. The pressure makes the first emotional impulse attractive. Your job is to use 5 Whys + Process View without becoming mechanical.

What Most People Do

They rush to explain, defend, search randomly, or choose the first visible option. The move is understandable because it reduces uncertainty quickly. It is also how people turn a manageable problem into a larger one.

Why it Often Fails

The response optimizes for immediate emotional relief instead of the underlying objective. It may hide assumptions, skip verification, or create a second problem while solving the first.

The Resourceful Response

Slow the first move enough to define the situation. Identify what is known, what is unknown, who can help, and what outcome matters. Then make the smallest useful move that increases information or reduces risk.

The Best Next Action

Write one sentence: “Right now, the most useful thing I can do is ______ because ______ .” Then do that action and observe what changed.

Words to Use

  • “Let me separate what we know from what we are assuming.”
  • “What outcome matters most right now?”
  • “Here is what I can verify.”
  • “I do not know yet, but I know the next step to find out.”

Words to Avoid

  • “Obviously…”
  • “Everyone knows…”
  • “It is definitely…” when it is not.
  • “That is impossible.” before checking alternatives.

What Could Go Wrong

The tool can be applied too rigidly, too slowly, or without regard to power and stakes. Under severe time pressure, safety may require decisive action before full information is available. A good process is adaptive: use more verification for high-consequence decisions and less ceremony for reversible low-stakes ones.

Retry

Now imagine your first move produced an unexpected consequence. What would you change? A capable person does not treat the first plan as sacred. They treat feedback as information.

Practice

Practice

  • Apply 5 Whys + Process View to one real situation you are currently facing.
  • Explain the result in three sentences to someone else.
Mission

Field Mission

Within the next 48 hours, use 5 Whys + Process View on one real, safe problem. Capture the before state, the action, and the result. The mission is complete only when you produce a visible result: a decision, message, checklist, process change, conversation, verified answer, or completed action.

Note

MISSION EVIDENCE What I changed: ______ Result: ______ What surprised me: ______

Bad room

Bad-room Version

The same skill now appears under pressure: the deadline is shorter, emotions are stronger, information is incomplete, and someone is watching. Do not add five new frameworks. Strip the problem back to the smallest safe next move. 5 Whys + Process View is your anchor, not your cage.

Reflect

Reflection

  • What did I notice sooner this time?
  • Where did I make an assumption?
  • What was the highest-leverage action?
  • What would I do differently next time?
Remember

One-line Memory

Note

REMEMBER THIS When this situation appears, use 5 Whys + Process View before reaching for complexity.

Deep dive

Optional Deep Dive

Advanced learners: compare your first instinct with the disciplined process. Identify where speed helps and where speed harms. Ask what information would materially change the decision. Then write a personal “if-then” rule for future use.
