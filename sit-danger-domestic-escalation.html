Physical Danger & Threat

Someone at home is becoming dangerous

critical The Exit Mapdomestic abusecriticalplanning
1

What is happening

The pattern is now familiar. The mood turns, the accusations start, the doors get blocked, objects get thrown or broken. You have started managing your own behaviour continuously to prevent the next episode.

2

What is actually going on

  • Escalation over time is the single most reliable predictor in this situation. The incidents do not usually plateau on their own, and the apology phase is part of the cycle rather than a break from it.
  • The behaviour is not caused by what you did or failed to do. The rules change precisely so that they cannot be satisfied — that is the mechanism of control, not a failure of your compliance.
  • The period around leaving is statistically the most dangerous. This is not an argument for staying; it is an argument for leaving with a plan rather than in the middle of an incident.
  • Certain markers correlate strongly with serious harm: strangulation at any point, threats to kill, weapons in the house, stalking, jealousy-driven monitoring, threats about children or pets, and escalation during pregnancy or after separation. If any are present, treat this as urgent rather than ongoing.
3

Do this, in this order

  1. During an incident, do not argue the factsReduce stimulus. Agree with whatever is cheap. Your objective inside the episode is only to get through it without injury.
  2. Keep yourself out of enclosed roomsStay near an exit. Avoid kitchens and bathrooms — hard surfaces, hard objects, no way past. Do not let yourself be positioned away from the door.
  3. Have a go-bag somewhere outside the houseAt a friend's, at work, in a locker. Documents, medication, spare keys, cash, chargers, copies of children's papers. Inside the house it can be found and used as leverage.
  4. Set up a code word with two peopleOne text or one word that means call for help now and do not reply with questions.
  5. Document quietly and store it externallyDates, photographs of injuries, screenshots of messages, medical visits. In a cloud account they do not know exists, or with someone you trust. Never on the shared device.
  6. Call a domestic abuse service before you need to leaveThey do safety planning for exactly this, confidentially, and they know what the local protections actually are. This is planning, not commitment.
4

Words you can borrow

Inside an episode
Whatever ends it. "Okay." "You're right." "I'm sorry." These are tools, not concessions, and using them is not weakness.
To the code-word contact
Agreed in advance, e.g. "Did you find my blue jacket?" — meaning: call the police to my address now.
To emergency services when you cannot speak freely
Many services support a silent call procedure. Learn your local one now. Otherwise: state the address first and leave the line open.
To a doctor or nurse
"I am not safe at home." That sentence opens a support pathway everywhere in healthcare.
5

What makes it worse

  • Announcing that you are leaving, mid-incident. That is the highest-risk moment to disclose a plan.
  • Keeping the evidence on a device they can access, or in a shared account.
  • Accepting that the calm, remorseful phase proves it has ended. Measure the pattern over months, not the current mood.
  • Handling it entirely alone to protect their reputation or the family's image. Isolation is the condition this behaviour needs to continue.
  • Waiting for physical injury to count it as real. Control, threats, financial restriction and monitoring are the same phenomenon.
6

Once the pressure drops

  • Get legal information about occupation and protective orders, and about children, from someone specialist. General advice from friends is often wrong in ways that cost you.
  • Change passwords, check devices and cars for tracking, and turn off location sharing you forgot existed — including on shared accounts and family plans.
  • Assume the story told about you afterwards will be unflattering. Your documentation matters more than your explanations.
  • Get support for yourself specifically. The recalibration of what is normal takes real time and is much harder alone.
Where this stops being yours to handle

If you are in immediate danger, call emergency services now. If strangulation has ever happened, if there are weapons in the house, or if there have been threats to kill, treat this as an emergency and contact a domestic abuse service or the police today — not when it next happens. These markers are associated with the most serious outcomes.

A note on this library

These are general principles written to be useful under pressure, not legal, medical, or financial advice. Laws and emergency procedures differ by country. If life is at risk, contact your emergency services first and read this afterwards.
