First Principles, Used Lightly

Go underneath without becoming a philosophy major

24 minMust / Maybe / Myth698 words22 sections
Skill
Strip a problem to what must be true
Tool
Must / Maybe / Myth
Result
You stop copying other people's solutions by default
In one line
Copying is fast. Musts are slower and sometimes the only way through.
The idea

The Hook

Someone says "that's just how we do it."

Sometimes they are handing you a hard-won must. Sometimes they are handing you a myth with a pension. You cannot tell by the confidence. You can tell by whether the thing survives contact with "what would still have to be true if we were on a desert island with this problem and no org chart."

First principles, in the Aristotelian sense, are the starting-points you reason from — what must be true for the rest to make sense. Silicon Valley turned the phrase into a personality. We will not. We will use a light tool: Must / Maybe / Myth.

The room

The Scenario

You inherit a weekly report that takes four hours. It has always existed. Elena wants it by Monday 9 a.m. Nobody can name a decision it changed in six months. Jonah says legal requires it. Legal, when asked, says they required a number, once, in 2019, in an email nobody can find.

Sort the report.

Your move

Your Move

Common: Keep doing it; add a better template. Better: Ask Elena if she reads it. Expert: Must / Maybe / Myth, then a Minimum Useful Version (3.7). Resourceful: Expert, with a two-week experiment, not a manifesto.

The idea

The Big Idea

Analogy ("they do it this way") is a gift until it is a cage. First principles are a cage-cutting tool. Used constantly, they become a way to avoid learning from people who already paid tuition.

Explained

The Simple Explanation

Must — constraints that remain if names, tools, and habits burn down. Physics, law you have actually read, a customer's real job, safety. Maybe — useful defaults. The weekly cadence, the tool, the format. Testable. Myth — "that's just how," "legal said" with no document, "the industry standard" meaning "a blog."

Aristotle's name is on this because going to first causes is old. You do not need the Metaphysics on the subway. You need to stop treating a 2019 email as geology.

When analogy is better: medicine you are not trained in, aviation checklists, a mature safety practice. Do not first-principles your way around a protocol that exists because people died.

The tool

The Practical Tool

Three columns. Five minutes. Then one experiment on a maybe.

Fast path

The Fastest Reliable Method

Ask: If we could not use the current tool, what would we still have to produce?

The hack

The Hack

Find the actual requirement (the law, the contract clause, the customer job). Myths hate primary sources.

AI move

The AI Move

Context: Process: [describe]
Claimed constraints: [list]
Goal: Sort must / maybe / myth. For each must, what source would confirm it. For each myth, a cheaper replacement.
Constraints: Do not invent regulations. Say when I must read the actual clause.
Tech

The Technology Move

Official documentation over tribal Slack. Capability: source of constraint. Brand: whatever system holds the contract.

Mistake

The Common Mistake

Using "first principles" to ignore expertise you have not earned. That is not physics. That is arrogance with a catchphrase.

Defense

The Defense

Some musts are values (8.2). They are not empirical. Label them so you do not fake a spreadsheet for a conscience.

Council

The Masters' Table

Aristotle. Starting-points. Feynman. Do not fool yourself about which are musts. Grove. Output, not theater. Where wrong. Infinite reduction. Atoms will not write the customer email.

Best move

The Best-Possible Response

You find legal needed a number, not a four-hour novel. Must: a number they can defend. Maybe: weekly. Myth: the current template. You run a one-pager for two weeks. Elena does not miss the novel. Jonah is annoyed, then free.

Practice

Practice

Pick an inherited process. Sort it. Propose one experiment.

Real life

Real-Life Challenge

Career. "I have to stay because of the market." Must / maybe / myth. Money. "You must buy a house." Check the must. Digital. "We have to use this vendor." Job test (6.1).

Bad room

Bad-Room Version

A VP says "non-negotiable." You ask, without sarcasm, "Which part is the must — the outcome or the method?" Then you shut up.

Reflect

Reflection

  • What myth are you loyal to because it makes you look thorough?
Remember

Remember This

If it cannot survive 'show me the constraint,' it is not a principle. It is a habit in a suit.

Deep dive

Optional Deep Dive

Elon Musk's popular first-principles riff is a useful advertisement and a bad religion. Use the tool. Do not join a fandom.

Mission

Field Mission

Kill one myth at work this week with a primary source or an experiment.
