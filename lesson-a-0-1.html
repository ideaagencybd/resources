How to Use This Course

The idea

The Hook

Most courses are designed for a person who does not exist: someone with a quiet morning, a clear desk, and a personality that enjoys being assigned 500 pages of self-improvement.

You are not that person. You have a job, a phone, a body that gets tired, and a life that will interrupt this.

So this is not a course you "complete" the way you complete a form. It is a gym with a map. The map is eight levels. The gym is your actual Tuesday.

If you try to read everything before you do anything, you will become very well-informed about resourcefulness and no more resourceful. That is the main way intelligent people fail this material.

The rule is printed on the cover of the method, even if it is not printed on a physical cover:

30% learning. 70% doing.

You will be tempted to invert it. Do not.

The room

The Scenario

It is 9:40 p.m. You finally sit down to "start the course." You open the contents. Eight levels. Boss fights. A final Unknown. A 30-day plan. A prompt library. You feel a familiar mixture of appetite and dread — the feeling of a person who buys a language app and then studies the settings.

You have twenty minutes before sleep wins.

What do you do?

Your move

Your Move

Common move. Promise yourself a serious Saturday. Close the book. Saturday arrives with laundry and a mood.

Better move. Skim Level 1 titles and highlight. Highlighting is a ritual of intention. It is not practice. Dunlosky and colleagues, in a 2013 review in Psychological Science in Public Interest, rated highlighting and rereading as low-utility techniques compared with practice testing and spaced practice. Your highlighter is not a villain. It is just not a teacher.

Expert move. Do the smallest complete thing: Lesson 1.1, the Pause, including the physical drill, in the twenty minutes you actually have.

Resourceful move. Expert move, plus a calendar note for the next 25-minute block. Resourcefulness is not intensity. It is a next time that exists.

The idea

The Big Idea

This course is built as a progressive transformation, not a pile of chapters.

Each lesson teaches one skill, one tool, one useful result. If you only remember the tool name and can use it under mild pressure, the lesson paid rent.

You will see the same tools return in harder rooms. Facts vs Stories shows up in thinking, then in negotiation, then in decisions. The Pause shows up in every Bad Room. The Decision 7 is a pocket version of the whole OS. This is deliberate. Reuse is how skill becomes a reflex.

Explained

The Simple Explanation

There are three honest ways through.

Fast Track — the 5-minute layer plus one practice. You will not be "done." You will be dangerous enough to use the tool tomorrow. Fast Track is for weeks when life is on fire and you still want the OS installed.

Core Track — the lesson through Practice and one Real-Life Challenge. This is the default. Most people should live here.

Mastery Track — Bad Room, Deep Dive, Field Mission, and teaching the tool to someone else. Teaching is retrieval practice with social stakes. It is one of the cheapest high-quality tests.

Every level also has a minimum viable completion:

  • If you have 20 minutes: the Fast Track of the next uncompleted lesson.
  • If you have 1 hour: Core Track of one lesson, including the field-sized practice.
  • If you want mastery: the whole level, then the boss fight without notes.

You are allowed to Fast Track a level you already partly live, and Mastery Track a level that is your actual weakness. A calm engineer may skim Level 6 and crawl through Level 4. A gifted talker may reverse that. Self-flattery is not a diagnostic.

The tool

The Practical Tool

Name: Choose a track in ink

  1. Write today's date.
  2. Circle one: Fast / Core / Mastery.
  3. Write the next lesson number — not "Level 1," a number like 1.1.
  4. Write the next 25-minute window that already exists in your week.
  5. When the window opens, start ugly. Do not re-choose the track.

If you miss the window, you do not owe the course an apology. You owe it the next window. Shame is not a learning technique. It is just loud.

Fast path

The Fastest Reliable Method

Open 1.1. Do the 4-Second Pause six times. Write one real trigger from today. Stop.

You have started. Starting is the only part of a course that cannot be faked later.

The hack

The Hack

Never leave a lesson without a Do This Now artifact: a sentence, a card, a rewritten email, a checklist, a holding phrase in your notes. If the lesson only produced a feeling of agreement, it did not happen.

AI move

The AI Move

Use a model as a tutor and a sparring partner, not as a replacement for the reps.

Do: ask it to quiz you, role-play a Bad Room, critique your draft, steelman the other side. Do not: ask it to "summarize the whole course so I don't have to take it." That is how you buy the illusion of competence.

Context: I am taking a lesson called [name]. The tool is [tool].
I just tried it on this real situation:
"""
[what happened]
"""
Goal: Quiz me with 5 questions that check whether I can USE the tool, not whether I remember the headings. Then role-play the other person for 8 turns.
Constraints: Do not lecture. If I use jargon without a move, call it out.
Output: Questions first, then wait. After the role-play, one paragraph on what I avoided.
Verification: Tell me what you cannot know about my real room.

Do not paste other people's private messages, personnel files, or anything that would shame you on a projector.

Tech

The Technology Move

The job is "make the next session inevitable."

Calendar block. A notes file named RH. A printed one-pager later (the cheat sheet). Offline copies of anything you need on a plane. The course reader will remember which chapter you last opened. None of this matters if you only optimize the setup.

Mistake

The Common Mistake

Treating the contents page as the work.

Intelligent people love maps. Maps feel like progress because they compress anxiety into a list. Then the list becomes a second job.

Recovery: close the contents. Open the next numbered lesson. Do the practice. The map will still be there. It is not going anywhere. Your Tuesday is.

Defense

The Defense

This design fails if you use Fast Track forever and never let a tool get hot. It also fails if you Mastery Track in the abstract — beautiful notes, no field missions.

If you are in a crisis (health, violence, acute psychiatric emergency), this course is not your first responder. Use local emergency services and qualified professionals. Resourcefulness includes knowing which problems are not "figure-it-out" problems.

Council

The Masters' Table

Aristotle. Through the lens of phronesis — practical wisdom — knowing is not the same as being able to choose well in a particular situation. This course is biased toward the particular situation.

Feynman. The lens: if you cannot explain it simply, you do not know it yet. The 15-minute explain in the 90-minute sprint is a Feynman tax.

James Clear. The lens: environment and identity votes, not motivational weather. Put the next lesson where your future tired self will trip over it.

Dunlosky. The lens: retrieval and spacing beat rereading. Every "Your Move" is a test. Take it before you read the expert answer, at least once.

Drucker. The lens: contribution. Ask what changed in your behavior this week, not how many pages you covered.

Where the council could be wrong. Some knowledge work really does require a longer first pass before practice — a new language's sound system, a safety-critical procedure. Do not use 70% doing as an excuse to skip the briefing when the briefing is the safety.

Best move

The Best-Possible Response

Back to 9:40 p.m.

You put the phone in another room. You open 1.1. You do the drill. You write four lines. You set a 25-minute block for Thursday lunch. You go to sleep slightly more capable than the person who optimized their highlight colors.

That is a resourceful start. It looks boring from the outside. Boring is often the adult form of dramatic.

Practice

Practice

Do this now.

  1. Circle Fast, Core, or Mastery for the next seven days.
  2. Write the next lesson id.
  3. Put 25 minutes on a calendar that you actually obey.
  4. Tell one person, in one sentence, that you are taking a course in figuring things out — not to perform, but to create a small witness.
Real life

Real-Life Challenge

Work. You have a commute. Audio is allowed for hooks and recaps, not for skipping practice. The practice still needs a pen or a notes app at a red light you are not in. Do not "practice" the Pause while driving.

Family. A household that mocks self-improvement will mock this. You do not need their permission to take four seconds before answering. You may need not to narrate the whole curriculum at dinner.

Bad room

Bad-Room Version

You are exhausted, behind, and a little ashamed you have started this late in life. The Bad Room move is the same as the 9:40 p.m. move: one lesson, one drill, one next window. Do not use shame as fuel. It burns dirty and leaves residue.

Reflect

Reflection

  • What is your actual pattern with books you admire?
  • Which track is ego, and which is honesty?
  • What will you drop to make 25 minutes real?
Remember

Remember This

If you cannot do it this week in twenty minutes, you do not have a reading problem. You have a starting problem.

Deep dive

Optional Deep Dive

On finishing: completionists will want to read every Optional Deep Dive. That is allowed. It is not required. The course was designed so a beginner can stop after the core and still be more capable, and an advanced learner can keep going without being punished by repetition. If an idea returns, it returns at a higher altitude — the same tool in a worse room.

On badges: they are milestones, not stickers for children. You earn First Figure-It-Out when you complete the Level 3 boss. You earn Resourceful Human when you complete the Unknown. Nobody is watching. The badge is a memory hook.

Mission

Field Mission

Before midnight, finish 1.1 through the Field Mission, or if you already did, send a two-sentence note to your future self: the track you chose, and the next window.
