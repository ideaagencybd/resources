Two-Way and One-Way Doors

Speed is a virtue only when reversal is cheap

20 minDoor Type3,862 words22 sections
Skill
Match decision speed to reversibility
Tool
Door Type
Result
You stop holding cheap decisions hostage
In one line
If you can walk it back, decide. If you cannot, slow down.
The idea

The Hook

The team has been in the room for forty minutes about a button.

Not the product. The color of a button on a page that three hundred people will see, and that a designer can change before lunch tomorrow. Jonah has a strong opinion. Someone from brand has a stronger one. Elena has not spoken because she is saving her political capital for the thing after this.

The thing after this is a three-year vendor contract. Auto-renew. Data lives on their servers. Switching later means a migration that nobody in the room has survived twice. Finance wants a signature by Friday. The vendor brought sandwiches.

Forty minutes for the button. Twelve minutes for the contract, because everyone is tired and the sandwiches were good.

That inversion is one of the most expensive habits in professional life. You spend your rigor on what can be undone, and your speed on what cannot.

The room

The Scenario

Wednesday, 11:05 a.m. The Room. Two items left on Elena's agenda.

Item one: Jonah wants to ship a search feature behind a flag, to 8% of users, on Thursday. If it is ugly, they turn it off. He has the rollback in a checklist. He is already talking too fast.

"It's a flag. We can kill it in ten minutes. Sitting on it is how we sit on everything."

Elena: "Last time you said that, support ate a weekend."

Item two: the vendor. Legal sent a one-page summary nobody has read except the sentence about auto-renew. The salesperson is in the lobby. The VP mentioned "momentum" in this morning's standup, which in this company means sign it.

Mara, who has to live with both decisions, can feel the room doing its usual trick: the reversible thing is becoming a debate about Jonah's character, and the irreversible thing is becoming a scheduling problem.

Elena looks at Mara. "We need to move. What's your call on both?"

Two doors. One meeting. What do you say?

Your move

Your Move

Name both calls before you keep reading. Button-feature and contract. Speed or rigor, for each.

Common move. Treat them as the same species of "decision," which means the louder person wins the first and the more senior person wins the second. Or: delay both, to be fair. Fairness is not a door type.

Better move. Notice that the feature is reversible and the contract is not. Ship the flag. Ask for a day on the contract. This is already better than the room's default. It can still fail if "a day" is a vague hope and Legal's one-pager stays unread.

Expert move. Name the door type out loud, in the room, so the rule is visible. Two-way: decide now, small blast radius, rollback named. One-way: slow the clock, read the actual clause, name who owns the undo (there isn't one), and do not let sandwiches set the tempo.

Resourceful move. Same as expert, plus you protect the two-way from being punished. If Elena burns Jonah for a reversible miss, the organization learns to treat every door as one-way. Then nothing ships, and the contracts still get signed tired.

Why the common move fails: organizations have a limited budget of carefulness. Spend it on buttons and you will rubber-stamp marriages.

What can go wrong with the expert move: you will mislabel a door. Some "flags" are not reversible — data written to customers, a public promise, a trust break. Some "contracts" have a 30-day out that nobody read. Door type is a judgment, not a vibe. Check the undo.

The idea

The Big Idea

Match the speed of the decision to the cost of being wrong.

If reversal is cheap, speed is a virtue. Hesitation is not prudence. It is a tax.

If reversal is expensive or impossible, speed is vanity. You are not being "decisive." You are being first.

Jeff Bezos popularized this split at Amazon as Type 1 and Type 2 decisions — one-way doors and two-way doors — in a shareholder letter. Steal the lens. You do not need the company, the wealth, or the mythology. You need the habit of asking, before the debate starts: can we walk this back, and what would that actually take?

Explained

The Simple Explanation

A two-way door is a choice you can undo at a cost you can afford: a feature flag, a two-week tool trial, a seating chart, a draft email unsent, a weekend trip, most experiments, most process tweaks, which vendor you try first for a small job.

A one-way door is a choice that cannot be undone, or can be undone only at a price that changes the rest of the game: a three-year exclusive, a public accusation, equity you sold, a medical procedure, a resignation without a floor, a child, a house you cannot carry, a trust you break in a room that does not forget.

Most people feel the emotional size of a decision, not the reversibility. Emotional size lies. A button can feel huge because Jonah's status is in it. A contract can feel small because it is boring and the VP already smiled.

Herbert Simon's bounded rationality shows up here as a resource allocation problem. You cannot run the full Decision 7 at the same depth on everything. Door type tells you where to spend the depth.

Kahneman's fast system hates this split. It wants one speed: the speed of the current mood. Anxious rooms slow everything. Manic rooms accelerate everything. Door type is how you refuse both.

A useful test, three questions:

  1. Undo: What would it take to go back, in time, money, reputation, and legal fact?
  2. Blast radius: Who else is stuck with this if I am wrong?
  3. Information: Will waiting teach me something I cannot learn by doing a reversible version first?

If undo is cheap, radius is small, and waiting will not teach you much — decide. If undo is expensive — run Decision 7 properly, add the premortem (7.4), and do not let the lobby set Friday.

The tool

The Practical Tool

Name: Door Type

Use it at the top of any choice, before the arguments, including your private ones.

  1. Name the choice in one line. "Ship search to 8% Thursday." "Sign the three-year vendor."
  2. Describe the undo in concrete terms. Not "we could change it." Who does what, by when, at what cost? If you cannot describe the undo, it is one-way until proven otherwise.
  3. Label it. Two-way / one-way / mixed. Mixed is common: the experiment is two-way, the customer email announcing it is one-way.
  4. Set the speed. Two-way: decide in this meeting or today; one owner; small version first. One-way: Decision 7 on paper; a second reader; sleep unless the house is on fire; no signature on low blood sugar.
  5. Separate the doors if they are stuck together. Jonah's flag is not the vendor. Do not trade them ("we'll let you ship if you stop blocking the contract"). That is how one-way doors buy silence.
  6. Say the label out loud if other people are involved. "This is a two-way door. I want a decision in ten minutes." "This is a one-way door. We are not signing because the salesperson is downstairs."

Undo → Radius → Label → Speed.

Fast path

The Fastest Reliable Method

When you have thirty seconds, ask only:

"If this is wrong, how do we go back?"

  • If the answer is a person, a toggle, a return window, a conversation — two-way. Pick.
  • If the answer is "we don't," "it would take a year," "we'd have to sue," "they'd never forget" — one-way. Slow down.
  • If nobody knows — one-way until someone knows.

Holding sentence:

"I need the door type before I need opinions. Can we undo this, and what does that actually take?"

The hack

The Hack

Default two-way decisions to yes, small.

Not yes-forever. Yes-for-a-week. A trial seat, a flag, a refundable booking, a draft, a pilot customer, a 30-day tool, a conversation you can apologize for.

The hack fails if you use "it's two-way" to skip ethics. You can undo a message technically and still have used someone. Reversibility is about cost, not about whether you were decent.

AI move

The AI Move

AI is useful for finding the undo you have not named. It is not qualified to tell you the legal or relational cost.

Use AI for:

  • Listing ways a choice might be more reversible than it looks (trial, flag, refund, sunset clause)
  • Listing ways it might be less reversible (data written, public claim, exclusive, reputation)
  • Drafting a "small version" of a one-way-looking choice

Do not use AI for:

  • Reading a contract so you do not have to
  • Deciding whether a trust break is "worth it"
  • Inventing a rollback that does not exist in your actual stack

Prompt (copy, then fill):

Role: Door-type analyst, not decider.
Choice:

2) Name three ways I might be mislabeling it.
3) Propose a smaller, more reversible version of the same intent.
4) Tell me what I should read or ask a human before treating this as two-way.

Constraints: No business-guru tone. No "move fast and break things."
Do not invent legal facts. Mark guesses as guesses.

What to verify: Contract clauses, data residency, who can actually turn the flag off at 2 a.m., whether "refundable" is true on the weekend. The model does not have your vendor agreement.

Do not paste: the full contract with names and prices if you would not put it on a postcard; customer data; personnel details.

Tech

The Technology Move

The job is "make reversal real, not rhetorical."

Category examples:

  • Feature flags, drafts, staging, time-boxed trials — two-way machinery
  • Versioned documents so a one-way email is not sent from a mood
  • Calendar cooling-off on purchases and outbound offers
  • Actual return windows and sunset clauses, written, not promised in a meeting

When not to use technology: a flag that nobody on-call can operate is a one-way door with a two-way story. The story is a lie. Check the night-shift path.

Mistake

The Common Mistake

Calling a door two-way because you want to move.

Jonah does this. The last weekend of support was not a random storm. It was information about blast radius. A two-way door with a large radius still needs a watch, a checklist, and an owner. Speed is not the same as neglect.

The mirror mistake: calling a door one-way because you are afraid. Then every hire, every paragraph, every tool choice becomes a marble statue. Teams like that do not become careful. They become still, and then they sign the irreversible thing on Friday because they are exhausted from the statues.

Recovery: describe the undo out loud. If you cannot, you do not have a label yet. Get one.

Defense

The Defense

Door Type fails when:

  • The undo exists on paper and not in the world. "We can always migrate" is a sentence, not a plan. Ask who last migrated, and how long they were out of the market.
  • The decision is one-way for someone else and two-way for you. You can leave a vendor. The junior who built their whole skill on that vendor cannot. Radius includes people who do not get a vote.
  • You use two-way speed to dodge a conversation. Shipping a flag instead of aligning Elena is not door-type discipline. It is avoidance with a toggle.
  • Life doors that are actually one-way get treated like product flags: public humiliation, a lie, a resignation in a fit. You can get a new job. You cannot un-say some sentences. The Pause still comes first.

Limitation: some of the most important doors are mixed, and honest people will disagree on the label. Disagreement about door type is a better argument than disagreement about Jonah's tone. Have that argument.

Council

The Masters' Table

Jeff Bezos (door lens). Type 1 / Type 2 in the 2015 shareholder letter: consequential irreversible choices get deliberation; reversible ones should be made quickly by people with judgment. Use the distinction. You do not owe the rest of the legend anything.

Herbert Simon. You have a limited budget of analysis. Door type is how a bounded mind spends it.

Warren Buffett. Through the long-term lens: some mistakes compound (debt you cannot service, reputation, a bad partnership). Those are one-way enough. Do not be brave with ruin.

Nassim Taleb. Fragility: a system that cannot absorb a miss should not be taking one-way bets at speed. "Never bet the house" is a door-type rule.

Charlie Munger. Invert: ask "how does this become irreversible?" before you ask "how do we win?"

Daniel Kahneman. Noise and mood will set your speed if you let them. A label in the first minute is a cheap defense against the room's weather.

Synthesis. Speed is not a personality. Rigor is not a personality. They are settings, and the door is what sets them.

Where the council could be wrong. Bezos was describing a company with cash, lawyers, and flags. Your world may have fewer two-way doors than a platform company. Taleb can scare you into treating every door as ruin. Buffett's patience can become delay on a two-way career experiment you should have run at twenty-nine. Label the door in your building, not in their books.

Best move

The Best-Possible Response

Back to 11:05 a.m.

Calm. One pause. You are not picking a favorite human. You are labeling doors.

Observe. Two items. Jonah has a rollback checklist. The vendor has a lobby and an unread auto-renew. Elena is under VP "momentum." Support has a scar.

Define. The job of the next fifteen minutes is to set two speeds, not to finish Jonah's personality review.

Verify. Can the flag actually be killed at 2 a.m., by whom? Does the contract have a 30-day out, or is that folklore? You do not guess.

Prioritize. One-way first in attention, two-way first in action. That sounds backwards and is not: start the contract rigor now (it needs hours), and let the two-way ship on its own clock so it stops eating the room.

Find resources / people. Legal, actually reading. The on-call who would own the flag. Mara, who lives with both.

Options. Ship / delay / shrink the flag. Sign / delay / add a sunset / walk from the vendor.

Risks. Punishing Jonah trains one-way culture. Signing tired is the classic one-way failure. Delaying the flag "until we are sure" is how two-way doors die.

Choose. Flag: Thursday, 8%, kill switch named, support watch, post-check Friday. Contract: no signature this week; clause-level read; premortem on lock-in; a date that is a date, not a vibe.

Communicate. "These are different doors. Jonah's is two-way if the kill switch is real — Jonah, show me who can turn it off at 2 a.m. The vendor is one-way until Legal shows me the undo. We are not using the lobby as a clock."

Act. Kill-switch demo before close of day. Contract in a quiet hour, not a leftover.

Document. Two lines in the notes: label, speed, owner.

Adapt. If the kill switch is theater, the flag becomes one-way and waits. If Legal finds a 30-day out and data export, the vendor becomes mixed and the speed can change.

Reflect. Did the room try to trade the doors? Did you let it?

If all three came out one-way, you are probably over-labeling fear. If all three came out two-way, you are probably protecting speed. Recheck the undos.

Real life

Real-Life Challenge

Work — the reversible ship vs the irreversible signature. Situation: a small release and a large contract in the same meeting. What most people do: debate the release (visible, personal) and rubber-stamp the contract (boring, senior). Why it fails: carefulness got spent on the wrong door. Resourceful response: say the labels; ship the small; slow the large. Words to use: "What is the undo?" "We are not signing because someone is in the lobby." Words to avoid: "Let's be consistent and hold both." Consistency is not a door type. Tool: Door Type, then Decision 7 on the one-way. AI assist: smaller version of the contract intent (pilot, 90-day, export clause). What could go wrong: you embarrass the VP in public. Label the door; do not perform a mutiny. Offer a date.

Money — the sale that ends at midnight. Situation: a course, a sofa, a tool annual plan. Refund window exists, or does not. What most people do: treat the seller's timer as a one-way emergency. Why it fails: their scarcity is a marketing door, not your door. Resourceful response: read the actual undo (refund days, restocking, whether annual is cancellable). If two-way, you may buy tonight. If one-way, the midnight deadline is not yours. Words to use: "The refund window is the door type." Words to avoid: "I'll never get this price again" as a substitute for an undo check. Tool: Door Type, fast version. AI assist: list questions to ask support before you treat it as two-way. What could go wrong: you delay a cheap, refundable tool for six weeks. Two-way doors should move.

Family — the public comment. Situation: a relative says something false about your kid, your partner, or your work at a table. What most people do: a one-way sentence (humiliation, alliance-break) at two-way speed. Why it fails: you cannot unsay it; the table will remember the tone longer than the fact. Resourceful response: Pause. Most corrections can be two-way if they are private and specific. A public execution is one-way. Words to use: "I'll take that up with you after dinner." Then do. Words to avoid: the clever line you will still be explaining at Thanksgiving. Tool: Pause + Door Type. AI assist: none in the chair. After, draft the private sentence. What could go wrong: you use "two-way" to swallow something that needed a boundary. A boundary can be calm and still one-way enough to say once.

Bad room

Bad-Room Version

Same meeting. Worse.

The VP is now in the room. "We're signing today. I already told them yes." Jonah, hearing that one-way door slam, decides his flag is also a loyalty test and says he will ship tonight to all users, no flag, "because we either trust the team or we don't." Elena looks at you. Support is already short-staffed. Legal is not in the building.

Door type still works. It just has to be said without a speech.

"Two different doors. The vendor is one-way — if we've already said yes, I need an hour to make the yes less stupid: export, sunset, who owns the data. I will not perform a scene. I will bring the minimum protections. Jonah's launch is two-way only with a flag. All-users-no-flag is one-way, and we are not taking a one-way product door to soothe a mood. Flag at 8%, Thursday, kill switch, or it waits."

If the VP overrules you on the contract, you document the label you gave it and the protections you asked for. That page is for 7.7, and for the day the migration starts.

If Jonah ships all-users anyway, that is a different lesson (people, 4.8). Door type does not control other adults. It keeps you from matching their speed.

Reflect

Reflection

  • Which decisions have you been treating as marble statues that are actually flags?
  • Which decisions did you make tired, because they were boring, that you cannot undo?
  • Who in your workplace is punished for two-way misses? What did that train?
  • What is the undo for the choice you are currently avoiding?
  • When has "being decisive" been a way to skip a one-way read?
Remember

Remember This

If you can walk it back, walk through. If you cannot, slow down — especially when the sandwiches are good.

Deep dive

Optional Deep Dive

Bezos wrote for shareholders, not for your kitchen. The useful core is an old operations idea: cost of reversal should set cost of analysis. You can find versions of it in military doctrine (two-way rehearsals vs one-way orders), in engineering (feature flags vs schema changes that do not roll back), and in Buffett's insistence that some losses are not averageable.

What the letter does not do for you: it does not tell you the door type. Companies under-label one-way doors when they want speed (growth, a quarter, a VP's promise in a lobby). They over-label two-way doors when they want control (review boards, brand colors, Jonah's tone). Your job is the label, not the quote.

A harder variation: asymmetric doors. The choice is two-way for the institution and one-way for a person. Firing is often like this. So is a "quiet" policy change that junior staff cannot reverse. So is collecting a kind of data you cannot later un-collect. When the radius is a person who does not sit in The Room, upgrade the label.

Another harder variation: doors that become one-way by delay. A two-way health check, a two-way apology, a two-way "I need a different role" can turn one-way if you wait a year. Speed on two-way is not only about shipping product. It is about not letting a reversible conversation rot into a resignation or a silent marriage.

If you lead, publish the rule: two-way doors should not need you. One-way doors should not avoid you. Then keep your own hands off the buttons.

Mission

Field Mission

In the next 24 hours, take one two-way decision you have been holding hostage and make it. Small version. Named undo.

In the same window, take one one-way decision that is being rushed and slow it: a clause, a night's sleep, a second reader, a Decision 7 page.

Write two lines: the label you used, and what you did to match speed to the door.
