Finish

Done is a definition, not a feeling

20 minDefinition of Done3,396 words22 sections
Skill
Ship, close, and leave a trail
Tool
Definition of Done
Result
You complete things instead of almost-completing them
In one line
Done is a definition, not a mood.
The idea

The Hook

"Almost done" is a habit, not a location.

The file is at 90%. The last 10% is the actual job: the send, the check, the name on the ticket, the note to the customer, the receipt in the folder, the "you can use this" to the next human. You keep the 90% open because open still feels like control. Closed is a verdict. Closed can be judged.

So you collect almosts. A desktop of almosts. A queue of almosts. A reputation for being brilliant in the middle and expensive at the end. People stop trusting your "done." They start adding a private buffer after your name.

Finishing is not a personality. It is a definition you write before you are tired, a ship that includes the last boring step, and a trail that is a gift to future you. Resourcefulness that never ships is a private hobby. This level's promise was "I can execute." Execution has a door. Walk through it.

The room

The Scenario

Friday, 4:10 p.m. Jonah's vendor recommendation is "basically done."

The doc is long and smart. It does not have a one-line rec on page one. The numbers are in a tab Jonah trusts and nobody else can find. Elena asked for a page she could forward. The customer is waiting on a date that depends on the rec. Mara asked, twice, "is this sent?" Jonah said "almost — I want to tighten the appendix."

Priya, who would check the quality step, is already gone for the weekend. The queue's oldest item could move if the rec existed as a thing in the world, not as a feeling in Jonah's head.

Elena, in chat: "Need that rec before I leave. Is it done?"

Jonah looks at the appendix. He looks at the clock. He looks at a paragraph he could make 8% prettier.

What does "done" mean in the next twenty minutes?

Your move

Your Move

Common move. "Almost — one more pass." Then 6:40, still in the appendix. Elena leaves without a forwardable page. The weekend inherits a fog.

Better move. Send the whole novel with "let me know if you have questions." That dumps the last 10% on Elena. You did not finish. You exported.

Expert move. A Definition of Done written now, even late: one-page rec, BLUF on top, numbers linked, customer-visible date, Mara tagged, file in the shared place, a two-line note in the queue. Appendix parked. Ship.

Resourceful move. The same, plus a trail: a "how to reopen this" note for Monday (what is solid, what is 60%, what would change the rec). Documentation as a gift, not as a stall. Then he closes the loop with the people waiting: Elena, Mara, the queue. "Almost done" is replaced by "sent, here, with these holes named."

Why the common move fails: polish is unbounded. Parkinson's law — work expands to fill the time you give it — is an old joke with a sharp edge. Without a definition, the appendix will eat the ship.

What can go wrong with the expert move: shipping garbage and calling it a philosophy. A Definition of Done is a floor, not a license to be sloppy on the part that can harm people. The one-pager must be true. The appendix can wait.

The idea

The Big Idea

Done is a definition you can tick, not a mood you wait to feel.

If you cannot write what "done" means, you are not in the last 10%. You are in a fog with a progress bar painted on it. Write the definition. Do the last boring steps. Tell the people. Leave a trail. Then stop touching it.

Explained

The Simple Explanation

Several different failures hide under "almost."

No definition. The work was never given a finish line, so you cannot cross one. This is 5.1's project-in-costume, still alive at the end of the week.

No ship. The artifact exists in a private place. Work that cannot be found is not done. It is a diary.

No close. The people who were waiting were not told. The loop is still open in their head, which means it is still open in yours. The Zeigarnik-style observation — unfinished tasks occupy attention — is popular psychology with a mixed research history; do not treat it as a law. Treat the operational fact: open loops are expensive. Close them on purpose.

No trail. Future you, or Sam, cannot see what was decided or why. You will re-litigate the vendor in three weeks. Documentation is not a corporate hobby. It is how a system remembers so heroes do not have to.

James Clear's systems-versus-goals line belongs here too: a goal says "finish the rec." A system says "nothing leaves my hands without a Definition of Done, a home, and a loop-close." You fall to the system on Friday at 4:10.

Drucker: effectiveness is getting the right things done. The last 10% is often the only part the rest of the world can use. A brilliant 90% is, from Elena's chair, zero.

Bezos, light touch: if this ship is a two-way door — a rec that can be updated Monday — waiting for a perfect appendix is how you treat a reversible thing like a tombstone. Full door-type treatment in 7.2. Here, the useful move is: ship the reversible; name the holes.

Agile software borrowed "Definition of Done" as a team contract: tested, reviewed, in the shared place, not "on my machine." Steal the idea for any work. You do not need a sprint board. You need a tickable floor.

The tool

The Practical Tool

Name: Definition of Done

Write it when you start (or now, if you failed to), and obey it when you would rather polish.

  1. Write the floor, five lines or fewer. What must be true for this to be usable by someone else. Example for Jonah:
  • One page, BLUF rec on top (keep / switch / split)
  • Numbers linked or pasted, not "in my tab"
  • In the shared folder with a boring filename
  • Elena and Mara told, with a link
  • Queue updated with the customer-visible date
  1. Name the non-goals. "Appendix not required. Visual design not required. 100% confidence not required — 60% is labeled 60%."
  2. Name the last boring steps. Send. File. Tell. Tick. These are the steps "almost" skips. Put them on a mini-checklist (5.3).
  3. Ship to a home. Shared drive, ticket, inbox of the owner — a place a stranger would look. Your desktop is not a home.
  4. Close the loop. A sentence to each waiter: "Sent. Link. What is solid. What is still open. What I need from you, if anything. If nothing, you can stand down."
  5. Leave a trail. Five to ten lines: decision, why, holes, where the files live, who owns the next crumb. Gift to Monday. Gift to Sam. Gift to future you after a fever.
  6. Hands off. Unless the definition is unmet, stop. Open a new Top 3 item if more work is real. Do not nibble the shipped thing into a new almost.

Fast path

The Fastest Reliable Method

Ten minutes, Elena is leaving:

  1. Write a 5-line BLUF rec at the top of whatever you have.
  2. Paste or link the numbers.
  3. Drop it in the shared place.
  4. Send: "Done for today means: rec + link + date. Appendix Monday. Holes: [one line]."
  5. Update the queue.

That is a finish. The appendix was never the product.

The hack

The Hack

Define "done" before you start, in the same place as Owner, Outcome, Deadline.

OOD gets it out of your hands. DoD gets it out of the universe of infinite polish. Together they are a complete handoff. If you are the owner, you still need DoD. Especially then. You are the person most likely to negotiate with yourself at 4:10.

Second hack: a "done for today" slice when the whole thing cannot ship. 5.1 already used this. Finishing a slice is a finish. Leaving a 40% file untitled is not.

AI move

The AI Move

AI is useful for the last mile people skip: the one-page cut, the loop-close message, the trail note. It is dangerous when it "finishes" by inventing the numbers you did not check.

Use AI for:

  • Cutting a novel to a one-page BLUF rec
  • Drafting the close-the-loop messages
  • A "trail for Monday" from your messy notes
  • Turning a DoD into a five-line checklist

Do not use AI for:

  • Inventing the recommendation you will not stand behind
  • A fake trail that looks complete
  • Sending without your eyes on every fact

Do not paste: customer-confidential attachments, credentials, anything that should not live in a model.

Tech

The Technology Move

The job is "a home, a tick, a trail."

Category: a shared place of record plus a way to mark closed. Current examples: a folder with names a stranger understands; a ticket that can be closed (and is); a sent email that is the close; a simple changelog at the top of the doc ("2016-04-12: rec shipped to Elena; appendix still open"). Version history is a trail if you write human sentences into it. Do not build a knowledge base as a way to avoid sending the page.

When not to use technology: renaming files for twenty minutes. That is appendix behavior. Ship, then tidy the home once.

Mistake

The Common Mistake

Confusing polish with responsibility.

It happens because the last 10% is where errors show, so fear lives there. Fear dresses as craft. Craft is real. Unbounded craft at 4:10 on a forwardable page is fear.

Recovery: if you already missed the send, do not write an apology novel. Send the floor. "This is the rec. Holes named. Appendix Monday." Late and usable beats a beautiful almost.

Defense

The Defense

Definition of Done fails when:

  • The floor is set too low and someone gets hurt (safety, legal, money, a demo on staging). Raise the floor. Checklists exist for this (5.3).
  • The floor is set too high and becomes a stall ("done means every stakeholder loves it"). That is not a definition. That is a wish for no judgment.
  • You close the loop with a lie. "All set" when it is not. You will spend Level 8 on character. Start here: the close must be true.
  • The culture rewards looking busy over shipping. Then your DoD is a small act of sanity, and you should still keep a trail so you can show what left the dock.

Limitation: a definition will not make the work good. It will make the work end. Quality comes from the map, the checklist, the start, the people. Finish is the door.

Council

The Masters' Table

Peter Drucker. Through Drucker's lens, the question is "what is my contribution?" A contribution that never leaves your desk is not one. Finish is respect for other people's time.

James Clear. You fall to systems. A DoD is a system. "I'll know it when I feel it" is a mood.

Andy Grove. Output. Indicators. A team that cannot tell done from almost cannot manage itself. The trail is an indicator, not a diary.

Jeff Bezos (light). Reversible work should not wait for a funeral. Ship, label, correct. One-way doors deserve a slower floor — not an appendix about fonts.

The software teams who named Definition of Done. Steal the contract: agreed floor, shared place, not "on my laptop." Leave the jargon if it does not help your room.

Synthesis. Write the floor. Do the boring last steps. Tell people. Leave a gift for Monday. Stop.

Where the council could be wrong. Shipping culture can become cruelty: "done" as a club to beat people who needed one more safety check. If the miss is catastrophic, the last 10% is the job. Pair this lesson with the checklist and with Level 8's cost test. Speed is not a virtue on a one-way door.

Best move

The Best-Possible Response

Back to 4:10.

Calm. Jonah puts the appendix in a parking lot. Pause on the urge to tighten.

Observe. Elena needs a forwardable page. The customer needs a date. Mara needs a true "sent." The appendix is Jonah's anxiety.

Define. DoD for the next twenty minutes: one page, BLUF, numbers, home, three loop-closes, a hole named.

Verify. The numbers in the tab — he checks the one figure Elena will forward. One figure, not a new analysis.

Prioritize. Forwardable truth over completeness.

Find resources / people. Mara can add the customer date to the queue if he sends in ten minutes. Priya is gone; the trail says "quality check Monday" instead of pretending.

Options. Appendix. Dump the novel. Or ship the floor.

Risks. A wrong number. He verifies the one that matters. A rec at 60% labeled as 100%. He labels it.

Choose. Ship the floor.

Communicate. To Elena: "Done for today: one-page rec, link below. Recommendation: split (reversible). Confidence 60% pending Priya's check Monday. Appendix not required to act." To Mara: "You can date the customer. I sent Elena the rec." To the queue: link + date.

Act. Move the file. Send. Hands off.

Document. Eight-line trail at the top of the doc.

Adapt. If Elena asks a question the appendix answered, he answers in two lines and then links the appendix. He does not reopen the whole cloth.

Reflect. What was "almost" protecting him from?

That is execute. Not almost.

Practice

Practice

Do this now. Twelve minutes.

  1. Pick one live "almost done" item.
  2. Write a five-line Definition of Done and two non-goals.
  3. Do the last boring steps you can do in this sitting (home, send, tick) or calendar them to the hour.
  4. Write the loop-close sentence.
  5. Write an eight-line trail.
  6. Mark the item closed or name the single unmet floor item. No "almost."

If you have nothing at 90%, pick a 40% item and write "done for today" instead. Finish a slice.

Real life

Real-Life Challenge

Work — the rec, the deck, the ticket. Situation: smart middle, missing send. What most people do: one more pass. Why it fails: the waiters are still waiting. Resourceful response: DoD, ship, close, trail. Words to use: "Shipped. Link. Holes: X. Next: Y." Words to avoid: "Almost," "FYI when you get a chance," "let me know if you want me to send it" (you send it). Tool: Definition of Done. AI assist: cut to one page; you verify facts. What could go wrong: shipping the wrong environment, the wrong number, the wrong person CC'd. The floor includes the check that has burned you before (5.3).

Home — the form, the bill, the repair. Situation: the portal is 90% filled, sitting. What most people do: "I'll finish this weekend." Why it fails: weekends inherit fogs. Resourceful response: done = submitted + confirmation saved + one sentence to whoever was waiting. Words to use: "It's in. Confirmation in the folder." Words to avoid: "I took care of it" when you almost did. Tool: DoD + a two-line checklist. What could go wrong: a confirmation page you did not screenshot. The trail is the screenshot.

Digital — the open loop pile. Situation: 40 tabs, 12 drafts, a brain that thinks they are in progress. What most people do: a new app for tasks. Why it fails: more almosts. Resourceful response: for each: ship, park with a date, or kill. Killing is a finish. Parking without a date is an almost. Words to use: "Kill / park-until / ship today." Words to avoid: "I might need this tab." Tool: DoD plus the parking lot from 5.1. What could go wrong: killing something that was actually a fuse. Scan once for dates and names before you slaughter.

Bad room

Bad-Room Version

Same Friday. Worse.

Elena's boss is waiting on the rec for a call at 5:00. Jonah's preferred option just took a hit from a number he does not fully trust. Mara is asking if she can tell the customer "we'll send tonight." Priya is gone. Jonah's body wants to pull the rec back into the cave.

Pause. State Check. This is fear.

DoD in a bad room:

  • One page.
  • Rec labeled with confidence.
  • The shaky number marked [unverified — source tab, Priya to confirm Monday].
  • A sentence on what would change the rec.
  • Sent to Elena now so she is not empty on the 5:00.
  • Mara told: "You can say a rec is in Elena's hands; do not promise the customer a vendor outcome tonight."

He does not ship a fake certainty. He ships a true floor. That is finish without fraud.

If Elena wanted a novel, she still gets a page first. The novel can follow. The 5:00 cannot use a feeling.

Reflect

Reflection

  • What are you currently calling 90% that is missing only the boring steps?
  • Who has learned to add a buffer after your "done"?
  • Where is polish protecting you from a verdict?
  • What would "done for today" be on the thing you are avoiding?
  • If you disappeared for two weeks, could someone find the trail?
Remember

Remember This

Done is a definition, not a mood. Ship the floor. Close the loop. Leave a trail.

Deep dive

Optional Deep Dive

The last 10%, status, and the gift of a trail.

"Almost done" often does social work. It keeps you in the identity of the person who is still needed. It delays the moment a boss can say "this isn't it." It delays the moment a customer can say "too late." Finishing is a status event. That is why a written floor, agreed early, is kind: the verdict is against a definition, not against your soul.

Cyril Northcote Parkinson's 1955 essay in The Economist joked that work expands to fill the time available. You do not need the joke as a law. You need a stop rule. DoD is a stop rule. So is a clock on the Top 3. So is "appendix is a non-goal."

On documentation: people write trails when they are afraid, and skip them when they are "fast." Invert that. Fast teams write short trails so they can be fast again on Monday. Ten lines. Decision, why, holes, home, owner. If your trail needs a wiki garden, you are stalling. If it needs nothing, you are gambling that you will never be sick, never be out, never be the Priya of 5.B.

Goldratt's "student syndrome" — starting late because the buffer exists — is the cousin of almost: the work inflates at the end. A 2-Minute Start (5.2) plus a DoD at the beginning attacks both ends of the task. You will use this again when Level 7 asks you to record decisions so you can learn from them. A Decision Page is a specialized trail. Practice now, on smaller ships.

Harder variation: pick three "almosts" in the next 48 hours. Each must be shipped, parked with a date, or killed. No fourth category. Report the body-feeling when you kill one. That feeling is often the whole addiction.

Mission

Field Mission

Before the next 48 hours end, finish one real almost.

Write, then obey:

  1. Definition of Done (≤5 lines)
  2. Non-goals
  3. The send / file / tell steps
  4. The trail (≤10 lines)

Then four lines of proof: what you shipped, who you told, where it lives, what you refused to polish.

If you cannot finish the whole thing, finish a named slice and close that loop. A slice with a door is a finish. A bigger almost is not.
