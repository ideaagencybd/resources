The 30/70 Rule

Learning is the smaller part

Agreement is not skill.
The idea

The Hook

You already know more than you use.

That sentence is either insulting or a relief. For most adults it is both. You have read about sleep, conversations, focus, money. You can explain a version of each. Then the week starts and you live the old software.

This course will not save you from that pattern by giving you more explanations. It will save you, if it does, by forcing contact.

Thirty percent of the value is understanding. Seventy percent is doing the thing while it is still awkward.

If that ratio makes you feel cheated — you wanted a beautiful book — stay anyway. The beauty is in the person you become when the awkward thing gets easier.

The room

The Scenario

You finish a lesson on difficult conversations. You feel moved. You send a screenshot of a quote to a friend. You do not have the conversation.

A week later you cannot remember the opening sentence the lesson gave you. You remember that you agreed.

What failed?

Your move

Your Move

Common move. "I'll do it when I have a real one." The real one arrives at 4:12 p.m. and uses your old mouth.

Better move. Journal about your feelings. Journals are sometimes the work. Here they are often a substitute.

Expert move. Book a 15-minute conversation this week that is only 20% as scary as the one you are avoiding. Practice on a smaller door.

Resourceful move. Write the opening sentence. Say it out loud. Send the invite. The lesson is not over until the world has been poked.

The idea

The Big Idea

Consumption produces familiarity. Practice produces retrieval. Retrieval is what you will have in the Bad Room, when the page is not in front of you.

Explained

The Simple Explanation

Dunlosky et al. (2013) reviewed ten common learning techniques. Practice testing and distributed practice came out as high utility. Rereading and highlighting came out as low. That does not mean reading is useless. It means reading without retrieval is a weak way to change what you can do later.

Hattie and Donoghue (2021) in a meta-analysis of learning techniques similarly found distributed practice and practice testing among the strongest.

This course is built as a series of tests: Your Move, Practice, Real-Life, Bad Room, Field Mission, Boss Fight, the Unknown. If you skip them, you are rereading your own potential.

The tool

The Practical Tool

Name: Do This Now

At the end of every lesson, before you stand up:

  1. Close the beautiful explanation.
  2. Do the smallest version of the tool in the real world, or a high-fidelity simulation (out loud, on paper, in a role-play).
  3. Write one line: what broke.
  4. Schedule the Field Mission if you could not do it on the spot.
Fast path

The Fastest Reliable Method

Read until you can name the tool. Then stop reading and use it. Return only if the use failed in a way you do not understand.

The hack

The Hack

Study in the same state you will perform. If the skill is for hot rooms, practice while slightly uncomfortable — standing, timed, with a person. Practicing the Pause in a scented candle session is better than nothing and worse than practicing it after a mildly annoying email.

AI move

The AI Move

Ask a model to be a bad client for eight turns. That is practice. Asking it to "explain difficult conversations again" is consumption.

Goal: Role-play a slightly angry colleague for 8 turns. I will practice [tool].
Constraints: Do not become cartoon-evil. Do not break character to praise me. After 8 turns, tell me the first moment I got defensive.
Tech

The Technology Move

A tracker with two columns: minutes read, minutes practiced. The number that should be larger is not the one your ego likes.

Mistake

The Common Mistake

Collecting frameworks like stamps. Five models where one would do. This course will fight you on that. One excellent model, used, beats a museum.

Defense

The Defense

Some days you only have the 30%. You are ill, or caring for someone, or the Field Mission is not yours to run. Then read. Rest. Do not lie to yourself that the 30% was 100%.

Council

The Masters' Table

Aristotle. Virtue as habit, not as lecture.

Ericsson. Deliberate practice: specific, feedback-rich, slightly too hard. Popular "10,000 hours" is a cartoon of a real research program — do not worship the number.

Duckworth. Grit as sustained interest plus practice, not as grinding for its own sake.

Where the council could be wrong. Over-practice of the wrong thing makes you reliably worse. Feedback has to be about reality, not about looking busy.

Best move

The Best-Possible Response

You finish the difficult conversation lesson. You write the opening. You ask a real person for fifteen minutes on Thursday. You are now in the 70%. The quote can wait.

Practice

Practice

Estimate last week's minutes of learning-about versus doing. Do not improve the estimate. Improve the ratio this week by one session of 70%.

Real life

Real-Life Challenge

Learning. The next article you save — use it in 24 hours or delete it.

Work. The next framework you hear in a meeting — apply it to a live task the same day or let it go.

Bad room

Bad-Room Version

You have an exam, a launch, a match tomorrow. 30/70 still holds, but Find and Act get stricter. Retrieval practice under time. Not a new book.

Reflect

Reflection

  • Where are you over-educated and under-practiced?
  • What do you consume to avoid a conversation?
Remember

Remember This

If you cannot point to a behavior that changed, you did not take the lesson. You visited it.

Deep dive

Optional Deep Dive

Desirable difficulties (Bjork): the things that feel like worse learning in the moment — testing yourself, spacing, interleaving — often produce better learning later. This course will feel slower than a summary thread. That slowness is part of the product.

Mission

Field Mission

Today, take one idea you already "know" (apologize well, start ugly work, verify a claim) and do it once, badly if needed.
