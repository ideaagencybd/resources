The Limits of Being Capable

Resourcefulness is not a license to carry everything

20 minAsk, Refer, Rest, Refuse3,821 words22 sections
Skill

Ask, Refer, Rest, Refuse
Result
You can put a problem down
In one line
Capability without limits is just a slower way to break.
The idea

The Hook

You have gotten this far by picking things up.

A broken process. An empty brief. A room that needed an adult. The Figure-It-Out Loop is in your hands. People have learned that if they set a problem down near you, it moves.

That learning is now a risk.

Resourcefulness is not a license to carry everything. Some loads are medicine, law, another person's job, or your own nervous system past the point where you are still a good instrument. Figuring it out, in those cases, is not virtue. It is a boundary violation with a competent face.

The advanced move in this course is not a more elegant carry. It is Ask, Refer, Rest, Refuse — four ways to put a problem down without becoming useless.

If you cannot put it down, you are not a hero. You are a bottleneck with a story.

The room

The Scenario

Sunday, 9:10 p.m. You were supposed to be off.

A message from a colleague: "My chest feels weird. You're good at staying calm. Do I go to urgent care or is it anxiety? You've had panic before, right?"

A second thread, Elena: "Legal asked if the clause is fine. Can you sanity-check it tonight? You're sharper than the outside counsel on the business point."

A third, from a friend who runs a shop: "Can you just rebuild their spreadsheet this weekend? I'd pay you in gratitude. You're faster than their finance person."

Jonah, in a fourth: "I know you're off. The deploy is on fire. I don't actually know what I'm looking at."

Four problems. You could move all of them. One of them could kill someone. One of them is a licensed profession. One is someone else's job. One might be yours — or might be a test of whether you can rest.

What do you pick up? What do you put down?

This is a scenario, not a true story. It is also not medical advice. That is the point.

Your move

Your Move

Notice the lean toward being the person who can.

Common move. Answer all four. Chest: "probably panic, breathe." Clause: a paragraph that sounds like law. Spreadsheet: open the laptop. Deploy: log on. You have now practiced medicine, law, theft of someone else's work, and the religion of never being off.

Better move. Handle the deploy, defer the rest. Still a person who only knows one kind of yes.

Expert move. Sort by category: refer the chest, refer the clause, refuse the spreadsheet, ask Jonah one diagnostic question and then either help or teach him where the runbook is.

Resourceful move. Use Ask, Refer, Rest, Refuse as a menu, not a mood. Put something down in language that is kind and final. If you are already depleted, Rest is a professional act, not a treat.

Why the common move fails: you are not a hospital, a bar, or a spare finance department. Dunning-Kruger is a real research program (Kruger and Dunning, 1999) that is also a popular cartoon; the modest steal is enough — people can be ignorant of their ignorance, especially in domains that look like "thinking." Capability in one domain leaks a false license into others.

What can go wrong with the expert move: you can refer as a way to never be implicated, including when the deploy is yours. Limits are not a personality of no. They are a map of where figuring it out is the wrong move.

The idea

The Big Idea

Ask, Refer, Rest, Refuse. Four legal ways, for a capable person, to put a problem down.

The Figure-It-Out Loop still stands. So does Who Already Knows (Level 3). This lesson is the missing gate: some problems should not enter the loop in your hands. Medicine. Law. Other people's work. Your own burnout. The resourceful human is the one who can tell which.

Explained

The Simple Explanation

Four moves. Different jobs.

Ask. You do not have the picture. A question, to a human who might, is the resourceful act. The Expert Request from Level 3: specific, bounded, easy to answer. Asking is not a failure of the course. It is the course.

Refer. The problem is real and not yours to solve. Chest pain: emergency services or a clinician, not your memory of panic. A contract clause: a lawyer. A mental-health crisis: a professional and, if they are in immediate danger, local emergency resources. You can sit with someone while the right door opens. You cannot be the door.

Rest. You are the constraint. State Before Strategy. The Recovery Protocol. A depleted instrument makes stupid law, stupid medicine, and stupid deploys. Rest is not a spa. It is taking yourself off the line so you do not cut anyone.

Refuse. The problem is someone else's work, a borrowed urgency, or a yes that would erase you (8.3, 8.5). A clean no. Not a delay that hopes they forget.

When figuring it out is the wrong move, in plain language:

  • Medicine. Symptoms, meds, other people's bodies. You may help them get to care. You may not diagnose from a thread.
  • Law. Contracts, threats, immigration, employment status. You may gather facts. You may not be counsel.
  • Other people's work. The spreadsheet that is their job. Doing it for them trains a shop that no one needs a finance person, only a you. Unblock, Delegate, Ask (Level 5) — not "I'll just do it."
  • Your own burnout. If the only tool you have is the Loop, you will Loop until you drop. That is not mastery.

Munger's circle of competence is the investor form of this. Stay inside what you know; know the edge. The edge is a professional kindness. It is also how you do not become dangerous.

A note on "I can learn it this afternoon." The 90-Minute Skill Sprint is for working skills, not for licensed harm. You can learn a pivot table in 90 minutes. You cannot learn medicine.

The tool

The Practical Tool

Name: Ask, Refer, Rest, Refuse

Use it when a problem lands and your first thought is I can take this.

  1. Name the domain. Body, law, licensed expertise, someone else's role, a system you own, your state.
  2. Check the instrument. State Check. If you are a 3/10, you do not get to be clever in someone else's chest or contract.
  3. Pick one of the four. Only one as the primary move. You can Ask and then Refer. You cannot Refer and also secretly practice medicine in a P.S.
  4. Say the sentence. Short. Kind. Final. Include the next door when you Refer.
  5. Do not hover. If you referred, do not sit in the thread rewriting the clinician. If you refused, do not do the work at 1 a.m. to be nice.
  6. If it is yours, pick it up properly. Limits include the duty to carry what you actually own — the deploy you shipped, the junior you manage, the apology you owe.

On a card:

DOMAIN → STATE → ASK / REFER / REST / REFUSE → SENTENCE → DON'T HOVER.

Sentences you can actually say:

  • Ask: "I don't have this. Who is the person who has done it twice?"
  • Refer: "I'm not the right help. For chest pain, I need you to call emergency services / get to urgent care. I can stay on the phone while you do."
  • Rest: "I'm off the line tonight. I'll be useful at 9:00. If this cannot wait, call [backup]."
  • Refuse: "I won't rebuild their sheet. I can send the three questions their finance person should answer."
Fast path

The Fastest Reliable Method

Ninety seconds. Four pings.

Sort with one word each: Refer, Refer, Refuse, Ask.

Send the Refer first when a body is involved. Do not clever-order the thread.

If you only have time for one sentence on the chest:

"I can't tell from a message, and I won't guess. Please get urgent care or emergency help now. I'll stay here while you call."

Then you may look at Jonah. Not before.

The hack

The Hack

Pre-write your referral list in peacetime.

Clinician / emergency number in your country. A lawyer you can call or a legal aid door. The backup for the deploy. The sentence for other people's work. When the Sunday pings arrive, you will not invent a license because a door was missing.

Put it in your phone as DOORS. Not a novel. Names and numbers.

AI move

The AI Move

AI will happily play doctor, lawyer, and finance department. That is one of the ways a capable person becomes dangerous in this decade. Use it to find doors, not to be the door.

Use AI for:

  • Drafting a Refer or Refuse sentence that is kind and clear
  • Listing categories of professional who handle this (not a diagnosis)
  • A State Check prompt: "I want to take this. Challenge whether I am the instrument."

Do not use AI for:

  • Diagnosing a chest, a rash, a child's fever, a mental-health crisis
  • Interpreting a contract as counsel
  • Doing someone else's licensed work "as a draft" you then send as if it were yours

WHEN TO USE: You need language for a limit, or a map of who to call.

WHAT TO PROVIDE: The category of problem, your role, your state. Not their medical history.

WHAT GOOD OUTPUT LOOKS LIKE: A labeled move, a sentence, a door, a warning not to treat the model as a professional.

Prompt (copy, then fill):

Context: I am a capable generalist. I need to know if figuring this out is the wrong move.
Problem category (no intimate medical detail):
[chest pain / contract clause / someone else's job / deploy I own / I am depleted]
My role:
[colleague / friend / manager / not their clinician]
My state (1–10):
[n]
What I am tempted to do:
[one line]

Do this:
1) Label: Ask, Refer, Rest, Refuse, or Carry (if it is mine).
2) Give me a sentence I can send, kind and final.
3) Name the human door (type of professional or backup role), not a diagnosis and not legal advice.
4) Warn me how a model can sound sure in this domain.

Constraints: Do not diagnose. Do not interpret law. Do not flatter my competence.
Output: Label, sentence, door, warning.

Do not paste: medical details, the contract, personnel files, anything that would be a problem in a log.

If someone is in immediate danger of harming themselves or others, you do not use a model. You use emergency resources. In the U.S., 988 is one door for suicide and crisis. Local emergency numbers are the door for a body in trouble. This course is not a hotline. It will not pretend to be.

Tech

The Technology Move

The job is "make the right door faster than your competence."

Category: referral and backup systems.

Current examples:

  • Phone: emergency, backup on-call, the lawyer's number
  • A team runbook so Jonah does not need your brain on Sunday
  • Status: "off the line" that is true, with a named backup
  • Password and access hygiene so "only you can" is not a silent trap (Level 6)

When not to use technology: a chatbot as a doctor. Fluency is not a license. Catch the Confident Lie (Level 6) applies hardest when you are scared and the model is warm.

Mistake

The Common Mistake

People hover after they refer.

They send the person to urgent care and then text a theory. They hire counsel and then rewrite the clause at midnight. They refuse the spreadsheet and then build it "just to unblock." Hovering is how limits fail. It is also how you stay central.

Recovery: if you already hovered, stop in one sentence. "I'm going to let the clinician / lawyer / owner run this. I'll be here for [specific, limited thing]." Then close the laptop.

The other mistake: using limits to abandon what you own. The deploy you shipped is yours until it is handed off. Rest still applies; rest includes a backup, not a vanishing.

Defense

The Defense

Ask / Refer / Rest / Refuse fails when:

  • You refer as theatre and the door is fake ("you should talk to someone") with no someone. Give a door or help them find one.
  • The culture punishes rest so hard that Rest is a career event. Then you are in a shop problem. Document, find people, use 8.3 and 8.6. Do not confuse a bad system with a personal failure to be infinite.
  • You are the only one with the access. That is a system failure (Level 5). Fix the machine on Monday. Tonight, you may still have to carry, and then you refuse to remain the only one.
  • A friend in crisis needs a human presence, not a lecture about licenses. Presence is allowed. Diagnosis is not. Stay, and still Refer.

Limitation: this tool will feel like a personality change if your identity is "I can take it." Good. Identity is the next lesson. You cannot carry everything and also become someone who lasts.

Council

The Masters' Table

Munger. Circle of competence. Application: the edge is a fact. Stating it is not modesty theatre. It is how you avoid a stupid year.

Curie / Hypatia as integrity-of-inquiry. Knowing is a discipline that includes what you do not know. Application: do not fake a result in a domain you have not earned. The cell, the clause, the chest — same ethic, different doors.

Marcus Aurelius. What is up to you. Application: their body is not up to you. Your next sentence is. Make it a door.

Who Already Knows (the Level 3 lens). Most problems already have a person attached. Application: find them. Do not replace them.

Elena as pressure, not master. Managers will ask you to sanity-check law because you are near and cheap. Application: a kind refuse is how you teach the shop what professionals are for.

Synthesis. The council agrees that a capable person without an edge becomes a hazard, and that rest is part of the work.

Where the council could be wrong. Circle of competence can become timidity. Stoic "not up to you" can become coldness in a crisis. Integrity of inquiry can become arrogance toward practical people. If someone is bleeding in front of you, you do not give a seminar on licenses. You call for help and use any first aid you actually have.

Best move

The Best-Possible Response

Back to Sunday, 9:10 p.m.

Calm. You do not answer in ping order. Body first.

Observe. Chest. Clause. Other people's spreadsheet. Deploy. Your state: it is Sunday night.

Define. The job is to not be dangerous, then to be useful where you actually are.

Verify. You cannot verify a chest over a message. That is the information.

Prioritize. Refer the chest immediately. Everything else waits.

Find resources / people. Emergency / urgent care for the colleague. Counsel for Elena. Jonah's runbook or the on-call. Not your memory of panic. Not a model.

Options. Play doctor. Play lawyer. Rebuild the sheet. Sleep. Or the menu.

Risks. A warm guess on the chest. A clause you "sanity-check" that becomes "we asked someone." A shop that never grows a finance person. A deploy you own and abandon.

Choose. Refer, Refer, Refuse, Ask — then Rest if Jonah has a path.

Communicate. Colleague: "I can't tell from a message and I won't guess. Please get urgent care or call emergency services now. I can stay on the phone while you call. I care about you; that's why I'm not playing doctor." Elena: "I won't sanity-check a clause as if I were counsel. I'll send the business question in three bullets for the lawyer. If they need a call, they can have me Tuesday." Friend: "I won't rebuild their sheet. I can send the three questions finance should own. Gratitude doesn't make me their department." Jonah: "What's the error message, and did you open the runbook section on rollback? Send those two. If it's still on fire after that, I'll log on for twenty minutes. If I do, we write the missing runbook line Monday. I'm not the permanent door."

Act. Stay on the phone if the colleague is scared. Then close the other laptops.

Document. For the deploy, the missing runbook line. For yourself, that you referred. Not a diary of virtue.

Adapt. If Jonah's two facts show it is your break, you log on. Limits are a map, not a dare to be unavailable.

Reflect. Where did being needed feel like a drug? That is the part of capability that wants a limit.

Practice

Practice

Ten minutes. Produce sentences.

  1. Write your DOORS list: emergency, a clinical door, a legal door, a work backup. If a door is missing, that is the homework, not a vibe.
  2. Draft four sentences — Ask, Refer, Rest, Refuse — in your mouth, for live domains.
  3. Take a problem you are currently carrying that is not yours. Label it. Write the put-down.
  4. Take a problem you are avoiding that is yours. Label it Carry. Name the first step.
  5. Find the error: "I'm just being helpful." Helpful to whom, at what risk, replacing whom?

Role-play the chest message out loud. If your first sentence is a theory, you failed the drill. Refer first.

Real life

Real-Life Challenge

Work — the clause at 9 p.m. Situation: Elena wants a sanity check that is actually law. What most people do: a paragraph that sounds like counsel. Why it fails: it will be remembered as review. Resourceful response: three business bullets for the lawyer; no legal conclusion. Words to use: "I'm not counsel. Here is the business intent. Please have legal answer X." Words to avoid: "This looks fine," "I'm not a lawyer but…" Tool: Refer. Circle of competence. AI assist: none on the clause. Language for the refuse, maybe. What could go wrong: you refuse and also refuse to name the business intent. That's sulking. Help at the edge.

Family / friends — the medical thread. Situation: a relative wants you to interpret a symptom because you are calm and online. What most people do: search, guess, soothe. Why it fails: soothe can delay a door. Resourceful response: Refer, stay human, do not diagnose. Words to use: "I can't tell. Please call [door]. I can stay on the line." Words to avoid: "It's probably nothing," your own old symptom as a template. Tool: Refer. Pause. AI assist: do not paste their symptoms into a model as a doctor. What could go wrong: you sound cold. Warmth and referral can be the same message.

Digital / money — other people's work. Situation: a friend wants you to be their unpaid specialist this weekend. What most people do: a quick favor that becomes a second job. Why it fails: you steal the learning from the person whose job it is, and your rest. Resourceful response: Refuse the carry; Ask or Refer to the right role; maybe a 90-minute teach, scheduled, not a secret rebuild. Words to use: "I won't do their job. I can teach one move on Tuesday." Words to avoid: "It's easier if I just…" Tool: Refuse. Unblock, Delegate, Ask. What could go wrong: you refuse a true emergency in a tiny shop where the person is drowning. Then a bounded yes, with an end, and a plan to stop being the system.

Bad room

Bad-Room Version

The colleague writes: "I knew you'd say go to urgent care. I don't have insurance. Just tell me it's panic. You're the only person I trust."

Elena calls: "I already told the board you looked at the clause."

Jonah's deploy is now a customer outage.

You are depleted. The drug of being trusted is on the table.

Pause. Body first, still.

To the colleague: "I hear you don't have an easy door. I still can't diagnose you. Let's find a door that works tonight — urgent care, ER, a nurse line. I'll help you look. I will not tell you it's panic. I care about you more than I care about being the person you trust to guess."

To Elena, after the body is in motion: "If you told the board I reviewed a clause, that needs a correction. I didn't. I'll send business intent. Legal has to own the sentence. I won't backfill a story."

To Jonah: the two questions, then a bounded log-on if it is yours, then Rest as a scheduled fact, not a hope.

You will feel like you have failed at being needed. That feeling is the tuition. Needed is not the same as good.

Reflect

Reflection

  • What do people set down near you because they know you will move it?
  • Where are you practicing a profession you do not have?
  • What would Rest cost in status this month?
  • Which of the four moves do you have no sentence for?
  • If you broke, who actually knows the runbook?
Remember

Remember This

Figuring it out is a skill. Knowing when not to is a limit. Limits are how capable people stay safe to be around.

Deep dive

Optional Deep Dive

Kruger and Dunning's 1999 paper, "Unskilled and unaware of it," in the Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, launched a thousand slides. Later work has argued about the size of the effect, the statistics, and the cartoon of "stupid people don't know they're stupid." The modest, usable claim: in a domain where you lack skill, you also lack the skill to see the lack. That is why Refer exists. It is why a fluent model is a special hazard. It is why this course keeps saying verify.

Circle of competence is not a personality. It is a map you redraw. You can grow a circle with the 90-Minute Sprint and with real practice. You cannot grow a medical license in a Sunday thread.

A harder variation: for one week, keep a tally of problems you picked up. Mark each A/R/R/R or Carry. You are looking for the column that is empty. Most capable people have no Rest and no Refuse, a fake Refer (advice in a costume), and an Ask they are ashamed of. Fill the empty column once. Shame is not data. The tally is.

If you are exhausted in a way that is not a week but a season, that is not a character test. That is a health and work-design problem. Use a human door. This lesson is a gate, not a clinic.

Mission

Field Mission

In the next 24–48 hours, put one problem down. Ask, Refer, Rest, or Refuse — out loud, to a human, in a complete sentence.

Write four lines:

  1. The problem
  2. The move
  3. The sentence
  4. The hover you did not do

If you cannot put anything down, you found the identity this level is about to name. Try a smaller object. A spreadsheet is enough.
