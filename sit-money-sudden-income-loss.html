Your income has stopped with no warning

high The Triage Budgetmoneyjob lossplanningbenefits
1

What is happening

Contract ended, client gone, hours cut to nothing, or dismissal. The money coming in is now zero or close to it, and the outgoings have not noticed.

2

What is actually going on

  • The first mistake is almost always cutting the wrong things — the small comforts — while leaving the large fixed costs untouched. Fixed costs are where the actual runway is.
  • The second is silence. Creditors, landlords, and utilities have hardship processes, but only for people who make contact.
  • The third is treating this as a personal failure and therefore a secret. Secrecy delays the practical steps by weeks.
  • You almost certainly have entitlements you do not know about, and they usually run from the date you claim, not the date you needed them.
3

Do this, in this order

  1. Calculate your runway in weeks, not vibesAvailable cash divided by essential monthly outgoings. One number. It converts dread into a plan.
  2. Split outgoings into essential, contractual, and optionalHousing, energy, food, transport, insurance are essential. Subscriptions, upgrades, and credit extras are not.
  3. Claim everything you are entitled to todayUnemployment support, housing help, council reductions, child-related support. Payments usually start from the claim date, so delay is money lost permanently.
  4. Contact every fixed creditor before missing anythingMortgage, rent, energy, phone, loans. Ask for a payment holiday or reduced plan. Pre-emptive calls get better outcomes than post-default ones.
  5. Protect the essentials list absolutelyHousing and energy before consumer debt. Do not let a loud creditor reorder your priorities.
  6. Start income generation in week oneAny income, including partial or temporary. Momentum matters more than matching your old salary immediately.
4

Words you can borrow

To a lender
"My income stopped on the 14th. I want to arrange a payment holiday or reduced payments before I miss anything. What are my options and can you confirm in writing?"
To an energy supplier
"I am in financial hardship. Please put me on your hardship register and tell me about payment plans and grants."
To your network
"I am available from Monday, I do X, and I am looking for anything from three weeks upward. If you hear of something, send it my way."
To your household
"Here are the actual numbers. This is what we are cutting and this is how long we have. I would rather we both knew."
5

What makes it worse

  • Payday loans, credit card cash advances, and buy-now-pay-later to plug the hole.
  • Cashing in pensions or long-term savings in week one, before checking entitlements and concessions.
  • Hiding it from your household. They will find out later and the delay costs real money.
  • Waiting for the perfect replacement role while the runway burns.
  • Cancelling insurance to save a little. One uninsured event ends the plan.
6

Once the pressure drops

  • Rebuild one month of essentials as your first savings goal, before anything else.
  • Keep the leaner budget for three months after income returns. That is where the buffer comes from.
  • Write down what you cut and never missed. It is usually a surprising amount.
  • Diversify: a second client, a second skill, a second income stream. Single points of failure are the real problem being revealed here.
A note on this library

These are general principles written to be useful under pressure, not legal, medical, or financial advice. Laws and emergency procedures differ by country. If life is at risk, contact your emergency services first and read this afterwards.
