Boss Fight: The Information Trap

Everyone is sure. The documents disagree.

35 minFacts vs Stories + Source Check1,517 words23 sections
Skill
Think clearly inside a flood of claims
Tool
Facts vs Stories + Source Check
Result
You leave with a verified picture, not a louder opinion
In one line
Confidence is not a source.
The idea

The Hook

The trap is not lack of information.

The trap is too much information, all of it wearing a suit, some of it wrong, most of it sure.

You will not beat this boss by being the person who read the most. You will beat it by being the person who can still see a fact when a room is trying to turn you into a narrator.

This fight uses everything in Level 2: two columns, 5W1H, better questions, a bias check, first principles used lightly, an argument that can survive, intellectual honesty, and the ability to hold two ideas without melting.

No one will announce which tool to use. That is the point.

The room

The Scenario

Monday, 11:02 a.m. A war room that is just a calendar name.

A customer says the feature you shipped last week is "losing them six figures a day." They have a screenshot. They have a lawyer on the thread. They have a number.

Jonah has a counter-screenshot. "It's their integration. I told them in April."

Elena has a dashboard. The dashboard says errors are up 14%. She does not say since when, or compared to what.

Finance has a Slack message from last night: "Leadership wants a name by EOD."

An intern pasted an AI summary of the tickets: "Root cause is clearly our API change on Thursday." The summary has three citations. One of them is a blog post from 2021. One is a ticket that is about a different product. One is plausible.

You have forty minutes until a call with the customer.

The documents disagree. Everyone is sure. You are not the most senior person. You are the person who just got asked, "So what's actually going on?"

What do you do?

Your move

Your Move

Write your first three moves. Then pick a path.

Option A — Pick a side fast

You back Jonah. He is usually right. You walk into the call with "it's their integration."

Option B — Summarize all the certainty

You make a slide with everyone's claims in equal type. You look fair. You have not verified a denominator.

Option C — Install a table and a clock

You put facts vs stories on a whiteboard. You time-box Find at 25 minutes. You treat the AI summary as a suspect. You go to the call with what you know, what you don't, and a next check.

Choose. Then read what happens.

Consequences

Consequence Engine

If you chose A. What happened: The customer produces a log from your API. Jonah goes quiet. Elena looks at you. You have donated your credibility to a story that was convenient. What you gained: speed, loyalty theater. What you missed: the 14% had no date range; the AI citations were junk; Jonah's April email was about a different endpoint. What you should have noticed: "I told them in April" is a story about being owed. The log is a fact. Retry: you still have 20 minutes. Open C.

If you chose B. What happened: The customer hears a buffet of theories. Their lawyer hears "you don't know." Elena hears "you won't own a sentence." Fairness without a left column is fog. What you gained: nobody can say you picked a villain too early. What you missed: a checkable timeline. Retry: C, with a sharper mouth.

If you chose C. What happened: You still might be wrong. But you walk in with: "Here are three facts we can show. Here are two unknowns. Here is the check we will finish by 4 p.m. We are not going to name a person before we name a mechanism." What you gained: a room that can still work. What you missed: the emotional need some people had for a villain before lunch. What happens next: sometimes the mechanism is you. Then you say that, cleanly, at 4 p.m. That is still a win for this boss.

The idea

The Big Idea

In an information trap, the scarce resource is not data. It is a standard for what gets to be called known.

Explained

The Simple Explanation

Run, in order, as much as the clock allows:

  1. Pause if the body is in a documentary (Level 1).
  2. Two columns for every confident sentence in the room.
  3. 5W1H on the customer claim: what exactly, when, where, who saw it, how measured, why it matters in dollars.
  4. Source card on the AI summary and the 14%: who, date, denominator, incentive.
  5. Question ladder: what would change our mind?
  6. Hold two ideas: it can be our bug and their integration and a measurement error. Then choose a next check, not a worldview.
  7. BLUF to the customer: known, unknown, next time.

You do not need to finish the mystery in forty minutes. You need to not ship a false mystery.

Fast path

The Fastest Reliable Method

On the call: "We can confirm X. We cannot yet confirm Y. We will not guess Z. Next check at 4:00." Then stop talking.

The hack

The Hack

Ask for the raw export, not the screenshot. Screenshots are stories with a crop tool.

AI move

The AI Move

Use a model to attack your current theory, not to write it.

Context: We have these claims:
[table]
Goal: List the top 5 ways this could be a measurement problem, a wrong endpoint, or a true bug. For each, the cheapest check.
Constraints: Do not pick a root cause. Do not invent logs.
Verification: What a real engineer must still open.

Do not paste customer PII, legal strategy, or credentials.

Tech

The Technology Move

Version control history, error dashboards with set time windows, status pages, official changelogs. Forums for "has anyone seen," not for "therefore." Capability: observability. Brand: whatever you actually use.

Mistake

The Common Mistake

Letting the person with the most certainty, seniority, or panic set the left column.

Defense

The Defense

Sometimes you must pick a temporary working theory to stop bleeding money. Then say "working theory" out loud and put a kill criterion on it. A story with a kill criterion is a hypothesis. A story without one is a trap.

Council

The Masters' Table

Feynman. The first principle is not to fool yourself.

Curie. Evidence, then speech.

Tetlock. 60% is a legal number.

Shannon. More messages is not more information if they are correlated noise.

Taleb. The six-figure claim may have a fat tail. Do not soothe with averages.

Where the council could be wrong. In a true emergency, a rough story plus action beats a perfect table. Know which day it is.

Best move

The Best-Possible Response

You draw the table. You kill the 2021 blog citation in front of people, kindly. You ask Elena for the dashboard's date range. You ask the customer for the sheet behind six figures. You put Jonah on endpoint matching, not on identity defense. You go to the call with three facts, two unknowns, a 4 p.m. check. You do not provide a name to Finance before a mechanism. You document.

If you are the cause, 4 p.m. includes that sentence.

Practice

Practice

Find a real thread with three confident, conflicting claims. Build the trap table. Do not post a theory. Post a check.

Real life

Real-Life Challenge

Work. The meeting where "everyone knows." Ask for the denominator.

Family. Two relatives, two versions of Thanksgiving. Camera vs narrator. You do not have to try the case.

Digital. An AI-written brief with a number. Click the number's source. If there isn't one, it is not a number yet.

Bad room

Bad-Room Version

The lawyer is on the call. Elena is silent. Jonah is angry. Finance is in DMs: "just give them a name."

Your line: "I can give you a name of an owner for the next check. I cannot honestly give you a name of a culprit in the next ten minutes. If you need a culprit more than a mechanism, that is a different job than the one I can do ethically right now."

That sentence has a cost. It is also how you remain a person you can work with in a year.

Reflect

Reflection

  • Whose certainty do you treat as evidence?
  • What is your tell when you are about to pick a side to relieve anxiety?
  • Did you skip the Pause?
Remember

Remember This

In a flood of confident documents, the adult is the person who will still say 'we don't know that yet.'

Deep dive

Optional Deep Dive

This boss is a rehearsal for Level 3 (search and verify), Level 6 (AI lies fluently), and Level 7 (decide under uncertainty). If you felt the urge to be a hero, notice it. Heroism is often just a story about your role. The table is a humbler instrument and a better one.

Mission

Field Mission

This week, in one real meeting or thread, put one claim through the trap table out loud: source, date, denominator. Survive the social awkwardness. That awkwardness is the tuition.
