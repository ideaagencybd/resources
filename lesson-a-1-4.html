State Before Strategy

A depleted body makes a stupid strategist

22 minThe State Check4,177 words22 sections
Skill
Check the instrument before the plan
Tool
The State Check
Result
You stop solving emotional problems with more caffeine
In one line
Do not take advice from a depleted body.
The idea

The Hook

You are trying to think your way out of a body problem.

Four hours of sleep. A coffee that has become a personality. Shoulders up around the ears since the 8:30 standup. A room that is too warm. A calendar that has had no gap since Monday. And now a "strategy" conversation in which you are about to agree to a date you cannot hit, or pick a fight you do not mean, or send a message that will take a week to unwind.

It will feel like a character issue. It is often a state issue.

The mind you make plans with is not a cloud. It is an organ in an animal. Sleep, food, movement, heat, and context are not lifestyle accessories. They are the instrument. A violinist who does not check the instrument still plays. They just play worse, and they blame the music.

This lesson is not wellness. It is quality control. Before you choose, you check the instrument. If the instrument is badly out of tune, the first move is not a cleverer plan. It is a repair, a delay, or a smaller decision.

You already have a Pause. You can name the weather. Now you ask whether the weather is coming from the problem — or from last night.

The room

The Scenario

Friday, 4:40 p.m. Elena has been in rooms since 7:50 a.m.

She skipped lunch. She is on her third coffee. Her left knee hurts from sitting. The office is overwarm. Her own manager asked, an hour ago, whether "the team is really on top of this." She has not had a closed door since Tuesday.

Mara and Jonah are on the call. The client wants a yes or no on an accelerated date. Jonah says, bluntly, "We can do it if we drop the other work." Mara's face says they cannot drop the other work. Elena feels the lean: just say yes, get this call over, fix it on Sunday.

She has ten seconds before the silence becomes a commitment.

What does she say?

Your move

Your Move

Do not keep reading yet. Notice your own state as you sit here. Sleep last night. Last meal. Jaw. Room. Then say the sentence you would actually say on Elena's call.

Common move. Agree from depletion. "Yes, we can hit that." The room relaxes. Monday becomes a crime scene. Elena will call this "being a leader." It was being tired.

Better move. Stall with fog. "Let us take this offline." Sometimes wise. At 4:40 on a Friday, with a client on the line, it can sound like evasion, and her manager already asked if they are on top of it.

Expert move. Check the instrument, then buy a decision that a rested person could still respect. She names the state privately: slept badly, no food, hot, pressure from above. She will not ship a date from that body. "I am not going to commit to a date on this call that I have not checked against the other work. I can come back Monday 10:00 with a yes, a no, or a yes-with-tradeoffs."

Resourceful move. Pause. Name the weather (heat, trapped, hungry). State Check. Then the expert sentence. If the body is this loud, the strategy is not allowed to be final.

Why the common move fails: depletion narrows time. The future becomes fog. The present discomfort becomes the only real thing. A yes is a painkiller. Painkillers are not plans.

What can go wrong with the expert move: a status-hungry client may hear delay as weakness. A manager who wanted a theatrical yes may be unhappy. You may still have to decide Monday, and Monday may still be ugly. The State Check did not make the problem small. It stopped you from solving it with caffeine and fear.

The idea

The Big Idea

State first. Strategy second.

A depleted body will dress its panic in the language of decisiveness. You will not notice. You will call it instinct.

The useful move is a short instrument check — Sleep, Fuel, Body, Heat, Context — before you let yourself make a hard call, send a sharp message, or agree to a date. If two or more lights are red, you shrink the decision or you repair the instrument. You do not "push through" and call it grit. Grit is for when the instrument is good enough and the work is still hard. Grit is not a substitute for food.

Explained

The Simple Explanation

There is a popular story that willpower is a tank that empties. Roy Baumeister and colleagues called a version of this ego depletion. It became a huge literature. Then it became a problem. A 2016 registered replication report led by Martin Hagger, with more than two thousand participants across twenty-three labs, did not find a reliable effect using the protocol they tested. Other researchers have argued about tasks, publication bias, and whether some form of fatigue is still real. Translation for this course: do not teach yourself a neat "willpower muscle" that science has settled. Do not ignore the ordinary fact that tired, hungry, overheated humans decide worse.

The recovery community has a cruder, sturdier checklist: HALT. Hungry, Angry, Lonely, Tired. Do not pick a fight, a drink, or a life decision from those four. You do not need to be in recovery for this to be good engineering. HALT is not a study. It is a field rule that has survived contact with real people.

Sleep is the largest lever most working adults still treat as optional. You do not need Matthew Walker's more dramatic claims — some of which other researchers have challenged — to take the modest version seriously: insufficient sleep reliably worsens mood, attention, and the quality of judgment. Treat sleep as performance equipment, not as a reward you earn after the strategy is done. The strategy is worse because the sleep was missing.

Heat, in this tool, means two things: body temperature and emotional temperature. A too-warm room and a too-hot conversation both narrow patience. Context means the room you are in — public or private, recorded or not, hungry audience or trusted one. The same sentence is a different act in a different context.

You are not looking for a perfect body. You are looking for a red-light count. Zero or one: proceed, with the Pause. Two or more: shrink the decision.

The tool

The Practical Tool

Name: The State Check

Use it before a hard call, a hard message, a hard yes, or a hard conversation.

  1. Sleep. Did you get enough for you last night, and the night before? Not the Instagram number. Honest. If you are a short-sleeper by myth and a wreck by evidence, believe the evidence.
  2. Fuel. When did you last eat actual food? Water. Alcohol last night. The third coffee is not fuel. It is a loan.
  3. Body. Pain, illness, jaw, shoulders, the need to move. A body that has not stood up since morning will vote for whatever ends the meeting.
  4. Heat. Room too hot or too cold. Emotional temperature already high. If you are still in the weather from 1.2, say so.
  5. Context. Public? Recorded? Client in the room? Your manager watching? A kitchen with a decade of history? Context decides whether you decide now or you buy time.

Score each green / yellow / red, fast, privately.

If two or more are red: you do not make irreversible calls. You use a holding sentence. You repair one red if you can (water, food, two minutes of air, a walk to the bathroom). You schedule the decision.

If one is red: proceed, but shrink. No extra commitments. No clever emails.

If all green: you still Pause. State is necessary, not sufficient.

Write this on a card:

Sleep · Fuel · Body · Heat · Context — Count The Reds.

A worked pass on Elena at 4:40:

  • Sleep: yellow (short night, week of them)
  • Fuel: red (no lunch, coffee)
  • Body: yellow (knee, no movement)
  • Heat: red (room, pressure, client)
  • Context: red (client, team, manager's question still in her ear)

Three reds. She does not get to say yes. She gets to say when the real answer arrives.

Fast path

The Fastest Reliable Method

When you have twenty seconds in a live room:

Ask, privately: How many reds? If the answer is "I don't know," count that as a red.

Then say one of these, which are designed to be true when the body is loud:

  • "I am not going to commit to a date I have not checked. I can come back at [time] with a yes, a no, or a tradeoff."
  • "I want this decision to survive the weekend. Let me come back when I have the constraints in front of me."
  • "I can give you a direction now, not a promise. Direction: we want to help. Promise: after I check the other work."

If you can leave the room for ninety seconds, do the cheap repairs: water, a handful of food, shoulders down, one physiological sigh from 1.1, a look out a window. You will not become a new person. You may become a person who can count.

The hack

The Hack

Do not make hard calls after 4 p.m. on a depleted day if they can wait until after food and a night.

That sounds like a luxury. It is often a cheaper luxury than the apology tour. Put a personal rule on the calendar: no irreversible yes after a certain hour unless safety is involved. The rule is a fence for a known failure mode, not a personality.

Second hack: keep a "red-light kit" where you actually work. Water. Something you will eat. A way to stand. Headphones as a door. The kit is environment design, which you will take further in 1.6. Motivation will not appear at 4:40. The banana might.

AI move

The AI Move

AI is excellent at helping you separate a state problem from a strategy problem after you have been honest about the reds. It is terrible at knowing how tired you are. If you ask it for a plan while you are depleted, it will give you a plan that assumes a different animal.

Use AI for:

  • Turning a messy situation into "decide now" vs "decide after repair"
  • Drafting a holding sentence that does not sound like fog
  • A Monday pre-brief so the delayed decision is real, not avoidance

Do not use AI for:

  • Medical advice about sleep, pain, or mood
  • Talking you into a yes you can already feel is a body-yes
  • A productivity plan that ignores the reds you just named

Prompt (copy, then fill):

Context: I have to respond in a high-stakes situation.
State Check (honest):
- Sleep:
- Fuel:
- Body:
- Heat:
- Context:
Red count: [n]
Here is the decision they want from me:
[one sentence]
Here is what is actually true about the work:
[facts]
Here is the sentence I am tempted to say:
[first sentence]

Goal:
1) Is this a state problem, a strategy problem, or both?
2) What is the smallest decision I can make now that a rested person would still respect?
3) A holding sentence under 40 words.
4) The repair I should do in the next 30 minutes.
5) What I must come back with, and by when.

Constraints: No wellness lecture. No fake energy. No yes that needs Sunday to be true.
Output: Labeled sections. One risk line.
Verification: What fact am I using hunger or fear to skip?

What to verify: The model cannot feel your knee. If you marked everything green to look tough, it will help you be tough and wrong.

Do not paste: medical records, other people's health details, confidential dates you would not put on a postcard.

Tech

The Technology Move

The job is "make state visible before choice."

Current examples of the category, not a shopping list:

  • A calendar that includes meals and a hard stop, not only meetings
  • A watch or phone that nags you to stand — useful if you obey, theater if you don't
  • Sleep tracking, used as a crude trend, not as a score to catastrophize
  • Drafting the holding sentence in a notes app before you return to the call
  • A recurring 4:00 reminder: STATE CHECK before any yes

When not to use technology: if the tracker has become another whip. A sleep score that ruins the morning is not performance equipment. It is a new heat source. Also: do not let a gadget diagnose you. Pain, depression, apnea, and injury belong to clinicians, not to a course and not to a ring.

Mistake

The Common Mistake

People treat state as a moral failing, then try to fix it with more strategy.

They make a better plan, a longer list, a sterner talk with the team, another coffee. The instrument is still out of tune. The plan is a speech the body cannot perform.

Why it happens: in many workplaces, bodies are embarrassing. Tired is allowed if you joke about it. Hungry is allowed if you work through it. Saying "I cannot make this call well right now" sounds like weakness. So people ship the call.

Recovery: if you already said the depleted yes, do not spend the weekend in silent panic and then miss the date. Pause. Then, as soon as you can: "I agreed too fast yesterday. Here is the real constraint. Here is what I can still protect." That sentence has more leadership in it than a heroic Sunday.

A second mistake: using state as an alibi forever. "I was tired" cannot be the ending of every story. The State Check includes a repair. If you never repair, you are not checking. You are narrating.

Defense

The Defense

The State Check fails when:

  • You are in actual danger or a true emergency. Then you act with the body you have. This tool is for social, professional, and personal decisions that can wait an hour or a night. It is not for a fire.
  • Every light is always red because your life is currently unsustainable. Then the tool is a diagnostic: the strategy is the state. You need a different load, not a better holding sentence. Level 8 will ask what is enough. You can ask it now if the reds never clear.
  • You use delay to avoid a decision a green body would still hate. The check is not a hiding place.
  • You have a medical condition that a checklist will not touch. Get a clinician. This course will not treat you.

Limitation: a good state does not make a hard tradeoff easy. It makes you able to see the tradeoff. That is the whole prize.

Council

The Masters' Table

The HALT rule (recovery tradition). Through the field-lens: do not let Hungry, Angry, Lonely, Tired pick the next irreversible thing. Crude. Durable. More useful at 4:40 than a theory of willpower.

Aviation's IMSAFE. Through the instrument-lens: Illness, Medication, Stress, Alcohol, Fatigue, Emotion — pilots are trained to check the operator, not only the plane. You are the operator of your yes.

Baumeister, with the replication caveat. Through the disputed-lens: the old "willpower tank" story is not settled science. Fatigue, however ordinary, still belongs in the decision. Hold the metaphor loosely. Hold the nap more tightly.

Sleep research, used modestly. Through the performance-lens: short sleep worsens mood and judgment in ways you will explain as personality. Believe the modest claim. Be wary of dramatic extra claims and of any book that never met a night-shift nurse.

Aristotle. Through the bodily-virtue lens: practical wisdom is not a pure intellect. It lives in a creature who eats and sleeps. A strategy that assumes an angel is not practical.

Synthesis. The council agrees that the operator is part of the plan. Checking the operator is not self-indulgence. It is how you stop exporting your blood sugar into other people's calendars.

Where the council could be wrong. Some days the reds are the job — new parents, acute grief, a launch, a ward at 3 a.m. A person who will only decide when green will sometimes fail the people who need them. The tool is a default for reversible and costly calls, not a permission slip to be unavailable whenever you are human. Also: wellness culture can launder avoidance as "listening to my body." Listen. Then come back with the answer.

Best move

The Best-Possible Response

Back to 4:40 p.m.

Calm. Elena drops her shoulders. One quiet sigh. She does not look at Jonah yet. She looks at the client.

Observe. Three facts: they want a date; the other work exists; her body is loud. Her manager's question is a fourth fact, and it is pressure, not data.

Define. The job of the next thirty seconds is to keep a bad yes from becoming the official plan.

Verify. She has not checked the other work. So she will not pretend she has.

Prioritize. No irreversible date. Preserve the relationship. Repair state before Monday's real answer.

Find resources / people. Mara knows the other work. Jonah knows the technical cost. Food. A night. A thirty-minute huddle Monday 9:00.

Options. Yes. Fog. A theatrical no. Or direction-without-promise plus a time.

Risks. Delay may look weak. A depleted yes will look worse in two weeks. A public no may box them in.

Choose. Holding sentence. State repair. Monday decision.

Communicate. "I want to help you hit an earlier date, and I am not going to promise one on this call that I have not checked against the rest of the work. You will have a yes, a no, or a yes-with-tradeoffs by Monday 10:00. Mara, Jonah — we huddle Monday 9:00. I'll send a three-line recap after this call."

Act. After the call: water, food, a walk, the three-line recap. Not a secret Sunday plan.

Document. Recap to client and team: what was asked, what was not promised, when the answer comes.

Adapt. If Monday's huddle shows the date is possible with a trade, she names the trade. If not, she says no without a speech about how tired she was on Friday.

Reflect. Which reds were on? Did the holding sentence hold? What would she change?

That is a resourceful human with an instrument. Not a hero. A person who would not let a coffee make a contract.

Practice

Practice

Do this now. Ten minutes.

  1. Run the State Check on yourself, right now. Sleep, Fuel, Body, Heat, Context. Green / yellow / red.
  2. Count the reds.
  3. Write one decision you made in the last week that a greener body might have made differently.
  4. Write the holding sentence you would use today if a client asked for a yes at 4:40.
  5. Build a red-light kit list: three items you will actually place where you work.
  6. Put a two-minute State Check on tomorrow's calendar before the hardest conversation or the hardest block.

Then, once this afternoon, eat or drink water before you answer a message that has heat in it. You are teaching the sequence: instrument, then speech.

Real life

Real-Life Challenge

Work — the Friday yes. Situation: a client or boss wants a commitment at the end of a depleted day. What most people do: agree, to end the discomfort. Why it fails: the discomfort ends; the commitment does not. Resourceful response: count the reds, give a direction, put a time on the real answer. Words to use: "I can give you a direction now, not a promise." Words to avoid: "We'll make it work," "No problem," a date you have not checked. Tool: The State Check, then a holding sentence. AI assist: use the prompt to draft the Monday recap from facts. What could go wrong: you delay forever. The time you name must be real.

Family — the fight after a long day. Situation: a partner raises a real issue at 9:40 p.m. You are hungry, hot, and half-asleep. What most people do: run the whole argument on a red instrument. Why it fails: you will say the first sentence, not the second. Resourceful response: "I know this matters. I am not going to do it well right now. I can do it tomorrow after breakfast, 8:15. I am not walking away from the issue." Words to use: a time. Words to avoid: "I can't deal with this," which sounds like "you don't matter." Tool: State Check plus Name / Need / Next. What could go wrong: you never show up at 8:15. If you defer, you keep the appointment.

Money — the late-night purchase or the panic transfer. Situation: you want to fix a feeling with a click. Resourceful response: two reds or more means the click waits until morning. Put the item in a cart. Sleep. The morning person is the one who has to live with it.

Bad room

Bad-Room Version

Same call. Worse.

The client says, "If you cannot commit now we will have to look at other vendors." Elena's manager is on the call, silent. Jonah says "We can do it." Mara looks sick. Elena has a headache and has not eaten. She might actually be able to make the date if they drop something she is afraid to name.

State Check still comes first. Three reds means she still does not ship a naked yes. She can ship a bounded one:

"I hear that the vendor question is real. I will not guess and lock us into a date we miss. Two options I can stand behind today: (1) we hold the original date, or (2) I come back Monday 10:00 with the trade we would have to make to pull it forward — including what we would drop. I will not sell you a date without the trade attached."

She has not begged. She has not thrown Mara under the bus. She has not let Jonah's bravado become a contract. She has made Monday about reality.

If her hands shake, that is allowed. Put them in her lap. The room can survive a human who is hungry. It cannot survive a promise a hungry human made to be done with the feeling.

Reflect

Reflection

  • Which red light do you most often refuse to see?
  • What decisions in your life are routinely made after 4 p.m. on a bad instrument?
  • When has delay been wisdom, and when has it been hiding?
  • What repair is actually available in your real day — not in a spa?
  • After you check state, do you change the decision — or do you just narrate the reds and then say yes anyway?
Remember

Remember This

Do not let a depleted body sign a contract a rested body will have to keep.

Deep dive

Optional Deep Dive

A short map of the operator.

The old willpower story was appealing because it made self-control look like a gym. Baumeister's strength model said effort now reduces effort later. Then the replication fight arrived, including Hagger and colleagues' 2016 multi-lab report, and the neat tank became a cautionary tale about social psychology's last two decades. You can still be tired. You can still make worse choices when you are tired. You just should not build a religion on a specific lab task with letters and e's.

HALT and IMSAFE come from domains that cannot afford cute theories: addiction recovery and aviation. They assume the operator fails in patterns. Check the pattern before the act. That is engineering.

On sleep: the core claim is boring and strong. Humans who do not sleep enough tend to be more emotionally reactive and less careful. Popular books have sometimes overreached on specific statistics and disease claims; Alexey Guzey and others have criticized parts of Walker's Why We Sleep. Use the boring core. Skip the panic.

On caffeine: it is a tool. It is not sleep. It cannot fix a red Context. It can hide a red Sleep until you send the mail.

If you want a practice beyond this lesson, run the State Check before any decision you would not want to explain on a podcast, for seven days. Track one thing: the number of times you changed the timing of a decision because of reds. That number going up is the win. Feeling like a well-rested advertisement is not required.

You will use this again in the Level 1 boss, in every Bad Room, and in Level 7 when the decision is large. A Decision 7 run on three reds is a ritual, not a decision.

Mission

Field Mission

In the next 24 hours, run the State Check before one real decision or one real hard message — not a rehearsal.

Afterward, write five lines:

  1. The five lights (green / yellow / red)
  2. The red count
  3. The decision they wanted now
  4. What you shipped
  5. What you repaired

If the red count was two or more and you still shipped an irreversible yes, you found your next practice. If you bought time, you already started to treat the instrument as part of the work.
