30 Thinkers Worth Sitting With · Modern Psychology & Decision Science

Viktor Frankl

184 words

1905–1997, Austria

You cannot always choose what happens. You can always choose your response.

What They Contributed

Frankl was a psychiatrist and Auschwitz survivor. His book Man's Search for Meaning combines a memoir of the camps with his development of logotherapy — a therapeutic school based on the search for meaning as the primary motivational force. Central to his work: the space between stimulus and response is where our freedom lives.

How To Apply It Now

The Pause (Level 1.1), the discipline of choosing response over reaction, and the pursuit of meaning through action (Level 8.4) all trace back to Frankl. In any difficult professional situation, his central question — what is life asking of me here? — is worth sitting with.

Where Their Thinking Breaks

Some readers extract from Frankl an unrealistic optimism about human agency. He explicitly cautioned against this. Meaning is possible even in extremity, but the extremity itself is not diminished. Do not use Frankl to dismiss other people's suffering.

One Book Or Source

Man's Search for Meaning (1946) — under 200 pages, read in one sitting, worth re-reading annually.
