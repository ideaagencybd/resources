How to Use AI Well Over the Next Several Years

564 words

This book was written at a specific moment in the development of AI tools. The specific tools will change; the underlying disciplines should not. This section attempts to name what will remain useful regardless of which specific tools dominate.

What will change

  • Model capabilities. Every year the specific capabilities expand. Rely on capability categories, not specific model versions.
  • Verification requirements. Some current verification burdens will ease as models improve; others will shift to new failure modes.
  • Cost structures. What is expensive today may be cheap tomorrow. What is free today may become premium.
  • Regulation. Legal frameworks for AI use in specific industries are actively developing. Match practice to the current framework in your jurisdiction.

What will not change

  • Verification of consequential outputs. As AI becomes more capable, it also becomes more capable of confident-sounding errors. The verification discipline matters MORE, not less.
  • Human judgment on ambiguous decisions. The ability to weigh considerations that AI cannot fully specify — personal relationships, organizational context, ethical trade-offs — remains distinctly human for the foreseeable future.
  • Trust and accountability. Someone signs the work. That someone is human. The 'AI produced it' defense has no legal purchase in most jurisdictions and no professional purchase in any of them.
  • Communication and relationships. Trust between humans is not AI-substitutable. Careers that invest in it will grow disproportionately.
  • The discipline of stopping. Knowing when to stop using AI and think for yourself is as important as knowing how to use it. This will remain the professional's meta-skill.

Three principles worth carrying forward

Note

THE TOOL — Regardless of which AI tools dominate

Note

1. USE IT DAILY Not experimentally. Daily. The people who benefit are the ones who have discovered what AI actually changes about their workflow — which requires using it enough to know. 2. VERIFY EVERYTHING CONSEQUENTIAL As AI becomes more fluent, the illusion of authority grows. Every citation, every number, every current-state claim gets verified against an independent primary source before consequential use. 3. INVEST IN WHAT AI CAN'T DO Judgment. Relationships. Presence. Character. Long-term trust. These compound in value as AI handles more of the compressible parts of professional work.

The one prediction worth taking seriously

Over the next 5-10 years, the professionals who thrive will not be the ones who use AI most aggressively. They will be the ones who use AI most DELIBERATELY — knowing what it can do, what it can't, what to verify, and when to close the tab and think for themselves.

That deliberate use is a skill. Like all the skills in this book, it is trainable — by practice, under real conditions, with honest feedback.

Do the practice.
