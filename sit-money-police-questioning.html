The police want to ask you some questions

critical The Single Sentencepolicelegalrightscritical
1

What is happening

At your door, at your workplace, or a phone call: they would like a chat. Nothing formal, they say. Just to clear something up, and it would look better if you cooperate.

2

What is actually going on

  • There is no such thing as an informal chat about a criminal matter. Anything you say can be recorded and used, and "voluntary" interviews are still interviews.
  • Innocent people convict themselves constantly by talking — not by lying, but by guessing at details, being helpful, and producing small inconsistencies that look like deceit later.
  • Asking for a lawyer is not an admission of anything and it is not treated as one by anyone who does this professionally. It is the normal behaviour of a sensible person.
  • You are unlikely to talk your way out. Officers cannot drop a case because you were convincing in your hallway.
3

Do this, in this order

  1. Be polite and identify yourself as requiredCooperate with lawful requirements — name, ID where required. Politeness costs nothing and buys goodwill.
  2. Ask two questions"Am I under arrest?" and "Am I free to leave?" The answers define the entire situation and your options.
  3. Say the sentence and then stopOne sentence requesting a lawyer, then silence. The stopping is the hard part and the important part.
  4. Do not consent to searches you are not obliged to allowAsk what power they are using. Consent removes protections you otherwise have.
  5. Get a lawyer before the interview, not duringDuty solicitors are free at the station in many jurisdictions. Free advice before you speak is the single highest-value thing available.
  6. Write your own account as soon as you are aloneTimes, places, who was there, what was said. Memory degrades and your account matters.
4

Words you can borrow

The core sentence
"I am happy to cooperate. I would like a lawyer present before I answer any questions."
At the door
"I am not going to discuss this here. If you need to speak to me, arrange it through a solicitor."
On searches
"I do not consent to a search. If you have a power to search, please tell me what it is."
When pressed that lawyers make it worse
"I understand. I still want one."
5

What makes it worse

  • Guessing at times, dates, or details to be helpful. A wrong guess becomes a lie in a courtroom.
  • Volunteering context, background, or your theory of what happened.
  • Deleting messages, files, or photos. That is a separate and often more serious offence.
  • Talking to other people involved. It can be characterised as collusion or interference.
  • Believing you can end it by being reasonable. Being reasonable and being silent are compatible.
6

Once the pressure drops

  • Get the reference number, the officer's name, and the station.
  • Say nothing on social media. Nothing at all, in any form.
  • Tell one trusted person and your lawyer, and nobody else — not colleagues, not the group chat.
  • If you are released under investigation, treat it as live for months and keep the silence intact.
Where this stops being yours to handle

If you are arrested, the only thing you need to say is that you want a lawyer, and then you stop talking. This is not obstruction; it is the process working as designed. Repeat it as often as necessary.

A note on this library

These are general principles written to be useful under pressure, not legal, medical, or financial advice. Laws and emergency procedures differ by country. If life is at risk, contact your emergency services first and read this afterwards.
