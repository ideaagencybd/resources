Medical & Mental Health

You have stopped being able to function

high The Hard Stopburnoutworkmental healthrecovery
1

What is happening

You are not sad exactly, you are flat. Simple emails feel impossible, you are getting ill constantly, you dread Sunday evenings, and you have started making mistakes that are not like you at all.

2

What is actually going on

  • Burnout is not weakness or laziness. It is what happens after sustained demand without adequate recovery, and it degrades cognitive performance measurably.
  • The cruel part is that the standard response — try harder, be more organised — is the exact thing that deepens it.
  • It does not resolve with a weekend, and it does not resolve while the load stays constant. Something has to actually come off.
  • The mistakes are a symptom, not a character revelation. They tend to be read as the reverse by the person making them.
3

Do this, in this order

  1. Sleep first, for a week, before deciding anythingSleep debt makes every judgement worse. No life decisions until you have slept properly for several nights.
  2. Remove one real thing this weekNot optimise — remove. A project, a commitment, a volunteer role. Reduction is the only mechanism that works.
  3. See a doctor and describe it functionallySay what you cannot do. Ask about physical causes too — thyroid, iron, vitamin D, sleep apnoea all mimic this.
  4. Tell one person at work, factuallyManagers cannot adjust what they do not know, and undisclosed collapse gets read as attitude.
  5. Reinstate the basics before the ambitionsFood at regular times, daylight, movement, and one social contact a week. Unglamorous and load-bearing.
  6. Stop the compensationsThe extra coffee, the evening alcohol, the doomscrolling. Each buys an hour and costs the next day.
4

Words you can borrow

To a manager
"I am running at a level I cannot sustain and it is starting to show in my work. I want to fix it properly rather than push through. Can we look at what comes off my plate?"
To a doctor
"I cannot concentrate for more than ten minutes, I am ill constantly, and I am making mistakes I would not normally make. This has been three months."
To family
"I am not withdrawing from you. I have nothing left by 7pm and I need a few weeks of doing less."
To yourself
"This is a capacity problem, not a character problem. Capacity problems are solved by reducing load."
5

What makes it worse

  • Quitting dramatically on a Tuesday evening. Decisions made inside burnout are usually reversed and expensive.
  • A productivity system as the solution. It adds a task to a list that is the problem.
  • A holiday as the whole plan. It resets nothing if you return to the identical load.
  • Alcohol as the off-switch. It reliably worsens the sleep that is your main repair mechanism.
  • Waiting until you collapse completely to make it legible to anyone.
6

Once the pressure drops

  • Recovery takes months, not days, and it is not linear. Expect good weeks followed by bad ones.
  • Identify the specific structural cause — headcount, a person, a commute, care responsibilities — and address that, not your attitude.
  • Install one hard boundary that survives busy periods: a finish time, a phone-free evening, one protected day.
  • Watch the early signals next time: dread on Sunday, snapping at home, skipped meals. They arrive weeks before the collapse.
Where this stops being yours to handle

If you are having thoughts of harming yourself, cannot get out of bed, or are unable to keep yourself or dependants safe, this is beyond burnout. Contact your doctor urgently or a crisis line today.

A note on this library

These are general principles written to be useful under pressure, not legal, medical, or financial advice. Laws and emergency procedures differ by country. If life is at risk, contact your emergency services first and read this afterwards.
