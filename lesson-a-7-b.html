Boss Fight: The Big Decision

Career, money, and a clock that will not wait

45 minDecision 7 + Premortem + Door Type4,510 words22 sections
Skill
Decide under real uncertainty
Tool
Decision 7 + Premortem + Door Type
Result
You make a choice you can defend in a year
In one line
Incomplete information, a clock, and two people who disagree: this is the job.
The idea

The Hook

Friday is real.

Not a metaphor. A time on an offer letter. A partner in the doorway. A manager who might counter if the calendar would sit still. A savings number that is a floor or a fantasy depending on which story you are in.

You have spent this level collecting a procedure so that this hour would not be a personality test. The Big Decision is not bigger because it needs a new theory. It is bigger because the bill is larger, the facts are incomplete, and the people you love do not agree.

If you wait for the fog to lift, Friday will still arrive. If you copy the last person who glowed, you will be Priya's sermon with your name on it. If you freeze to stay decent, you will have chosen by default and called it loyalty.

This is a boss fight. You will choose. The world will answer. Then you will choose again with better eyes — which is the whole point of a consequence engine.

The room

The Scenario

Thursday, 6:40 p.m. Mara's kitchen. You are Mara, or you are close enough.

The clock. The offer expires tomorrow, Friday, 5:00 p.m. No theater: they have already said they will not extend.

The offer (North). A company two stages past "cute." Thirty percent more cash. Equity that might be a number someday and might be wallpaper. Role is a stretch — more scope, messier org, a hiring manager, Alex, who was excellent in three interviews. Glassdoor mixed. One person you reached was cautious: "It depends who you work for." Runway rumor: eighteen months of cash, maybe. You could not verify the last round's details without being a pest or a spy. Start date: three weeks.

The current job. Not on fire. Elena is a decent manager under a VP who loves momentum. "Soon" about a promotion for eighteen months. Work you know. A commute that leaves you evenings. Salary: the floor your household is calibrated to.

The maybe-counter. Elena, when you told her you had an exploding offer: "I don't want to lose you. I can probably get a raise and a title. The VP is on a plane until next Wednesday. I need two weeks." She is not lying. She is also not a bank.

Money. Joint savings: four months of essential bills if you had no salary. A house down payment you have been circling. Sam has a stable job. If North goes badly in six months, you will not be on the street next week. You will be delayed, embarrassed, and possibly house-less for another year. If it goes badly in six weeks — unpaid gap, bad luck on the next search — the four months starts to look thin.

Incomplete information. You do not really know Alex. You do not know the runway as a fact. You do not know whether Elena's "probably" survives a VP who did not hear the offer. You do not know whether the cautious reference was a warning or a personality.

Two stakeholders who disagree.

Sam, in the doorway, plate in hand: "I want a child in the next two years. I want a house that is boring. I do not want to bet that on a person you met four times. If Elena can counter, let her. If she can't, we still have a life here."

A mentor, Jordan, on a 20-minute call an hour ago: "You're over-indexing on Sam's fear. Stretch roles don't come on a schedule. If you stay, you will be having this conversation at the same table in eighteen months, with worse options. Take North. You can leave if it's ugly."

Jonah, by message: "Alex is either a real operator or a good interview. You don't know. Don't let a Friday make you stupid. Also don't stay because 'soon' is a lullaby."

Elena, last line of the 5:30 call: "I need you to trust me until the VP is back. Please don't do anything irreversible tonight."

It is 6:40. You have the evening and tomorrow.

What do you do?

Your move

Your Move

Stop. Choose A, B, or C as you would actually choose, not as the person who read seven lessons. Write one reason. Then continue. You will get to run the others.

Option A — Take North. Sign before 5:00 Friday. Tell Elena in the morning, clean. Tell Sam tonight. Start in three weeks.

Option B — Stay and wait for Elena's counter. Let the North offer die. Give Elena the two weeks. Live with Sam's relief and Jordan's prediction.

Option C — A third path. You must invent it, but here is a legal version: tonight you run the full procedure; tomorrow morning you give Elena a real deadline she can meet (a counter in writing by 3:00 Friday, even a small one, or a dated written commitment the VP can ratify next week if North will wait, which they said they will not). You ask North, once, for a 72-hour extension with a specific reason. You write a downside plan (runway floor, 12-month review, no house-bet until month 12). You choose by 4:00 Friday among whatever is actually on the table — including "take North with the floor" or "let it die with eyes open." C is not "stall." C is procedure plus a third option.

Common move. Pick from the last voice in the room. Jordan if you want to be brave. Sam if you want to be decent. Elena if you want to be loyal. Friday if you want the clock to be the adult.

Better move. Pros and cons until 1 a.m. Poll two more people. Feel thorough. Still walk into Friday with a mood.

Expert move. Door type first, Decision 7 on paper, premortem, a percent not a slogan, a page with expecteds.

Resourceful move. The expert move, plus you do not pretend Sam and Jordan are oracles. They are stakeholders with different ruin lines. You put both ruin lines on the page. You refuse to bet the house. You refuse to launder a 90%. You choose in time.

Why A can fail: one-way-enough year, uncalibrated manager, household delay, Sam's trust. Why B can fail: "soon" continues, Jordan is right about the table in eighteen months, you used a partner's fear as a veto without a procedure. Why C can fail: you use "procedure" to hide, North hears delay as no, Elena hears a threat, you arrive at 4:00 with the same fog and a more exhausted Sam.

You still need C in the toolkit. Binary traps are how this boss wins before Friday.

The idea

The Big Idea

A big decision is a normal decision with a larger bill, a harder clock, and people you cannot ignore.

The tools do not get fancier. They get used. Door type, Decision 7, premortem, calibration, a page, the two social questions if a room is pushing you. Then a choice you can defend as a process when the outcome is unkind — because it may be.

Explained

The Simple Explanation

This boss is built so you cannot "deserve" the ending. North can be a good bet that hurts. Staying can be a good bet that withers. A lucky North does not make Jordan a prophet. A layoff at North does not make Sam a prophet. 7.3 is the spine: good decision / bad luck is not the same as bad decision. If you take only one thing from this fight, take that, in your bones.

Door type: North is mixed. You can leave in six months (undo exists). You cannot unspend the year, the equity story, or Sam's trust if you dismissed a real ruin line. Elena's counter is two-way until you let North die; then the death of North is one-way. The house down payment is a slow one-way if you raid it.

Bounded rationality: you will not know Alex. You will satisfice. Satisficing with a floor is adult. Satisficing by copying Jordan's certainty is not.

Fat tail: do not take a version of North that can zero the household. Four months of savings is a bruise-buffer, not a personality. If your real number were six weeks, Option A would be a different — worse — bet. Same job, different ruin. Taleb's caution, not his brand.

Calibration: "North will work out" is not scorable. "I will still be employed at North in 12 months" is. Your percent is not 90. If you cannot say 60 without flinching, you are not ready to promise Sam a story.

The tool

The Practical Tool

Name: Decision 7 + Premortem + Door Type (with a Confidence Range and a Decision Page, because you have them)

Run this tonight, on paper, before you argue with Sam again.

  1. Door type. North: mixed. Letting North die: one-way. Elena's "trust me two weeks": two-way for her, one-way for you if Friday is real. House raid: one-way. Label them out loud with Sam.
  2. Decision 7.
  • Want: growth and more cash without betting the household or the next two years of "boring" that Sam named. Write the conflict. Do not pretend one want is fake.
  • Know: expiry, 30% cash, four months savings, Elena's two-week ask, VP on a plane, cautious reference, mixed Glassdoor, Sam's timeline, Jordan's speech.
  • Assume (circle the load-bearing): Alex is as advertised; North's runway is real; Elena's counter will be real; you will regret staying; you will regret going. Which of these can you still check by tomorrow noon?
  • Options: A, B, C (extension + written floor + Elena deadline + 4:00 choose), plus "decline both and search" if you can afford the gap.
  • Wrong: North stalls or lays off (bruise if floor holds; ruin if you raid the house or if the gap eats the floor). Elena's soon never comes (bruise that can become a year). Sam's trust breaks (mixed, slow, expensive). You look timid to Jordan (bruise, almost a vanity).
  • Next: the 8:30 Friday actions, the 4:00 block, the page.
  • Choose: after the premortem, not before.
  1. Premortem. "It is 12 months later and this failed." Write both failures: I took North and it failed because… I stayed and it failed because… Pick three preventable causes. Change the plan (floor, review date, Elena in writing, a reference call tonight, no house-bet).
  2. Confidence Range. Scorable: "Employed at North in 12 months" / "Elena's counter exists in writing by a date" / "Household still has ≥ 3 months runway in 12 months." Coarse percents. Plan at 60, not at Jordan's 90.
  3. Who disagrees? What are we afraid of? Sam disagrees with Jordan. You are afraid of regret, of being ungrateful, of being a coward, of being reckless. Name them. They are not all ruin.
  4. Decision Page. Tonight. Review dates at months 3, 6, 12. Expecteds that can fail.

Card:

Door → Seven → It Failed → Percent → Page → 4:00.

Fast path

The Fastest Reliable Method

If you only have thirty minutes before a fight in the kitchen:

Want in one sentence (conflict included). Ruin line (what you will not bet). Three options. One checkable assumption you can still ping tonight. Choose a time-to-choose before Friday 5:00. Tell Sam the want and the ruin, not the campaign.

Holding sentence to Elena: "I will not give you a fake yes. I can give you a real answer by 4:00 tomorrow. A written counter by 3:00 changes the decision. A 'probably' does not."

Holding sentence to North, if you ask once: "I can sign by Friday 5:00. A 72-hour extension lets me do this cleanly with my household. If you cannot, I will still decide on your clock."

The hack

The Hack

Write Sam's best case for staying and Jordan's best case for going, each in a paragraph as strong as your favorite. If you cannot write one of them, you are in a campaign. Then invert: write how each path dies. Then pick.

Do not use the hack to split the difference as a personality. C is a third option, not an average of two fears.

AI move

The AI Move

Use AI as clerk, not as parent.

Use AI for: a Decision 7 from your notes; a double premortem; scorable expecteds; a BLUF to Elena and a kitchen paragraph to Sam.

Do not use AI for: what you should want; fake facts about North; being the adult so you can avoid Sam.

Prompt (copy, then fill):

Role: Decision clerk for a high-stakes mixed door. Do not choose for me.
Facts:
"""
[clock, offer, current job, savings, stakeholders' actual sentences]
"""
Tasks:
1) Door type for: taking North; letting North die; Elena's two-week ask; raiding the down payment.
2) Decision 7. Flag conflicts in WANT. Do not invent facts.
3) Premortem in past tense for BOTH "took North" and "stayed." Three preventable causes each.
4) Confidence ranges on two scorable claims. Coarse percents. Label guesses.
5) A downside plan that keeps ruin off the table.
6) BLUF to Elena (max 80 words) and a kitchen paragraph to Sam that names want + ruin, not a campaign.
7) Ask me the one question that would most change question 7.

Constraints: No destiny talk. No "follow your gut." No fake company research.

What to verify: Anything the model says about North, runways, "typical" equity, or Elena's odds. It does not know.

Do not paste: the offer letter's identifying numbers, your bank details, Sam's private medical or family facts that are not yours to put in a model.

Tech

The Technology Move

One note titled with the date. One calendar block at 4:00 Friday. One reminder at month 3. Not a spreadsheet with seventeen weights. Rigor is the seven questions and the ruin line, not a model of a life.

Mistake

The Common Mistake

Treating the outcome as the grade.

If you take North and they restructure in month four, the common novel is "I was reckless." If you stay and Elena's counter is real, the novel is "I was wise." Both novels are resulting. The boss fight is whether you ran a process you would repeat on the other ending.

The cousin mistake: using Sam or Jordan as the procedure so you never have to be the person who chose.

Defense

The Defense

These tools fail when the conflict is a values lock that no percent will open — child timing vs stretch, a life Sam does not want vs a life Jordan romanticizes. Then the Decision 7 still clarifies, and Level 8's questions (what matters, who this makes you) sit on top. Do not pretend a premortem can pick your soul.

They also fail if North requires a house-bet. Then you do not take it, however excellent Alex was. Ruin is a veto.

They fail if you run C as stalling. A third path without a 4:00 is B in a costume.

Council

The Masters' Table

Herbert Simon. Satisficing on Friday is the job. Complete maps are not coming.

Daniel Kahneman. The last voice and the vivid story (Jordan's stretch, Sam's child) will try to be the decision.

Gary Klein. Premortem both deaths while you can still add a floor.

Jeff Bezos (door lens). Do not spend one-way rigor on a two-way counter, or two-way speed on a household ruin.

Philip Tetlock. 60% plus a hedge beats Jordan's 90 and Sam's "I just know."

Charlie Munger / Warren Buffett. Invert for ruin; do not interrupt household compounding for a glamorous year.

Nassim Taleb. Never bet the house. Same job, different savings, different bet.

Synthesis. Procedure, floor, time-to-choose, a page. Then live with a world that does not owe you a matching ending.

Where the council could be wrong. Long-term caution can become a life of "soon." Stretch-role folklore can become a cult of leaving. Your want is allowed to include Sam's boring house. It is also allowed to include a harder job. The council does not get a vote. It gets a lens.

Best move

The Best-Possible Response

Calm. Phone in another room for forty minutes. Pause. This is not a predator.

Observe. Friday 5:00. Mixed door. Four months. Two disagrees. Elena's probably. Cautious reference. Incomplete runway.

Define. Tonight: procedure and a floor. Tomorrow: one check, one ask, a 4:00 choice. Do not choose in the doorway at 6:45.

Verify. Tonight: one more reference if possible; savings number as a fact with Sam; whether North has any extension (ask once, accept no). Elena: what written thing can exist by 3:00 Friday.

Prioritize. Ruin off the table. Then want. Then relationships, told the truth.

Find resources / people. Sam (stakeholder, not oracle). Elena (option, not parent). One reference. Not Jordan, not tonight — you already have that sermon.

Options. A, B, C. Invent the floor: no down-payment raid; 12-month review; leave if Alex is not real by month 4 (two-way enough).

Risks. Listed above. Include the risk of using C to hide.

Choose. In this walkthrough, C until 4:00, with a default: if no written Elena counter and no extension, take North only with the floor and Sam having heard the ruin line — or let it die if the floor is not real. The default must be written tonight so 4:00 is not a mood. (Your default may be the opposite. It must still be written.)

Communicate. Sam: want + ruin + 4:00. Elena: BLUF and 3:00. North: one extension ask, no grovel. Jordan: a thank-you, not a vote.

Act. Page. Calendar. Messages. Sleep.

Document. The page is the document.

Adapt. New fact tomorrow (reference, extension, Elena's written maybe) updates the percent and maybe the choice. It does not update the ruin veto.

Reflect. Did you campaign? Did you write both deaths?

That is a resourceful human on a Thursday. Not a hero. A person with a procedure and a floor.

Practice

Practice

Consequence engine

Play it. All three. Do not skip the ending you do not want. After each branch, you may retry with one process change.

If you chose A — Take North.

What happened (version 1 — bad luck, good process): You wrote the page. You did not raid the house. Alex is real. In month 4, North does a restructure anyway. You are on the list. Sam is angry and also remembers the ruin line you spoke. You land a job in seven weeks. The down payment is delayed. Jordan says "that's startups." Priya glows somewhere.

Audit: If the process was real (floor, page, 60%, Sam told), this is good decision / bad luck. Do not learn "never stretch." Learn a base rate. Do not learn "Sam was right" as a religion.

What happened (version 2 — bad process): You signed at 11 p.m. to end the feeling. No page. You told Sam "trust me." You spent part of the down payment on the commute car. Month 4 restructure. Now it is bad decision / bad outcome, and the luck is not the point. The house-bet was the point.

Retry: Replay A with a floor and a page. Notice the ending can still hurt. The grade changes.

If you chose B — Stay and wait.

What happened (version 1 — good process, mixed outcome): You let North die with a page that said why. You gave Elena a written ask anyway. The VP returns, the counter is 8% and a title that is not the work you wanted. You stay, eyes open, review date in six months. Sam is relieved. Jordan is smug in advance.

Audit: Process can still be good. The expecteds will score the "soon." If at month 6 nothing moved, the page will not let you mythologize.

What happened (version 2 — freeze as loyalty): You never ran the seven. You used Sam as the procedure. Elena's two weeks become a quarter. North's role is filled by someone you will watch. Bad decision / outcome still unfolding. The trap was agreement with the nearest fear.

Retry: Replay B as a chosen stay with expecteds ("written counter by date X, scope Y by month 6") and a next search trigger. Staying is allowed. Sleepwalking is not.

If you chose C — Procedure plus third path.

What happened (version 1): North says no extension. Elena sends a paragraph, not a number, by 3:00. You choose at 4:00 — take North with the floor, or let it die. Sam heard the ruin line at 7:15 Thursday, not at 4:50 Friday. You have a page.

This is the point of C: Friday is still a choice, but it is not a stampede.

What happened (version 2 — C as stall): You asked for time, ran a beautiful Decision 7, and arrived at 4:50 with the same fog. Elena is hurt. North is cold. Sam is raw. You sign or you don't, in heat. Procedure as costume.

Retry: C with a written default at Thursday 9:00 p.m. "If nothing new, I do X." The default is the adult in the room when the clock is.

After all three, write: Which process would I repeat if the ending flipped? That sentence is the boss.

Real life

Real-Life Challenge

Work — exploding offer vs "soon." Situation: as above, or a transfer, or a project that is a year of your name. What most people do: last voice, or freeze. Why it fails: clock and loyalty are not a Decision 7. Resourceful response: door, seven, both premortems, floor, 4:00, page. Words to use: "My want is… The ruin I will not bet is… A written counter by 3:00 changes this. A probably does not." Words to avoid: "If I don't take this I'll die here." "Trust me." Tool: the stack. AI assist: clerk prompt. What could go wrong: threatening Elena with a leave you will not take. Do not bluff a one-way social door.

Money — the house, the buffer, the stretch. Situation: a career bet that secretly raids a floor. What most people do: hide the raid in a story about growth. Why it fails: same job, different ruin. Resourceful response: put the savings number on the page; ruin veto; do not take A if the floor is fake. Words to use: "If this fails, we still have…" Words to avoid: "We'll figure it out" as a substitute for a number. Tool: Outcome Audit logic before the fact + Door Type. AI assist: none on the bank. You and Sam, a sheet. What could go wrong: treating four months as cowardice. It is a tail-cutter.

People — two stakeholders, one Friday. Situation: Sam vs Jordan, or Elena vs a mentor. What most people do: pick a person to be the adult. Why it fails: they have different ruin lines. Resourceful response: both ruin lines on the page; neither is an oracle; who disagrees is already answered — now size the fears. Words to use: "I need you as a stakeholder, not as my procedure." Words to avoid: a campaign speech. Tool: Who disagrees? What are we afraid of? + Decision Page. AI assist: kitchen paragraph, then say it in your mouth. What could go wrong: a joint life choice made as a solo adventure. If the door is household-one-way, it is a shared page or it is a betrayal.

Bad room

Bad-Room Version

Worse Thursday. Savings are six weeks, not four months. Sam: "If you take this, I don't know what we're doing." North will not extend and Alex "needs a yes tonight on the phone." Elena is in the air, unreachable. Jordan: "This is your window." The cautious reference texts: "I wouldn't."

Ruin is now in the kitchen. The stretch role did not change. The bet did.

Door type upgrades toward one-way. Option A without a floor is a house-bet. You do not take it. Taleb and Buffett, without the posters: do not ruin the game.

C shrinks: you cannot ask Elena by 3:00. You can still refuse a tonight-on-the-phone one-way. Holding to Alex: "I decide tomorrow at 4:00. If you need a yes in the next ten minutes, it is no." That no is a Decision 7 with question 5 in charge.

If you are tempted to sign to stop Sam's fear or to stop Jordan's scorn, that is the Panic Trap and the Agreement Trap holding hands. Pause. Ruin line. Page. Sleep if you can. Tomorrow 4:00.

If you sign anyway, write the ugly page (7.7 Bad Room): performed 90, real 40, fear named. You will need it when you try to learn.

Reflect

Reflection

  • Which ending would make you abandon a good process — and is that resulting?
  • What is your actual ruin line, in money and in trust, not in mood?
  • Who is your Sam, and who is your Jordan, and which one did you last use as a procedure?
  • What would a written default at 9:00 p.m. Thursday have been, for a live choice you have now?
  • If this choice goes well, will you be able to admit the luck?
Remember

Remember This

A good decision can still hurt. Write the process, cut off ruin, choose on a clock — and do not let the ending rewrite the grade.

Deep dive

Optional Deep Dive

The consequence engine is a drill for 7.3. Most education works the other way: we show a winner and hide the distribution. Here the distribution is the lesson. Run A until it hurts and is still a good bet. Run A until it hurts and was a house-bet. Feel the difference in your body. That difference is the course.

On stakeholders: a joint life is not a committee of oracles. Sam does not get a veto on every two-way career experiment. You do not get a solo vote on a one-way household ruin. Door type, again, is how you stop a love argument from being a category error. If you cannot agree on door type, you are not yet in the Decision 7. You are in a values fight. Name it. Level 8 is next for a reason.

On clocks: exploding offers are sometimes real (a hiring manager with a genuine other candidate) and sometimes a sales door. Ask once. Accept the answer. Do not build a personality around "I don't negotiate with timers." Also do not let their timer become your brain.

A mastery add-on: after you choose, send yourself the review prompt for month 3 now. Future you will be in a novel. The prompt is the rope.

Mission

Field Mission

If you have a live Big Decision, run the full stack in the next 48 hours: door, Decision 7, double premortem, percents, page, a time-to-choose earlier than the panic.

If you do not, run the stack on the largest open choice you have — career, money, or people — and play the consequence engine on paper for A/B/C.

Tell one stakeholder the want and the ruin line. Set the review date.

You are allowed to be 60% and still choose. You are not allowed to bet the house and call it courage.
