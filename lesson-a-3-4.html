Who Already Knows?

Most problems already have a person attached

22 minThe Expert Request3,968 words22 sections
Skill
Ask for help without looking lost
Tool
The Expert Request
Result
You can get a useful answer from a busy human
In one line
Do not make the expert do your homework.
The idea

The Hook

Somewhere in the building, or one message away, a person already has the piece you are trying to invent.

They have the login path. They remember why the 2024 one-pager excluded intercompany. They know that the error is a packaging issue, not a firewall issue. They are also busy, slightly territorial, and allergic to the sentence "Got a sec?"

Most people treat asking as a status injury. They wait until panic makes the question huge. Then they dump the huge question on the expert and call it collaboration. The expert does the homework, resents it, and gives a thinner answer next time.

Resourceful people ask earlier, smaller, and with proof of work. They do not pretend they already know. They also do not outsource the thinking. The skill is a template, not a personality transplant. Context. What you tried. A specific ask. A deadline. What good looks like.

The room

The Scenario

Tuesday, 2:14 p.m. Mara has finance-tool access. She has the 2024 one-pager. She still cannot tell whether two vendors in the extract are "real" spend or intercompany dressed as a supplier.

The person who knows is Priya in procurement. Priya is two levels up, in another city, and famous for replies like "This is in the wiki" when it is not. Jonah's advice, from across the desk: "Just Slack her 'hey how do we treat intercompany in vendor spend?' She can say yes or no."

Elena wants a skeleton by 4:00. Priya's calendar is solid green until Thursday. Mara can guess, and maybe poison Friday's Room. Or she can ask in a way that a busy, status-sensitive expert will actually answer.

What does she send, and what does she refuse to send?

Your move

Your Move

Draft the first message you would actually send. Then look.

Common move. "Hi Priya! So sorry to bother you — I'm new and a bit lost. Whenever you have a moment, would love to pick your brain about vendor spend. Happy to hop on a call!" You have asked her to hold your whole confusion. She will ignore you, or schedule a meeting that eats both of your afternoons.

Better move. Jonah's version: a short yes/no. Better. Still missing what Mara already saw in the extract, so Priya has to reconstruct the problem from scratch. Experts should not have to do your seeing.

Expert move. A tight request: context, what you tried, a specific question, a deadline, a picture of a good answer. Offer a default: "If I don't hear by 3:30 I will treat IDs 4401 and 4402 as intercompany and footnote them." You have made answering cheaper than ignoring.

Resourceful move. The Expert Request as a named tool, plus status-safe framing: you are not asking Priya to mentor you. You are asking her to confirm or correct a decision you are prepared to own. The loop's Find step, aimed at a human.

Why the common move fails: "pick your brain" is a request for unlimited labor dressed as humility. Busy people learn to smell it. New people overuse it because they think politeness is the same as respect. Respect here is preparation.

What can go wrong with the expert move: a default can look like a threat. Tone matters. "I will footnote unless you say otherwise" is a gift of time. "I'm going with my version" is a shove. Also: if Priya is the actual owner, she may want a five-minute call. Offer it as an option, not as the ask.

The idea

The Big Idea

Most problems already have a person attached. Your job is to make that person a cheap, specific, time-bounded query — not a dumping ground.

Status-safe asking is not groveling and it is not bravado. It is: I did the homework a reasonable person could do in the time I had. I need one thing only you can provide. I will not make you regret answering.

You will use this on experts above you, beside you, and outside the building. The template does not change. The temperature does.

Explained

The Simple Explanation

Five parts. Miss one and the message gets ignored or explodes.

Context. Where this sits, in one or two lines. "Friday steering one-pager on Q3 vendor spend, Elena's ask. I have access and the 2024 file." Not your biography. Not "as you may know."

What you tried. Proof of work. Searched the wiki, found X. Compared the extract to the 2024 definition. Still stuck on two IDs. This is how you do not make the expert do your homework. If you cannot fill this part, you are not ready to send. Go back to the loop.

Specific ask. One question, or a numbered two, not a cloud. "Are vendor IDs 4401 and 4402 intercompany for this report?" is a question. "How do you think about vendor spend?" is a cloud.

Deadline. A real time, and why. "I send Elena a skeleton at 4:00." Deadlines without reasons feel like dominance. Reasons without deadlines feel like optional homework.

What good looks like. The shape of a useful answer. "A yes/no plus any other IDs I should exclude" is a shape. "Any thoughts?" is not. Optionally: a default you will use if they cannot reply in time. Defaults are respect for their calendar.

Status-safe extras, because humans have rank:

  • Ask for a correction, not a lecture. "I am treating these as intercompany — please correct me" is easier to answer than "Please teach me procurement."
  • Do not CC the expert's boss as pressure unless they are actually a stakeholder. That is a political act. You will do politics later in the course. Do not invent it here.
  • If you are junior, you may name Elena as the requester so Priya knows this is real work, not your curiosity.
  • Thank them for a specific thing afterward, and close the loop: "Used your yes; footnoted 4401/4402; Friday went clean." Experts starve of this. It is how you get a second answer next month.

Andrew Grove treated meetings and one-on-ones as production. A bad ask is scrap. A good ask is a small, well-specified input. You do not need his whole factory model. You need to stop sending scrap.

The tool

The Practical Tool

Name: The Expert Request

Copy this, then cut it until it fits in a screen:

Context: [what this is for, who asked, the deadline that is not the expert's problem yet]
What I tried: [2–4 bullets of actual work]
Ask: [one specific question]
By: [time] because [the real reason]
Good looks like: [yes/no, a number, a link, a 2-line correction]
If I don't hear: [the default you will use, if you are allowed to have one]
Thanks: [one line, not a paragraph of apology]

Worked example — Mara to Priya:

"Context: Elena asked for a Q3 vendor-spend one-pager for Friday 10:00 steering. I have finance access and the 2024 file (external vendors over $10k, fiscal Q, excluding intercompany).

What I tried: Matched this year's extract to that definition. Wiki search for 'intercompany vendor' returned a 2022 deck with a broken link. IDs 4401 and 4402 look like sister-company legal names but sit in the vendor table.

Ask: For this report, should 4401 and 4402 be excluded as intercompany?

By: 3:30 today if possible — I send Elena a skeleton at 4:00.

Good looks like: yes/no, and any other IDs I should treat the same.

If I don't hear: I will exclude them and footnote 'classified as intercompany pending procurement confirm.' Happy to correct after.

Thank you — I know this is a small question in a full calendar."

That message is longer than Jonah's. It is shorter than a meeting. Priya can answer from a phone in forty seconds.

A spoken version, if you catch her in a doorway:

"Priya — twenty seconds. Friday vendor page. Two IDs I think are intercompany, 4401 and 4402. I'm excluding them unless you say they stay. Is that wrong?"

Same template, compressed. You still did the homework. You still offered a default. You did not ask her to stand in a corridor and teach you procurement.

Fast path

The Fastest Reliable Method

When you have one shot in Slack and the expert is moving:

  1. One line of context.
  2. One line of what you already did.
  3. One question that can be answered with a yes, a no, a number, or a link.
  4. A time.

"Elena's Friday vendor page. I matched the 2024 definition; stuck on IDs 4401/4402 — intercompany or keep? Need a nod by 3:30. I'll footnote-exclude if silent."

If they reply "wiki," ask for the link, once, without sarcasm. If the wiki is empty, you now have a fact, not a vibe.

The hack

The Hack

Write the default first.

If you cannot state what you will do without them, you do not understand the decision yet. The default forces Think. It also makes the expert's job "correct this" instead of "invent an answer from fog," which is a lower-status-threat request. People will fix a wrong specific faster than they will fill a blank.

Do not fake a default you are not allowed to use. If only Priya can classify vendors, say that: "I cannot ship this number without your call. What is the smallest thing you need from me to make it?"

AI move

The AI Move

AI can draft the request. It cannot tell you whether you have done enough homework, and it will make the tone either groveling or robotic if you let it.

Use AI for: tightening a rambling draft into the five parts; checking that you asked one question, not five; a tone pass: "remove apology, keep respect"; role-playing a busy expert's likely reply so you can pre-empt a missing piece.

Do not use AI for: inventing what you tried; guessing the expert's personality from a public profile in a creepy way; sending a message you have not read aloud.

Prompt (copy, then fill):

Context: I need to ask a busy expert for help without making them do my homework.
Here is the situation:
[facts]
Here is what I have already tried:
[bullets — only real work]
The specific thing only they can provide:
[one sentence]
Deadline and why:
[time + reason]
My relationship to them:
[new / peer / they are senior / they are territorial]

Goal: Draft an Expert Request.
1) A written version under 150 words with: Context, What I tried, Ask, By, Good looks like, optional default.
2) A 20-second spoken version.
3) A list of anything I still need to try before sending, so I am not dumping homework.

Constraints: No groveling. No fake warmth. No "circle back" or "pick your brain." Do not invent work I did not do. Flag if my ask is still a cloud.
Output: Two drafts plus a pre-send checklist.
Verification: Tone risks given the relationship I described.

What to verify: every claim about what you tried. If the model "helpfully" adds "I searched the wiki and the 2024 file," and you did not, you are now lying with better grammar.

Do not paste: the expert's performance reviews, private HR context, medical details, anything that would shame you if forwarded.

Tech

The Technology Move

The job is "put a complete ask where they will actually see it, without creating a meeting by default."

Current examples of the category: async first (email, ticket, a documented chat) so they can answer at 3:28 from a phone; a screenshot of the two IDs, because a picture is sometimes the whole context; a calendar hold as an offer, not a kidnapping — "I can do five minutes at 3:00 if that's cheaper than typing"; a ticket if the expert is really a function (IT, legal, compliance), because those systems are how they are measured.

When not to use technology: a @channel cry for help. A meeting titled "Quick sync" with no agenda. A document with comments instead of a question. Those are ways to spread the homework across a crowd.

Mistake

The Common Mistake

Asking before you have a question.

You feel lost, so you send the lostness. The expert now has to do See, Think, and Find for you. That is not mentorship. That is you handing them your job.

The sibling mistake: never asking, then guessing on a number that can hurt people. Pride is not resourcefulness. The loop includes Find. People are a valid bucket.

Recovery: if you already sent a cloud, do not send a second cloud of apology. Send the template: "Updating my earlier note with a specific ask:" and then the five parts. People forgive a cleanup. They do not forgive a thread of vibes.

Defense

The Defense

The Expert Request fails when:

  • The person is not an expert; they are a bottleneck who enjoys being asked. You will learn this when their answers are vague and their calendar is a throne. Then you need a different path — a document, a second source, Elena.
  • The question is actually yours to decide and you are hiding. "What would you do?" can be cowardice. If you own the call, own it, and ask for information, not permission dressed as curiosity.
  • There is a safety, legal, or medical issue. Do not "default" your way around a license. Ask, and wait.
  • You use the default as a threat in a political fight. That is not this tool. That is a shove. We will teach conflict later without teaching you to ambush Priya at 3:29.

Limitation: a perfect request cannot make a burned-out expert care. It can only make answering easy. If they still do not answer, you Adapt: Elena, a second person, a footnoted unknown. That is the loop, not a failure of charm.

Council

The Masters' Table

Benjamin Franklin. The Junto: a club for asking well, bringing a question, and leaving with other people's knowledge. Through Franklin's lens, networks are not "personal brand." They are a Find step you maintain with useful questions and returned favors.

Andrew Grove. Specify the input. A manager's time is a production resource. A fuzzy ask is a defective batch. High output is partly the discipline of what you put on other people's desks.

Herbert Simon. The expert is a satisficing shortcut. You are not going to become Priya by Friday. You need the one distinction she has already paid for with years. Ask for that distinction, not her career.

Richard Feynman. The honest edge of ignorance, spoken without theatre. "I do not know whether these two IDs are intercompany" is a scientific sentence. "I'm so lost, any guidance?" is a status sentence.

Marie Curie. Collaboration with specialists — she worked with chemists, industrial partners, physicians — without pretending the collaboration replaced measurement. Ask the person. Still check the extract.

A good reference librarian's lens, again: the best patrons arrive with what they already tried. The worst arrive with "I need everything on vendors." Be the first kind of patron, even when the "library" is a human.

Synthesis. Prepare, specify, bound the time, close the loop. Asking is part of competence, not a subtraction from it.

Where the council could be wrong. Franklin's networking can become using people. Grove's production language can make you treat Priya as a machine. Feynman's blunt edge can sound arrogant from a third-day employee. Soften the edges; keep the skeleton. If your culture punishes any ask, that is a Bad Room, not proof that you should guess.

Best move

The Best-Possible Response

Back to Tuesday, 2:14.

Calm. Mara does not fire Jonah's one-liner. She also does not write a grovel.

Observe. Access: yes. 2024 definition: yes. Two ambiguous IDs. Priya is the likely owner. Calendar green. Skeleton due 4:00.

Define. The job of this message is a classification, not a tour of procurement.

Verify. She re-checks the wiki, so "what I tried" is true. She writes down 4401 and 4402 correctly.

Prioritize. A message Priya can answer on a phone beats a meeting request.

Find resources / people. Priya first. Elena only if Priya cannot reply and the number is material. Jonah is not the expert; he is a peer with opinions.

Options. Guess. Cloud-ask. Jonah's yes/no. Full Expert Request with a default.

Risks. Default might annoy a territorial owner. Cloud-ask will get silence. Guess might misstate spend in The Room.

Choose. Full request, polite default, Elena not CC'd yet.

Communicate. The worked message above, sent at 2:22.

Act. While waiting, she builds the rest of the page with a red box on the two IDs so she is not blocked on layout.

Document. The ask, the time, the default, in the LOOP note.

Adapt. Priya replies at 3:10: "4401 exclude. 4402 is a real vendor — different legal name, not intercompany. Don't use a blanket rule." Mara's default was half wrong, which is why you ask. She corrects, thanks Priya with the specific outcome, and moves.

Reflect. The homework (IDs, 2024 definition, wiki miss) is what made a forty-second answer possible. Next time she will not even be tempted by "pick your brain."

That is a resourceful human with a senior person in another city. Not charming. Clear.

Practice

Practice

Do this now. Ten minutes.

  1. Pick a live thing you are stuck on that a human probably knows.
  2. Write the five parts on paper before you open chat.
  3. Cut until the ask is one question.
  4. Write a default, or write "I am not allowed to default this because ___."
  5. Read it out loud. If you hear groveling or a cloud, cut again.
  6. Optional: run the prompt, then compare. Keep the version you would respect if you were Priya at 2:14 with a green calendar.

Do not send yet if this is the practice block — unless the Field Mission is now. The skill is the draft.

Real life

Real-Life Challenge

Work — a territorial expert. Situation: you need one fact from someone who hates "quick questions." What most people do: delay, then send a long apology. Why it fails: the apology is more to read than the ask. Resourceful response: Expert Request, proof of work, a default if you are allowed. Words to use: context, tried, ask, by, good looks like. Words to avoid: "pick your brain," "sorry to bother," "whenever you get a chance," "quick sync." Tool: Expert Request. AI assist: tone pass only, after the five parts are true. What could go wrong: they reply "this is your job." Then: "Agreed. The part I cannot see from here is X. If you can confirm that, I'll own the rest."

Family — a relative who actually knows. Situation: a parent or in-law has the history of a bill, a house, a medical file. What most people do: a rambling call that turns into an old fight. Why it fails: the cloud-ask invites the fight. Resourceful response: "I pulled the last three statements. I cannot see why the April charge doubled. Do you remember a change in April, or should I call the provider?" Words to use: what you already gathered, one question. Words to avoid: "You always know this stuff," which is flattery and a trap. Tool: spoken Expert Request. What could go wrong: they take over. Thank them for the specific fact and keep the next action.

Travel — a human behind a desk. Situation: missed connection, a queue, an agent with a screen you cannot see. What most people do: a story about the trip. Why it fails: they need a record locator, not a narrative. Resourceful response: "Missed [flight] on ticket [code]. Same itinerary today, or hotel voucher if not. I already checked the app — it still shows the old gate." Words to use: ticket, flight, the specific outcome you need. Words to avoid: "This is unacceptable" as an opening. You can escalate later if the system fails you. Tool: Fastest version. What could go wrong: the agent is not the expert (wrong desk). Ask: "Is this the desk that can rebook this ticket?" That is also a specific ask.

Bad room

Bad-Room Version

Priya replies, public, in a thread Elena can see: "The definitions are obvious if you read the file. Please don't ping me for basic work." Jonah looks at his keyboard.

Status heat. The Pause first. Then do not defend your honor in the thread.

Mara, privately to Priya, still using the tool: "Understood — I had read the 2024 file; 4402 is labeled as a vendor and a sister-company name, which is why I asked instead of guessing a steering number. I'll wait for your yes/no on 4402. If Thursday is the first window, I'll footnote it for Friday and correct after."

To Elena, if Elena saw the slap: "I asked Priya one classification question after matching the 2024 file. 4402 is still ambiguous. I'll ship a footnoted number rather than invent it."

You did not make the expert do your homework. You also did not accept a public story that you did. Bad rooms try to turn asking into a status crime. The template is your evidence.

If Priya is simply cruel, that is a management problem, not a reason to guess on a material number. Document the ask. Use the default. Move.

Reflect

Reflection

  • What do you usually omit: what you tried, the deadline, or what good looks like?
  • Whom have you been asking to hold your whole confusion?
  • When did not asking cost more than a tight question would have?
  • What default are you avoiding writing because it would force you to think?
  • After someone helps you, do you close the loop with the outcome?
Remember

Remember This

Ask for a correction to homework you already did. Do not mail someone your fog.

Deep dive

Optional Deep Dive

Status, help, and the myth of the "stupid question."

There are no stupid questions, says the poster. There are expensive questions: ones that transfer your unstarted work onto a person who cannot say no. There are also expensive silences: guessing on a number that will be read in The Room. The Expert Request is a way to pay a fair price — your preparation — for a scarce resource — their distinction.

Research on advice-seeking (for example, work by Alison Wood Brooks and colleagues on asking for advice, and related social-psychology on help-seeking) often finds that people overestimate how annoyed a competent helper will be, and underestimate how much a specific request can raise their standing. Treat that as a useful direction, not as a license to spam seniors. The studies are about reasonably framed asks, not "got a sec?"

A harder variation: the expert is outside your company — a vendor, a clinician's scheduler, a government clerk. The template still holds. They have a script; your job is to arrive with the identifiers their script needs (account number, case ID, what you already tried on the portal). You will feel the urge to tell the story of your day. The story is for your friends. The clerk needs the record locator.

If you lead, reward the template. When Mara sends the five parts, answer the ask first, then optionally add the extra. If you only ever reward people who never ask, you will get surprises in steering meetings. Grove would call those surprises a process failure. He would be right.

Mission

Field Mission

In the next 24–48 hours, send one real Expert Request.

Constraints: five parts, one question, no "pick your brain." If you cannot send it yet, deliver the draft to the person you would have asked and ask them to rate whether they could answer it in sixty seconds.

After they reply, write four lines: what you asked, what you had tried, what they gave you, what you still had to own. That last line is the point. Help is not a transfer of the job.

Sources to Explore

  • Alison Wood Brooks, Francesca Gino, and Maurice E. Schweitzer, "Smart People Ask for (My) Advice," Management Science, 2015 — evidence that asking for advice can raise perceptions of competence, under conditions; not a blank check.
