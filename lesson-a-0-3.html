How Capable People Think

See, think, find, choose, act, adapt, reflect

20 minThe Figure-It-Out Loop1,123 words22 sections
Skill
Use one loop for almost any problem
Tool
The Figure-It-Out Loop
Result
You stop waiting to feel ready
In one line
Ready is a feeling. The loop is a sequence.
The idea

The Hook

Watch a capable person enter a mess.

They do not look like a movie. They do not have a speech. They look like someone who has a sequence. They get quiet for a second. They name what is in front of them. They find a missing piece. They pick a next move that is allowed to be ugly. They watch what reality does. They change.

That sequence is this course, compressed.

See. Think. Find. Choose. Act. Adapt. Reflect.

Later: become wise — which is the loop plus the question what actually matters?

If you only steal one thing from these opening chapters, steal this loop. Level 3 will teach it as a full craft. You get it now so every later tool has a home.

The room

The Scenario

You start a job on Monday. The software has no manual. Your manager is in three time zones. A customer email is already waiting with a subject line in all caps. A colleague says "you'll pick it up" and walks away.

You are the least experienced person in the building. The building does not care.

What do you do in the first hour that is not theater?

Your move

Your Move

Common move. Pretend. Nod. Browse files at random. Send a cheerful Slack. Anxiety wearing a costume.

Better move. Ask twelve questions at once in the group channel. You will get noise and a reputation for helplessness.

Expert move. See: what is actually on fire (the customer email). Think: the job of hour one is contain, not master. Find: the last similar ticket, the person who owned it, the official doc. Choose: a holding reply plus one expert request. Act. Adapt when the expert says "we do it this other way." Reflect in four lines at 5 p.m.

Resourceful move. The expert move, without performing competence you do not have. "I don't know yet" is a legal sentence in a capable mouth.

The idea

The Big Idea

You will never have complete information at the moment of action. Capable people are not people who got complete information. They are people who can move through incomplete information without becoming random or frozen.

The loop is how.

Explained

The Simple Explanation

See. What is here, if a camera recorded it? Not the story. Level 2 lives here.

Think. What might it mean? What am I assuming? What would change my mind?

Find. Information, people, tools, money, time, precedents. Level 3 lives here.

Choose. A next move, not a life identity. Level 7 lives here.

Act. A useful, bounded action. Level 5 lives here.

Adapt. Reality gets a vote. If the move failed, that is data.

Reflect. What did I learn that transfers? This is how you stop paying full price for the same lesson.

Become wise. Not a step you skip to. A pressure that acts on choosing: character, enough, the person you are becoming. Level 8.

People skip See and jump to Act. Or they live in Find forever. Or they Act and never Reflect, so they get ten years of experience that is one year repeated ten times.

The tool

The Practical Tool

Name: Loop on one page

Draw seven boxes. Fill them in order. If you cannot fill See, you are not allowed to Choose yet — unless the building is actually on fire, in which case Act is "get out," and you can think later.

Time-box Find. The Enough Test (Level 3) exists because Find is addictive to the conscientious.

Fast path

The Fastest Reliable Method

Three questions, ninety seconds:

  1. What is true.
  2. What is the next useful action.
  3. Who already knows.

Then do 2 or ask 3.

The hack

The Hack

Name which verb you are in. Out loud if you are alone. "I am still in Find." It keeps you from mixing research with self-attack.

AI move

The AI Move

Context: I am running a figure-it-out loop. Situation:
[facts]
I think I am in the [See/Think/Find/Choose/Act] step.
Goal: Tell me if I skipped a step. Ask me the missing question. Propose two next actions, one cheap and reversible.
Constraints: No generic project plan.
Verification: What you cannot know.
Tech

The Technology Move

Notes app with a template of the seven verbs. A ticket system is a loop if you use it that way. A ticket system is a graveyard if you don't.

Mistake

The Common Mistake

Waiting to feel ready. Readiness is often just familiarity. The loop produces familiarity by contact, not by mood.

Defense

The Defense

The loop is not for grief in the first hour, not for medical emergencies, not for abuse. Sequence is for problems that can be worked. Some situations need a human, a hospital, or a door.

Council

The Masters' Table

Boyd / OODA (observe, orient, decide, act) — a military loop with a similar skeleton. We add Find and Reflect because civilian life hides information in people and repeats without learning.

Deming. The PDCA cycle: the system learns if you close it.

Feynman. Guess, compute, compare to experiment. Adapt is the comparison.

Grove. Output is what remains after you stop admiring your analysis.

Where the council could be wrong. Some art and some love do not want a loop. Do not process a kiss.

Best move

The Best-Possible Response

Monday, hour one. You read the angry email. You write three facts. You find last month's similar thread. You send a holding sentence to the customer and one specific question to the colleague who walked away. You do not try to understand the whole company. You close one loop. You are already more useful than the person performing omniscience.

Practice

Practice

Take a current mess. Fill the seven boxes. Put a time next to Act. Do Act or shrink it until you can.

Real life

Real-Life Challenge

Travel. Missed flight. See the board. Think: overnight vs rebook. Find: desk, app, hotel, charger. Choose. Act. Adapt when the desk's story differs from the app.

Home. Recurring fight. See the last specific incident, not the decade. The loop still works. Kindness is part of Choose.

Bad room

Bad-Room Version

Everyone is panicking. Your loop becomes visible calm: "Two facts, one unknown, one next action." You become a metronome. Metronomes are underrated in fire drills.

Reflect

Reflection

  • Which verb do you overuse?
  • Which do you skip when you are afraid?
  • Where did you last wait to feel ready?
Remember

Remember This

You don't need the whole map. You need the next honest step, then contact with what happens.

Deep dive

Optional Deep Dive

The ultimate learner outcome of this course is not a certificate. It is the ability to walk into a new job, a new country, a broken process, a room of experts, a crisis, or a situation where nobody knows the answer, and think: okay. Let's understand what is happening. Let's figure this out. The loop is that sentence, made operational.

Mission

Field Mission

Run the loop once in the next 24 hours on something small and real. Write the seven verbs and one line each.
