Card #21: The Client Who Ghosts After a Verbal Commitment

275 words

The Situation

A prospective client verbally committed to a deal at the end of a great meeting. Two weeks later, they've stopped responding to emails.

What Most People Do

Send progressively desperate follow-ups. Or: give up and mentally close the deal as lost. Or: assume they must be angry about something and try to guess what.

Why It Fails

Escalating desperation reads as neediness and damages your negotiating position on this deal or any future one. Giving up abandons a real possibility that a simple recovery move would have resolved. Guessing at hidden anger creates paranoid dynamics that sometimes materialize.

The Resourceful Response

Send ONE more well-crafted message, then MOVE ON publicly. The clean move: 'I've reached out a few times without hearing back. That's fine — timing is hard. I'm going to stop reaching out for now. If circumstances change, I'd love to hear from you.' Then actually stop.

Words To Use

"I wanted to close the loop rather than keep adding noise. It sounds like the timing may not be right on your end — no need to explain. If things shift, I'd be glad to pick this up again. In the meantime, wishing you well."

"No response needed."

Words To Avoid

"Just following up on my previous message…"

"Circling back in case my last email got missed…"

"Just wondering if you had a chance to consider…"

What Can Still Go Wrong

Sometimes clients come back weeks or months later — and the clean closure message is why they feel comfortable doing so. Sometimes they don't. Either outcome is fine. You have preserved your dignity and your negotiating stance for the next opportunity.
