30 Thinkers Worth Sitting With · Modern Psychology & Decision Science

Carol Dweck

174 words

1946–, Usa

The belief that you can change is what enables you to change.

What They Contributed

Dweck's research on 'mindset' distinguishes fixed mindset (believing abilities are innate and static) from growth mindset (believing abilities develop through effort). People with growth mindset outperform on learning, resilience, and long-term development — not because they are more talented, but because they engage with challenge differently.

How To Apply It Now

For learning (Level 3.1), difficult conversations (Level 4.3), and personal development (Playbook 5): adopting a growth mindset is not motivational fluff. It is the underlying assumption that makes the practices in this book work. If you believe your capability is fixed, you won't do the practice.

Where Their Thinking Breaks

The 'growth mindset' concept has been over-simplified and mis-implemented widely, particularly in schools. Praising effort alone without competence, or telling people they can do anything without providing structural support, has produced real harm. Dweck herself has clarified this repeatedly.

One Book Or Source

Mindset: The New Psychology of Success (2006), with the later revisions in mind.
