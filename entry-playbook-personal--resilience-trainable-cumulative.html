Personal Intelligence

Resilience — trainable, cumulative

219 words
Note

THE IDEA — Resilience is not what you have. It is what you have built.

Note

The people who bounce back from setbacks fastest are not tougher by nature. They have accumulated more bounces — small ones — and thereby know that they can. Volume of small setbacks handled well produces resilience for larger ones. Start with the small ones.

Note

THE GREAT MINDS COUNCIL — On knowing yourself

Note

Through Carl Jung's lens: 'Until you make the unconscious conscious, it will direct your life and you will call it fate.' Through Marcus Aurelius's lens: 'Look within. Within is the fountain of good, and it will ever bubble up, if thou wilt ever dig.' Through Socrates's lens: 'The unexamined life is not worth living.' Not a moral judgment. An empirical claim. Through Nassim Taleb's lens: 'The three most harmful addictions are heroin, carbohydrates, and a monthly salary.' The last one is more dangerous than the first two combined, because it disguises itself as safety. Through Viktor Frankl's lens: 'When we are no longer able to change a situation, we are challenged to change ourselves.' Personal intelligence is the discipline of that change. Where the Council might be wrong: Self-improvement can become self-obsession. At some point, look up from the mirror.

Remember This

“Know yourself well enough to work with yourself, not against.”
