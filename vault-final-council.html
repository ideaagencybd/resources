The Great Minds, One Last Time

645 words 4 entries From book

On what actually matters.

Now, one final time, we convene the Council. Not for a specific problem — for the largest question the book has been circling around.

What does a well-lived life look like?

No consensus exists. That is the point. Below, the thinkers who have contributed to this book offer their answers. Not as verdicts — as hypotheses to test against your own experience.

THROUGH THE LENS OF……A WELL-LIVED LIFE IS ONE THAT…
Aristotle…exercises virtue over time; flourishes (eudaimonia) through practice, not accident.
Marcus Aurelius…serves duty, treats others with charity, and remains unmoved by things outside one's control.
Epictetus…is spent working only on what is in your power. Everything else is passing weather.
Viktor Frankl…finds meaning by asking what life is asking of YOU, not what you can get from it.
Confucius…cultivates ren (humaneness) — relationships done well with sustained attention.
Buddha…reduces suffering — one's own and others' — through presence, non-attachment, and compassion.
Jesus (the ethical teacher)…loves neighbor as self; forgives; serves; treats the least the way you would be treated.
Naval Ravikant…is spent with a calm mind, a fit body, and a house full of love — none of which can be bought.
Warren Buffett…is measured at 80 by how many of those you love still love you back.
Charlie Munger…avoids the standard stupidities and lets compounding do the rest.
Steve Jobs…is spent doing work you love, with people you respect, on things that matter to someone.
Jeff Bezos…minimizes long-term regret. Ask 80-year-old you.
Angela Duckworth…sustains interest and effort toward things worth sustaining them for.
Carol Dweck…is spent growing, not defending an identity of already being enough.
James Clear…is the sum of small, deliberate, kept promises to yourself and others.
Nelson Mandela…is spent standing up for something, and forgiving what cannot be undone.
Winston Churchill…endures. Never surrenders. Keeps its humor. Does not give up on hard things.
