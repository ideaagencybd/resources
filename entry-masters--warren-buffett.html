30 Thinkers Worth Sitting With · Strategic & Practical Thought

Warren Buffett

191 words

1930–, Usa

Patience compounds. So does foolishness.

What They Contributed

Buffett's investing record is legendary, but his more durable contribution is his articulation of a philosophy of long-term thinking, cost-consciousness, and discipline against fashion. His annual letters to Berkshire Hathaway shareholders are among the most readable business documents in existence.

How To Apply It Now

For any career, business, or long-term project: Buffett's discipline of patience, of avoiding the 'unforced errors,' and of maintaining a clear circle of competence is directly applicable. His observation that 'you only have to do a very few things right in your life so long as you don't do too many things wrong' is the compression of a lifetime of practice.

Where Their Thinking Breaks

Buffett's approach is possible partly because of his specific circumstances (capital, time, tax structure). Not every principle transfers to someone with less flexibility. Also: 'don't do too many things wrong' is easier to say than to practice; his specific avoidance of tech in the '90s was as much luck as skill.

One Book Or Source

The Essays of Warren Buffett (edited by Lawrence Cunningham) — the annual letters organized by topic.
