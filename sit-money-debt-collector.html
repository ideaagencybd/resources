A debt collector is contacting you aggressively

medium The Paper Traildebtcollectionsrightsmoney
1

What is happening

Calls at work, texts at night, letters in red type, threats about doorstep visits and court. Possibly for a debt you do not recognise, or one you thought was settled years ago.

2

What is actually going on

  • Collections is a psychological operation. Volume and urgency are chosen to produce payment before you check whether the debt is valid, correctly sized, or still enforceable.
  • You have the right to require proof. A surprising proportion of purchased debt has no proper paperwork behind it, and without it the claim is weak.
  • Debts can become statute-barred after a defined period with no acknowledgement or payment. A small "goodwill" payment can restart that clock, which is why they push for one.
  • Collectors are subject to regulation on conduct and hours. Documented breaches are leverage.
3

Do this, in this order

  1. Move everything to writingStop discussing amounts on the phone. Say you will correspond in writing and then do it.
  2. Request proof of the debtAsk for the original credit agreement, a full statement of account, and evidence of assignment to them. Do not admit the debt while asking.
  3. Do not pay anything until it is verifiedNot a token payment, not a "gesture." Verify first.
  4. Check the date of last activityThe last payment or written acknowledgement determines whether it is even enforceable.
  5. Log every contactDate, time, number, who called, what was said, what was threatened. This is the record that ends misconduct.
  6. Get free debt adviceA national debt charity will tell you in minutes whether this is valid, and can take over correspondence.
4

Words you can borrow

First response
"I do not acknowledge this debt. Please send me the original agreement, a full statement, and proof of assignment. I will not discuss it by phone."
On calls at work
"Do not call me at work. All contact in writing to my address. This call is being logged."
On threats
"Please confirm in writing that court proceedings have been issued, with the claim number. If they have not, stop describing them as imminent."
If it is valid and you can pay something
"I accept the balance of £X. I can pay £Y monthly. Confirm in writing that interest and charges are frozen and I will start on the 1st."
5

What makes it worse

  • Paying a token amount to make the calls stop. It can reset limitation and it confirms liability.
  • Agreeing to an unaffordable plan under pressure. A broken plan is worse than a lower honest one.
  • Giving continuous payment authority on a card. Standing order or direct debit keeps control with you.
  • Ignoring an actual court claim. Court documents have hard deadlines and default judgments are entered automatically.
  • Opening the door to a doorstep collector. Most have no powers of entry — that is bailiffs with a court order, which is different.
6

Once the pressure drops

  • Get written confirmation of any settlement, including the words "full and final" if that is what was agreed.
  • Check your credit file afterwards and dispute anything wrong. Errors persist for years by default.
  • If conduct breached the rules, complain in writing and then escalate to the regulator or ombudsman. It is free.
  • Deal with the underlying budget. Collections is a symptom.
Where this stops being yours to handle

If a court claim form arrives, respond within the stated deadline even if you dispute it, and get advice the same week. Ignoring it converts a disputable claim into an enforceable judgment against you automatically.

A note on this library

These are general principles written to be useful under pressure, not legal, medical, or financial advice. Laws and emergency procedures differ by country. If life is at risk, contact your emergency services first and read this afterwards.
