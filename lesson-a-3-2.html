Search Like a Professional

Finding is a skill, not a personality trait

26 minSearch / Compare / Verify4,298 words22 sections
Skill
Get to the right information fast
Tool
Search / Compare / Verify
Result
You can research an unfamiliar topic in 30 minutes
In one line
The internet is a warehouse. Searching is knowing which door.
The idea

The Hook

Two people paste the same error message into a search box.

One finds a 2017 forum post, copies a command, and disables a safety setting. The machine goes quiet. So does a piece of the system that was doing a job they did not understand.

The other person puts the error in quotes, adds the product name and the version, and filters for the official documentation. They find a known issue, a patch, and a sentence that says "do not disable X." Same internet. Different afternoon.

Finding is not a personality trait and it is not "being good with computers." It is a short sequence: search on purpose, compare what you found, verify before you trust. The Figure-It-Out Loop's Find step is a door. This lesson is how you walk through it without setting the building on fire.

The room

The Scenario

Jonah is staring at a red banner.

He is installing a reporting add-on Elena wants before Friday. The banner says: Error 0x80070005: Access is denied. Failed to register component. He pastes the whole sentence into search. The first result is a Reddit thread. The top comment: "Just run it as admin and turn off the firewall. Worked for me." The second result is a Stack Overflow answer from 2016 with a 400-vote command that edits permissions on a system folder. The third is a video titled FIX Access Denied FOREVER.

Jonah's cursor is in the terminal. Elena is in a meeting until 3:00. The add-on is supposed to be live for a dry run at 4:00.

What does he search next, and what does he refuse to click?

Your move

Your Move

Write the next query you would actually type. Then look.

Common move. Click the highest-voted post. Run the command. If it fails, try the video. You have outsourced judgment to whoever showed up first and spoke with certainty.

Better move. Add the product name. Still click a forum, but read the comments for "that broke my install." You have added a little caution. You still have no idea whether the post matches your version.

Expert move. Exact-match the error. Add version. Prefer official docs and the vendor's issue tracker. Treat forums as experience reports, not instructions. Before any command, ask: is this reversible, and does it match my setup?

Resourceful move. Search / Compare / Verify as a named loop. Three queries, three kinds of source, one comparison pass, then a reversible action — or a stop. Jonah is not trying to become a systems programmer by 4:00. He is trying not to brick a machine for a dry run.

Why the common move fails: search engines rank engagement, not safety. Old answers linger because they were useful once. A command that "worked for me" on a home PC in 2016 is not a procedure for a company laptop with disk encryption and a central policy.

What can go wrong with the expert move: official docs can be wrong, late, or written for a different edition. "Official" is a strong prior, not a sacrament. That is why Compare exists.

The idea

The Big Idea

Search is not typing a worry into a box. It is aiming a question at the right kind of door.

The amateur searches for comfort: "how do I fix this." The professional searches for a match: this product, this version, this error, this date, this kind of source. Then they hold two or three answers next to each other until the disagreement is visible. Then they verify.

You will use this on error messages, on regulations, and on claims that sound medical. The last one has a hard rule: you are not a doctor, and a confident paragraph is not care.

Explained

The Simple Explanation

Three passes. If you skip the second or third, you are scrolling, not searching.

Search. Make the query do work. Default search is a popularity contest. You can do better with a handful of operators that have been stable for years:

  • "exact phrase" — use for error strings, law titles, quoted claims.
  • site: — aim at a domain. site:learn.microsoft.com, site:gov, site:who.int.
  • -word — drop noise. -pinterest -forum when you want a manual, not a mood board.
  • filetype:pdf — useful for standards, papers, official guidance.
  • OR — when a thing has two names. "vendor spend" OR "third-party spend".
  • A year or recency filter — because a 2016 permissions fix is a different planet.

Add the version. Always. Software, policy, and medical guidance all rot.

Compare. Open at least two kinds of source, not two tabs of the same kind.

KindWhat it is good forWhat it is bad for
Official docs / primary textWhat the maker or the law actually saysJargon; lag; missing the "what people actually do"
Primary research / original paperMethods, caveats, actual findingOverclaim in the abstract; you still need the body
Forums, Reddit, Stack OverflowExperience: "this happened to me on version 12.4"Instructions treated as gospel; selection bias; dead versions
VideoDemonstration — where to click, what it looks likeCharisma; missing preconditions; unauditable steps
News / LinkedIn / blogsThat a conversation existsIncentive, compression, and invented certainty

Verify. Before you act, match four things: product, version, date, reversibility. If any one fails, you do not run the command, cite the regulation, or change a health behavior on the basis of a thread.

A worked pattern for an unfamiliar error:

  1. Copy the exact string, not your paraphrase.
  2. Query: "Error 0x80070005" "register component" [product] [version].
  3. Second query: site:[vendor-docs] 0x80070005 [product].
  4. Third query: [product] status or the vendor's known-issues page — maybe it is an outage, not your machine.
  5. Compare: does the official page mention this error? Do the forum posts share your version and your setup (admin vs standard user, managed device vs home)?
  6. Verify: if a fix wants you to disable a firewall, UAC, antivirus, or a policy — stop. That is usually someone solving a different problem with a grenade.

Forums are diaries. Diaries are useful. They are not manuals.

The tool

The Practical Tool

Name: Search / Compare / Verify

Use it whenever the answer is "out there" and the cost of a wrong one is real.

  1. Write the question as a match, not a mood. Not "why is this broken." Instead: "What does error 0x80070005 mean for [product] version [x] when registering a component on a managed Windows laptop?"
  2. Build three queries on purpose.
  • Exact-match + product + version.
  • site: aimed at official docs or the relevant government domain.
  • One experience query (forum, issue tracker) with the version included.
  1. Open one of each kind, not ten of one kind. Official. Experience. (And if the claim is factual: a primary or a quality secondary — a handbook, a regulator, a review.)
  2. Fill a three-line compare. What they agree on. Where they disagree. What would have to be true for the forum fix to apply to you.
  3. Verify before action. Product match? Version match? Date sane? Reversible? If the action is a health, legal, or safety change, the next step is a qualified human, not a comment with upvotes.
  4. Act small, or stop. One reversible change, or a ticket with the exact string, version, and what you already ruled out.

Three doors, three different jobs.

Error message (Jonah's banner). Door 1: vendor docs and known issues. Door 2: the company's internal wiki or ticket history — someone here has seen this. Door 3: a forum for experience, read for setup match, not for a command to paste. Likely real answer: he lacks a permission his admin must grant. The firewall-off ritual is a stranger's workaround for a home PC.

Regulation. Scenario, not a news story: Elena forwards a LinkedIn post, "New rule: all vendor contracts over $10k need extra approval starting Monday." Door 1: the regulator's own site (site:gov or the relevant official gazette), current text, effective date. Door 2: your counsel or compliance person, with the citation in hand. Door 3: a trade-press summary, used only to find the citation, never as the rule. LinkedIn is a rumor with formatting.

Medical-looking claim. A relative sends an article: "This supplement reverses inflammation — study says." Door 1: the paper, not the headline. Who was studied, how many, what was measured, who paid. Door 2: a quality review if it exists (a systematic review, a regulator's page, a major clinic's consumer page). Door 3: your clinician, because you are not going to dose a body from a search lesson. This course will say this until it is boring: you are not a doctor; verify with professionals. Search here is how you stop being a vector for a confident lie, not how you diagnose.

Fast path

The Fastest Reliable Method

Ninety seconds, one claim, one error, one rumor:

  1. Put the distinctive string in quotes.
  2. Add the official name and, if you have it, the year or version.
  3. Run one site: query toward the maker, the regulator, or a recognized health authority.
  4. If the first official page disagrees with the viral version, you already have your next sentence: "The post says X. The page that issued the rule says Y. I am going with Y until counsel/clinician/admin says otherwise."

You do not need a research career. You need a habit of aiming.

The hack

The Hack

Search the citation, not the slogan.

When a post says "studies show" or "the new regulation requires," your query is the named study, the bill number, the error code — not the moral. Slogans are optimized to travel. Citations are optimized to be checked. If there is no citation, that is the finding.

Second hack: paste error messages with the variable bits removed. Usernames, paths, IDs. Search the stable skeleton. You will match other people's machines instead of only your unique mess.

AI move

The AI Move

AI is a strong query machine and a weak librarian. It will invent a plausible Microsoft doc title, a plausible paper, a plausible section of a law. Fluency is not a source. You will train this muscle harder in 3.3 and in Level 6. Start now: the model proposes doors; you walk through them.

Use AI for: turning a messy problem into three queries; listing official domains you should try (site: targets); explaining a dense paragraph after you have the paragraph; generating a compare table from sources you pasted.

Do not use AI for: "What does the law say?" without a link you opened; medical advice; a command you have not read against official docs; summarizing a paper the model may be fabricating.

Prompt (copy, then fill):

Context: I need to search like a professional, not get a made-up answer.
Question I am trying to match:
[one sentence]
Exact strings I have (error, claim, title):
[paste]
Product / jurisdiction / version / date if known:
[facts]

Goal:
1) Give me three search queries: (a) exact-match, (b) site: toward an official domain, (c) experience/forum with version.
2) Name the kind of source I should trust first for this question, and why.
3) List what I must match before I act (version, date, reversibility, qualified human).
4) Warn me about the most likely bad first result for this kind of problem.

Constraints: Do not answer the underlying factual question as if you know it. Do not invent citations, URLs, paper titles, or law names. If you are unsure of an official domain, say so.
Output: Queries first, then a short checklist.
Verification: Anything in your output I should treat as a guess.

What to verify: every URL, title, and quote. Open it. If it 404s or does not contain the words, the model hallucinated. Throw it away.

Do not paste: internal error logs with customer data, medical records, credentials, unreleased legal memos.

Tech

The Technology Move

The job is "aim, capture, compare" — not "install a productivity religion."

Current examples of the category: a search engine that still honors operators; the official doc site's own search, which is often worse at ranking and better at matching the product; a browser's reading list or a single note where you paste the three sources and dates; a PDF reader with find, because official guidance is still a PDF more often than vendors admit; for video, playback at speed and a pause to write the actual steps, because a demonstration you cannot repeat is entertainment.

When not to use technology: a second search app will not save you from clicking the first confident fool. The skill is the sequence.

Mistake

The Common Mistake

Querying your anxiety.

"How do I fix access denied" is a mood. It will return moods: videos, listicles, home-PC rituals. "What does 0x80070005 mean when [product] [version] tries to register a component" is a match. It will still return junk. It will also return the page you needed.

The sibling mistake: treating the top forum answer as a primary source. High votes mean "this helped a lot of people in a previous era," not "this is safe on your machine on Tuesday."

Recovery: if you already ran a command you did not understand, stop stacking more. Write what you did, in order. Search the official undo. Tell a human with the log. Do not "try one more thing" from a second stranger.

Defense

The Defense

Search / Compare / Verify fails when:

  • There is no official page and no primary, only vibes. Then you do not have a finding. You have a rumor. Say that.
  • The official page is three years behind the product you are holding. Note the date. Use experience reports as clues, then confirm with support or a test that cannot destroy anything.
  • You are in a language or jurisdiction the index barely covers. Local official sites, a human, a translator you can check — not a model inventing a statute.
  • Speed is being used as a weapon on you. "Just Google it" in a Bad Room is sometimes a status move. You can still aim. You do not have to do it on speakerphone.

Limitation: thirty minutes of professional search will not make you a lawyer, a clinician, or a security engineer. It will make you the person who does not paste a 2016 grenade into a 2026 laptop.

Council

The Masters' Table

Richard Feynman. Through his lens, go toward the original, not the lecture about the original. Cargo-cult search is copying a ritual (the command, the supplement, the LinkedIn rule) without the conditions that made it work. Official text and primary papers are how you get closer to the thing.

Marie Curie. Notebooks and measurement. A search that does not capture date, source, and what would disconfirm is not an investigation. It is collecting.

Benjamin Franklin. His commonplace books were a Compare step: other people's observations, kept so they could be weighed. Through Franklin's lens, you do not have to remember the internet. You have to keep the few lines that survived contact.

Herbert Simon. Satisficing applies to search. You are not going to read the entire literature on access-denied errors. You need a stopping rule: official match + version match + reversible next action, or a named human. Endless search is Find with no Choose — you will meet that failure in 3.8.

Andrew Grove. Output. A beautiful trail of tabs is not a fixed add-on. The search should produce either a safe action or a clear blocker for Elena: "This is a permissions error; I need admin, not a workaround."

A reference librarian's lens (the profession, not a celebrity): the first question is "what kind of source answers this?" A statute, a protocol, an experience report, and a demonstration are different objects. Professionals do not use one door for every weather.

Synthesis. Aim at the right kind of door, prefer the original, write down dates, stop when the next action is safe and clear.

Where the council could be wrong. Worship of official docs can miss that vendors lie, lag, or bury bad news. Feynman's original-paper instinct can become snobbery toward useful experience. Simon's stopping rule can fire before you have opened a single primary. Grove's output pressure is how Jonah almost ran the firewall command. The tool has three words. Search without Verify is how smart people make expensive messes.

Best move

The Best-Possible Response

Back to Jonah's red banner.

Calm. Hands off the terminal. The Pause still applies to a cursor.

Observe. Exact error. Product name. Version on the About screen. Managed laptop. Time: dry run at 4:00. Elena in a meeting.

Define. The job is a working add-on, or a named blocker, by 4:00. The job is not "prove I can hack a permissions error."

Verify. He does not yet know whether this is permissions, a known vendor bug, or a company policy blocking registration.

Prioritize. Do not run irreversible commands. Do find whether admin is the real door.

Find resources / people. Vendor docs via site:. Internal ticket search for the error. The admin or help desk. A colleague who installed this last quarter. Forums only as experience.

Options. Reddit command. SO 2016 permission edit. Video. Or Search / Compare / Verify, then a ticket.

Risks. The grenade-fix could violate policy or break the disk encryption stack. Doing nothing until 3:55 guarantees a public failure. A tight ticket at 1:00 might get him a grant by 3:00.

Choose. Three queries. No commands yet.

Communicate. In the ticket, not in a vibe: "Installing [add-on] v[x] for 4:00 dry run. Error 0x80070005 on component register. Official doc [link] describes this as a permissions issue on managed devices. I have not changed firewall or UAC. Need admin install or a policy exception by 3:00. Elena is the requester."

Act. Runs the queries. Official known-issues page: yes, this error, this version, "requires local admin or an approved package." Internal wiki: a six-month-old ticket, same banner, resolved by packaging, not by disabling security. Forum posts that say "turn off firewall" are all home devices.

Document. Links, dates, version, what he did not do.

Adapt. Help desk can push the package by 3:30. Dry run slips 30 minutes. Better than a bricked laptop.

Reflect. The first result was a trap with votes. The winning move was aiming at official + internal, then asking a human with a completed homework assignment. That last part is 3.4. He already did half of it.

Practice

Practice

Do this now. Fifteen minutes.

Pick one live question: an error on your machine, a policy rumor at work, or a health claim you have seen this month.

  1. Write the question as a match (product/jurisdiction, version/date, exact string).
  2. Build three queries: exact-match, site:, experience.
  3. Open one official, one experience, one other (primary, handbook, or quality secondary).
  4. Fill the compare table: agree / disagree / what would disconfirm.
  5. Write the next action, or write "stop — need a qualified human."
  6. If the topic is medical, legal, or safety, the action is not "try it." The action is "bring this source card to a professional" — you will build the full card in 3.3.

You should leave with a filled table, not a new opinion you could have had at minute one.

Real life

Real-Life Challenge

Work — unfamiliar error. Situation: a tool fails with a code, deadline this afternoon. What most people do: first forum command. Why it fails: version mismatch, or a fix that disables a control your company requires. Resourceful response: exact string + version + official docs + internal tickets. Reversible action or a named admin ask. Words to use: the error, the version, "I have not changed security settings," "this matches known issue X." Words to avoid: "I tried a fix I found online," "It's probably nothing." Tool: Search / Compare / Verify. AI assist: query generator only; you open every link. What could go wrong: official docs are stale. Check the page date. Confirm with support if the product has moved.

Family — a medical-looking forward. Situation: a parent sends a video: a food, a supplement, a "they don't want you to know." What most people do: argue, or quietly start taking the thing. Why it fails: argument becomes identity; compliance becomes an uncontrolled experiment. Resourceful response: find the named study or admit there isn't one. Read who was studied. Take the source, not the fight, to a clinician before any change in medication, diet-as-treatment, or supplements that interact. Words to use: "I found the paper / I cannot find a paper. Can we ask [clinician] before anyone changes a dose?" Words to avoid: "That's pseudoscience" as an opening move in a kitchen. True or not, it ends the search. Tool: three doors, then a professional. AI assist: "Help me find the actual paper title, if it exists. Do not summarize health advice." What could go wrong: you become the family skeptic instead of the family searcher. Stay on the sources.

Civic — a regulation rumor. Situation: a post says a new rule starts Monday. Elena wants to change the process this afternoon. What most people do: implement the post. Why it fails: the post compressed, dated, or invented the rule. You now have a fake process and a real cost. Resourceful response: official text, effective date, scope. Then counsel/compliance. Trade press only to find the citation. Words to use: "The post says X. The issuing page says Y, effective [date], applying to [who]. I need a yes from compliance before we change anything." Words to avoid: "I saw online that we have to…" Tool: site: toward the issuer, then a human with authority. What could go wrong: you delay a real rule. Dates cut both ways. Put the effective date on the compare table.

Bad room

Bad-Room Version

Same install. Worse.

Elena, off the meeting for thirty seconds: "Jonah, just Google it. It's an access error. I used to fix those by running as admin." A senior person in the doorway laughs. The 4:00 dry run has a client on the call.

Search still happens. It just has to survive status.

Jonah, out loud, short: "I already hit the official known-issues page — this version needs a packaged install, not a local admin override, on managed machines. If I run it as admin I can violate the disk policy. I have a ticket in with the exact error. I can be ready at 4:00 if they push it, or I can show the client a recorded demo from the last working build. Your call on which failure you prefer."

He has not given a lecture on information literacy. He has compared, verified, and handed Elena a choice. That is professional search in a room that wanted a ritual.

If they order him to run the grenade anyway, he documents the instruction and the risk. Search cannot save you from a boss who wants theatre. It can keep you from volunteering the theatre.

Reflect

Reflection

  • What kind of source do you usually open first, and what kind do you never open?
  • When did a high-vote answer last waste your time — or worse?
  • Which operator will you actually use this week: quotes, site:, or a year filter?
  • Where do you currently treat experience (Reddit, a colleague's memory, a video) as a manual?
  • On health and law, do you search to act, or to arrive at a professional with better questions?
Remember

Remember This

Forums are diaries. Official pages are claims. Neither is a command until they match your version and survive a check.

Deep dive

Optional Deep Dive

A short field guide to "primary."

In science, primary usually means the original study, not the press release. In law, it means the statute, regulation, or opinion, not the explainer. In software, it means the vendor's doc or the source, not a blog with a screenshot. In history, it means the document from the time, not the documentary. The word shifts. The habit does not: get as close as you can to the thing that did the saying, then read the date.

Stack Overflow and Reddit have a specific use that professionals actually rely on: edge cases, error text that never made the manual, and the social information that "this broke after last Tuesday's update." Read them like a detective reads witnesses. Witnesses are invaluable. Witnesses are biased, partial, and sometimes lying for status. Look for: version numbers, exact steps, other people saying "this also happened to me on X," and a reply from a maintainer. Discount: "just disable," insults, and anything that requires you to download a random fixer.

On medical-looking claims, two extra filters that do not make you a doctor: sample size and outcome. A twelve-person study that measured a blood marker is not "reverses inflammation." A mouse study is not a human protocol. If you cannot name the outcome in the paper's own words, you do not have a finding. Then — still — a professional.

If you want a harder variation, take a claim you already believe and run Search / Compare / Verify as if you were trying to break it. The point is not to become a cynic. The point is to notice that your side of the internet also ranks by engagement.

Mission

Field Mission

In the next 24 hours, run Search / Compare / Verify on one live question you would have "just Googled."

Deliverable: three queries, three sources of different kinds, a filled agree/disagree/disconfirm line, and a next action (or a stop).

If the question is medical, legal, or safety-critical, the next action must include a qualified human. Bring them the sources, not a vibe.
