A Quarter of Deliberate Practice

Decisions, Reflection, and Onward

462 words

Anchored in: Level 7 & 8

The Briefing

The final week of the program is not about adding a new skill. It is about integrating the previous eleven and looking forward.

You will make one consequential decision using the Decision 7. You will run one pre-mortem on a live project. You will do the 10-year audit. And you will decide — deliberately — what practice from this program you will carry into the next quarter.

This is where the program becomes a permanent part of how you work rather than a training series you finished.

This Week'S Practice

• Take one open decision. Run all seven questions in writing. Sleep on it. Decide.

• For one live project: run a full pre-mortem. Write the failure story. Extract the top 5 risks with owners.

• Do the 10-year audit questions: what will I wish I had spent more time on this year, less on, and what specific change will I make in the next 90 days?

• Choose ONE practice from the 12-week program that you will continue permanently. Design the 90-day version of it.

• Write a one-page letter to yourself, describing the person you were at the start of this quarter and the person you are at its end. File it. Read it in one year.

End-Of-Week Reflection

— Which of the 12 weeks changed me most, and why?

— Which practice do I most want to carry forward — and what is the specific way I will keep it going without the structure of a program?

— What is the next 90-day project I choose to give this level of intention to?

Remember This

“The book ends. Your practice does not.”

· Closing The 12-Week Program ·

Twelve weeks. Twelve briefings. Ninety days of deliberate practice.

If you did this program honestly — not perfectly, but honestly — you are a different professional than you were at week one. Not because you know new information. Because you have installed new habits, under real conditions, with real friction, and they have started to compound.

The next quarter is up to you. Some readers will restart the program with different emphases. Some will pick a single week and go deep on it for three months. Some will design their own program using this structure. All three are valid.

What matters is that a year from now you can look back and see that this quarter was not indistinguishable from the one before it. That is the only measure of a practice worth doing.

The Resourceful Human is not who you are. It is who you become — one week, one conversation, one difficult conversation, one small kept promise at a time.

Go do the work.

— End of the 12-Week Program —
