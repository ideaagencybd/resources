Mastery · 5.6

Unblock, Delegate, Ask

Doing it all yourself is not virtue

22 minOwner, Outcome, Deadline3,613 words22 sections
Skill
Move work through other people
Tool
Owner, Outcome, Deadline
Result
You can hand off without abandoning
In one line
Work needs an owner, an outcome, and a clock.
The idea

The Hook

You are the bottleneck, and you are proud of it.

The unfinished things are on your desk because you would not dump them on someone else. You answer the ping because it is faster than explaining. You stay late because the last check "just needs you." People call you reliable. The machine calls you a single point of failure. You will meet that machine on fire in the boss fight. Tonight it still looks like virtue.

Doing it all yourself is not character. It is an unmapped process with one box, and the box is you. Resourcefulness is not personal heroics. Level 3 already taught you to ask who already knows. This lesson is the next move: get work out of your hands without throwing it on the floor.

Unblock. Delegate. Ask. Three different acts, one structure: someone owns it, the outcome is visible, a clock exists. Without those, you did not delegate. You abandoned. Or you did not ask. You hinted, and then resented.

The room

The Scenario

Tuesday, 3:20 p.m. Mara's Top 3 is already dead.

She is still doing the intake triage from 5.4 because the queue has no other owner. Jonah is waiting on a decision only Elena can make. Priya needs a sample file Mara was going to "quickly" clean. A junior, Sam, asked if they could help and Mara said "I've got it" because explaining would take twenty minutes and doing it would take twelve.

Elena pings: "Can you also take a first pass at the Q3 note?"

Mara feels the lean: say yes. Be the person who can.

On her pad, unspoken: she needs Jonah to own the queue for 48 hours, she needs Elena to decide keep/switch by Thursday, and she needs to let Sam take the sample file even if it will be 80%. She also needs to say no to the Q3 first pass, or take it only with something else dropped.

She has a 3:30. Four minutes.

What does she actually send?

Your move

Your Move

Common move. Yes to Elena. "I've got it" to Sam. A vague "can you help with intake?" to Jonah. A hope that Elena will "just know" the decision is blocking everyone. At 7:00 Mara is still the box.

Better move. A long explanation of how busy she is. Sometimes true. Often it asks the other person to manage her load for her. Elena cannot see the machine. She can see a yes.

Expert move. Three crisp handoffs: Jonah owns the queue until Thursday 5:00, outcome = every item has an owner and the oldest three have dates; Elena is asked for a decision by Thursday with two options and a default if silent; Sam owns the sample file with a Definition of Done and a check-in. Q3 note: "I can own that if we drop X, or Sam can draft and I edit Friday. Which?"

Resourceful move. The same, plus the ask without dumping: she does not throw a burning bag. She includes context, the outcome, the deadline, what "good" looks like, and where to go if stuck. She does not disappear. She schedules the check at the level the person actually needs — Grove's task-relevant maturity, not a vibe about "trusting people."

Why the common move fails: unspoken ownership is how work returns at 5:59. Hints are not requests. Heroic yeses are how Top 3s die.

What can go wrong with the expert move: dumping a mess with a deadline and calling it delegation. If Sam cannot see the outcome, you did not delegate. You exported panic.

The idea

The Big Idea

Work moves when three things are named: owner, outcome, deadline.

Not a committee. Not "the team." One owner. An outcome a stranger could check. A clock that is a date, not "soon." Everything else is manners, coaching, or bureaucracy. Manners matter. Bureaucracy is optional. The three are not.

Explained

The Simple Explanation

There are three different stucknesses, and people mix them up.

Unblock is when the work is waiting on a decision, a permission, a file, or a person who does not know they are the constraint. Your job is to make the ask small and decidable. Elena cannot "help with the vendor." She can pick keep, switch, or split by Thursday, given two pages and a default.

Delegate is when someone else should own the outcome. Your job is to transfer the outcome, not the anxiety. That requires a Definition of Done (5.8 will go deeper) and a follow-up matched to their experience with this task.

Ask is when you need help and your pride is the bottleneck. Level 3's Expert Request still applies: specific, bounded, respectful of their time. Asking is not dumping. Dumping is "can you deal with this?" with a smile.

Andy Grove, in High Output Management, argued that how closely you monitor should follow task-relevant maturity — the person's skill and track record with this kind of work — not your general feelings about them, and not a fashion for "empowerment." Low TRM: tighter check-ins, more examples. High TRM: outcome and deadline, then get out of the way. Micromanaging a high-TRM person wastes both of you. Abandoning a low-TRM person is not trust. It is neglect.

RACI — Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed — is a map of who does, who owns, who speaks, who is told. In large, risky work it can prevent a mess. In a six-person team it often becomes bureaucracy theater: a spreadsheet of letters instead of a sentence. The lite version is enough for this course: one owner (accountable), one doer if different (responsible), everyone else is either consulted on purpose or informed on purpose, not accidentally CC'd into a fog.

Drucker, again: the job is output, not activity. If the work can leave your hands and the output still happens, your job was to make that possible, not to be the hero in the box.

The tool

The Practical Tool

Name: Owner, Outcome, Deadline

Use it every time work must move through a human who is not you — or through you, named, so others can stop guessing.

  1. Name the outcome as a checkable sentence. "Every intake item has an owner and the oldest three have customer-visible dates." Not "help with intake."
  2. Name one owner. A person. If two people own it, no one does. If "the team" owns it, it will wait.
  3. Name a deadline that is a date and time. "Thursday 5:00." Add timezone if you must. "EOD" is a fog.
  4. Add the three extras that prevent dumping:
  • Context: why this matters, in two lines.
  • Good: what done looks like, and what 80% looks like if 100% is a stall.
  • Stuck: where they go (you, a doc, a person) without waiting in shame.
  1. Match the check-in to TRM. New to the task: a midpoint. Proven: a ping at the deadline, not a hover. Write the check-in when you delegate, not when you get nervous at 4:45.
  2. If you are asking up (unblock): give options plus a default. "Need keep / switch / split by Thu 5:00. If I don't hear you, I'll proceed on split as the reversible path." You will recognize two-way doors; full treatment in 7.2. Do not hold a reversible decision hostage.
  3. If you are saying no or swapping: do not deliver a mood. Deliver a choice. "I can take Q3 if X drops, or Sam drafts and I edit Friday."

Delegation script (copy, then fill):

I need you to own [outcome] by [date/time].

Why it matters: [two lines].

Done looks like: [checkable]. 80% looks like: [checkable].

Start here: [link, example, first step].

If stuck: [person or rule].

I'll check at [midpoint or deadline]. Until then it's yours.

Ask script (without dumping):

I need [specific help] by [date], about [time estimate].

I've already done [X]. The stuck point is [Y].

If you can't, say no — I can go to [backup].

Fast path

The Fastest Reliable Method

A sticky note or a three-line message:

Owner: Jonah Outcome: Queue visible; oldest three dated Deadline: Thu 17:00

Send it. If you cannot fill the three lines, you are not ready to hand it off. Two more minutes on the outcome will save you a week of "I thought you had it."

The hack

The Hack

Delegate the outcome, not the method — unless the method is the job.

People pad instructions when they are anxious. If Sam's TRM is low, give an example and a midpoint. If Sam's TRM is high, your extra method is noise and a status play. Grove is dry about this: match the management to the task, not to your anxiety.

The other hack: never "help" by silently taking it back. If you take it back, say so, and reset the owner. Silent repossession is how you train people not to own things.

AI move

The AI Move

AI can draft the script, the options for an upward ask, and a first-cut RACI-lite. It cannot tell you who actually has TRM. That is a human fact.

Use AI for:

  • Turning a messy "I need help" into Owner / Outcome / Deadline
  • Drafting two options plus a default for an Elena decision
  • A meeting recap that names owners (meeting notes that do not name owners are souvenirs)

Do not use AI for:

  • Deciding who is "good enough"
  • A fake RACI matrix for a three-person task
  • Softening a no into a paragraph that still means yes

Prompt (copy, then fill):

WHEN TO USE: Work is stuck in my hands or in a fog between people.

Context: [two lines]
The work: [what]
Constraints: [time, people, what I will not dump]
People available: [names/roles, their experience with this task if known]

Goal: Draft
1) Owner / Outcome / Deadline for each piece.
2) A delegation message using the script (context, good, 80%, stuck, check-in).
3) An upward unblock with two options and a default.
4) One thing I should keep because it is actually mine.

Constraints: No corporate fog. No fake RACI. Short enough to paste into chat.
WHAT GOOD OUTPUT LOOKS LIKE: Messages I could send in the next ten minutes.

What to verify: The outcome is checkable. The deadline is real. You are not assigning a person who cannot say no.

Do not paste: performance judgments, salaries, private reasons someone is out.

Tech

The Technology Move

The job is "make ownership visible."

Category: a place where a task has a single name and a date. Current examples: a board column with one assignee; a doc header (Owner / Due); a calendar invite that is the deadline; a shared queue from 5.4. Chat is a bad home for ownership — it scrolls. If the handoff happened in chat, screenshot it into the queue.

When not to use technology: a project tool with fourteen custom fields and no owner. The field that matters is the name.

Mistake

The Common Mistake

Two owners, or zero, and a deadline of "when you get a chance."

It happens because naming one person feels rude, and dates feel like pressure. Fog feels kind. Fog is how the eleven-day request is born.

Recovery: if it is already stuck, send the three lines late. "I'm naming an owner now. Jonah, it's you until Thu 17:00. Outcome: … If I have this wrong, say so by 10:00 tomorrow." Late clarity beats early politeness.

Defense

The Defense

Owner, Outcome, Deadline fails when:

  • The person cannot refuse. Then "delegation" is just load-shifting onto the least powerful. Offer a real no, or you are not resourceful. You are convenient.
  • The outcome is still a project in a trench coat. Slice it (5.1, 5.8).
  • You are in a true emergency and you are the only box. Then say that, time-box the heroics, and map the machine after. Do not make a personality of it.
  • Cross-cultural or cross-company work where "owner" is unclear. Spend the extra sentence. Do not assume your three-letter habits travel.

Limitation: this will not make you less needed overnight. It will stop you pretending that unexplained work is a gift.

Council

The Masters' Table

Andy Grove. Task-relevant maturity, managerial leverage, output as the thing that counts. Delegation is not a moral posture. It is how you raise the team's output instead of your step count.

Peter Drucker. Knowledge workers own outputs. If you cannot name the output, you cannot delegate it. You can only create a busy junior.

James Clear. The system is the owner-plus-date, not the burst of being helpful. Identity: a person who moves work, not a person who holds it.

The RACI tradition. Useful when many roles collide. Dangerous as theater. Steal the distinction between who does and who owns. Leave the spreadsheet until the work is actually multi-team and risky.

Level 3's "Who Already Knows?" Asking is a research skill and an execution skill. The Expert Request plus a deadline is this lesson in a smaller coat.

Synthesis. Name a person, a result, a clock. Match follow-up to skill. Do not confuse holding everything with being good.

Where the council could be wrong. Grove's production language can sound cold in a frightened team. People are not stations. If you only speak in outcomes and never in thanks, you will get compliance and quiet failure. The script includes "why it matters" for a reason. Meaning is not fog. It is fuel.

Best move

The Best-Possible Response

Back to 3:20.

Calm. Mara does not type yes to Elena first. Pause. Top 3 is already dead; she can still stop the bleeding.

Observe. She is the intake bottleneck. Elena is the decision bottleneck. Sam offered capacity. Q3 is a new rock.

Define. Next four minutes: three messages that name owners, outcomes, deadlines; one choice for Elena on Q3.

Verify. Jonah can actually take the queue? She asks, not assumes — a yes that is a real yes.

Prioritize. Unblock Elena's decision (everyone is waiting). Delegate queue and sample file. Do not silently keep Q3.

Find resources / people. Jonah, Sam, Elena. Priya for the quality definition of done.

Options. Hero yes. Busy novel. Or OOD.

Risks. Jonah feels dumped. Sam produces 40% and Mara repossesses in silence. Elena hears "no" as attitude.

Choose. Scripts, short.

Communicate.

To Jonah: the delegation script for the queue, 48 hours, midpoint tomorrow 10:00 because his TRM on this queue is low.

To Sam: sample file, example attached, 80% welcome, check at 11:00 tomorrow.

To Elena: "Need keep/switch/split by Thu 17:00. Two-pager from Jonah is in the doc. Default if silent: split (reversible). On Q3: I can own a first pass if intake stays with Jonah through Thursday, or Sam drafts and I edit Friday. Which do you want?"

Act. She does not clean the sample file "quickly." She lets Sam start (5.2, for Sam).

Document. The three lines live on the queue card, not only in chat.

Adapt. If Jonah says no, she still cannot be the only box. She goes back to Elena with a capacity fact, not a martyr story.

Reflect. Where did "I've got it" try to come out?

Practice

Practice

Do this now. Ten minutes.

  1. List three things currently stuck. Mark each: unblock / delegate / ask / actually mine.
  2. Rewrite one of them as Owner, Outcome, Deadline.
  3. Write the full delegation or ask script. Include 80% and "if stuck."
  4. Write the check-in time that matches their TRM, not your anxiety.
  5. If the stuck thing is you needing to say no, write Elena the choice sentence.
  6. Send one of these in the next 24 hours, or it was journaling.
Real life

Real-Life Challenge

Work — the silent bottleneck. Situation: people are waiting on you, or on a boss, and nobody has named it. What most people do: work faster, hint harder. Why it fails: speed without a decision is a bigger pile. Resourceful response: OOD plus options and a default. Words to use: "Owner is X. Outcome is Y. Deadline is Z." "If I don't hear you, I'll do D." Words to avoid: "Whenever you get a chance," "looping you in," "we should maybe." Tool: Owner, Outcome, Deadline. AI assist: draft the message; you check the tone and the facts. What could go wrong: a default that is a one-way door. Flag irreversible choices; do not auto-proceed those (7.2).

Family — chores and mental load. Situation: one person is the household bottleneck and calls it caring. What most people do: explode, or a sarcastic "never mind, I'll do it." Why it fails: repossession trains the old machine. Resourceful response: one recurring outcome, one owner, one weekly check. A checklist (5.3) for the how. Words to use: "Can you own groceries by Sunday 4:00, list on the fridge, budget X?" Words to avoid: "Help more." Help is not an outcome. Tool: OOD, gently, not as a performance review of your partner. What could go wrong: using the script as a weapon. If the load is unfair, say the unfairness. Then pick an owner.

Ask — the expert. Situation: you need twenty minutes from a busy person. What most people do: a long context dump, or silence until it is late. Why it fails: they cannot see the ask. Resourceful response: Expert Request + deadline + time estimate + permission to say no. Words to use: the ask script above. Words to avoid: "Do you have a minute?" (they do not). Tool: Ask without dumping. What could go wrong: asking the same overworked person every time. Rotate. Map the bottleneck (5.4).

Bad room

Bad-Room Version

Same Tuesday. Worse.

Elena's ping is not a request. "Need you on Q3, the Jonah thing, and client call at 4. Don't drop any of it." Sam is new and scared. Jonah is blunt in a thread: "I'm not your backup." The queue is already slipping.

Pause. You will not win a character fight in Slack.

BLUF to Elena: "I can do two of those three at the quality we need. Q3 draft or client call or intake. If I keep all three, intake becomes another eleven-day letter. Recommendation: I take the client call, Jonah owns intake through Thursday (I'll send OOD), Sam drafts Q3 for my Friday edit. Tell me to change the split."

To Jonah, privately, not in the thread: "Not asking you to be my backup as a personality. Asking you to own a defined queue for 48 hours. If you can't, I need that as a fact so I can take it to Elena. Outcome and deadline here: …"

You have not dumped. You have not swallowed. You have made the machine visible and asked for a decision. That is manage-up adjacent. Next lesson is the rest of that muscle.

If your default is still "I've got it," write those two words on a card and cross them out. Replace with the three lines.

Reflect

Reflection

  • What are you currently holding that is actually an unnamed wait on someone else?
  • Who offered help that you refused because explaining felt expensive?
  • Where do you use two owners to avoid the rudeness of one?
  • Does your follow-up match the person's skill, or your anxiety?
  • When you ask, do you give people a real no?
Remember

Remember This

If it has no owner, no outcome, and no clock, it is not delegated. It is wandering.

Deep dive

Optional Deep Dive

Leverage, TRM, and the myth of the helpful hero.

Grove's math is uncomfortable: a manager's output is the output of the team under their influence. Every hour you spend doing a task someone else could own at 80% is an hour you did not spend on a bottleneck only you can move — a decision, a customer, a map, a hard conversation. The hero feels moral. The system gets slower.

Task-relevant maturity is specific. Jonah can be high-TRM on code and low-TRM on a customer queue. Delegating "because he is senior" is how you get a blunt thread and a still-invisible pile. Check the task, not the halo.

RACI-lite is enough until it is not. When legal, safety, or money requires a second pair of eyes, name Consulted and Informed on purpose. The failure is not the letters. The failure is a twelve-person "consulted" list that is actually a fear of leaving someone out. If everyone is consulted, you have a meeting, not a process. Map it (5.4) and put a checklist on the risky step (5.3).

On asking: many capable people would rather burn than be a burden. That is identity, not logistics. The Evidence Board (1.8) can include evidence that your asks are bounded and that people said no and survived. If you cannot tolerate a no, your asks are still control.

Harder variation: for one week, refuse to touch a task that does not have OOD written at the top. You will hate this. You will also discover how much of your day was unnamed wandering. Then finish (5.8) so the new owners do not live in "almost done" with you.

Mission

Field Mission

In the next 24–48 hours, move one real piece of work with Owner, Outcome, Deadline. Delegate or ask — not a hypothetical.

Afterward:

  1. The three lines you sent
  2. Whether the other person could repeat the outcome
  3. Whether you hovered, disappeared, or checked as promised
  4. What you will change on the next handoff

If they said no, that is a successful ask. You still have a fact. Take the fact to the map or to Elena.
