Selected Sources & Further Reading

Selected Sources & Further Reading

3,729 words 1 entries From book

The books, papers, and thinkers that made this book possible — arranged by Level.

This list is curated for usefulness, not completeness. Each entry is a real book, paper, or body of work whose ideas materially shaped the corresponding chapter. In most cases the ideas have been synthesized rather than quoted — where a specific attribution matters, the in-text mention is your citation.

For rapidly evolving fields (AI, security, tools), treat any specific book here as a foundation, not the current state of the art. Cross-check with recent sources.

Level 1 — Get Yourself Together

• Viktor Frankl, Man's Search for Meaning (1946) — the foundational text on choosing your response.

• Marcus Aurelius, Meditations (c. 170–180 CE) — the original personal-operating-system manual.

• Lisa Feldman Barrett, How Emotions Are Made (2017) — on constructed emotion and emotional granularity.

• Matthew Walker, Why We Sleep (2017) — read with appropriate skepticism about specific claims; the core message on sleep is sound.

• James Clear, Atomic Habits (2018) — the practical framework used in Lesson 1.5.

• BJ Fogg, Tiny Habits (2019) — the tiny-habit design framework and habit-stacking.

• Cal Newport, Deep Work (2016) and Slow Productivity (2024) — on attention as the fundamental resource.

• Tim Pychyl, Solving the Procrastination Puzzle (2013) — on procrastination as emotion regulation.

Level 2 — Think Like a Sharp Person

• Daniel Kahneman, Thinking, Fast and Slow (2011) — the field guide to System 1 and System 2.

• Amos Tversky & Daniel Kahneman's original papers on heuristics and biases (Science, 1974).

• Charlie Munger, Poor Charlie's Almanack — the '25 human misjudgments' lecture is essential.

• Philip Tetlock, Superforecasting (2015) — on calibrated confidence and updating.

• Nassim Nicholas Taleb, Fooled by Randomness (2001) and The Black Swan (2007).

• Richard Feynman, Surely You're Joking, Mr. Feynman (1985) — as a masterclass in question-asking.

• Warren Berger, A More Beautiful Question (2014) — on the discipline of the inquiry.

Level 3 — Become Resourceful

• K. Anders Ericsson, Peak (2016) — the actual research on deliberate practice (as opposed to the popular myth).

• Josh Waitzkin, The Art of Learning (2007) — expertise development from a multi-domain champion.

• Barbara Oakley, A Mind for Numbers (2014) — practical learning-to-learn techniques.

• Angela Duckworth, Grit (2016) — sustained interest and effort as trainable capacities.

• Herbert Simon on bounded rationality and satisficing — his Nobel lecture is short and worth reading.

• Marie Curie, Autobiographical Notes (1923) — a model of evidence-first reasoning under adversity.

Level 4 — Become Good with People

• Stone, Patton, Heen, Difficult Conversations (1999).

• Douglas Stone & Sheila Heen, Thanks for the Feedback (2014).

• Chris Voss, Never Split the Difference (2016) — tactical empathy from an FBI negotiator.

• Roger Fisher & William Ury, Getting to Yes (1981) — the principled-negotiation foundation.

• Kim Scott, Radical Candor (2017).

• Dale Carnegie, How to Win Friends and Influence People (1936) — enduring principles despite the dated packaging.

• Amy Edmondson, The Fearless Organization (2018) — on psychological safety.

• Erin Meyer, The Culture Map (2014) — on cross-cultural communication.

• Robert Cialdini, Influence (1984) — the six levers.

Level 5 — Get Things Done

• Atul Gawande, The Checklist Manifesto (2009).

• Andy Grove, High Output Management (1983) — still the best management book ever written.

• Peter Drucker, The Effective Executive (1966).

• David Marquet, Turn the Ship Around! (2013) — on 'I intend to' delegation.

• Greg McKeown, Essentialism (2014).

• Cal Newport, Slow Productivity (2024).

• W. Edwards Deming, Out of the Crisis (1982) — the foundation of quality management.

• Gary Klein on the pre-mortem — see his work with Kahneman in 'Conditions for Intuitive Expertise' (2009).

Level 6 — Master the Digital World

• For AI (rapidly evolving — check publication date): Ethan Mollick, Co-Intelligence (2024). Stuart Russell, Human Compatible (2019). Vendor documentation from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google — always the most current source.

• For spreadsheets: the vendor documentation is the single best source (Excel's help, Google Sheets' help). For deeper: Michael Alexander & Richard Kusleika, Excel Bible.

• For data literacy: Darrell Huff, How to Lie with Statistics (1954). Edward Tufte, The Visual Display of Quantitative Information (1983). Hans Rosling, Factfulness (2018). Nate Silver, The Signal and the Noise (2012).

• For security: Bruce Schneier's blog (Schneier on Security) is the canonical ongoing source. Kevin Mitnick, The Art of Deception (2002).

Level 7 — Make Better Decisions

• Annie Duke, Thinking in Bets (2018) and How to Decide (2020).

• Philip Tetlock & Dan Gardner, Superforecasting (2015).

• Michael Mauboussin, The Success Equation (2012) — on separating luck from skill.

• Gary Klein, Sources of Power (1998) — naturalistic decision-making.

• Chip & Dan Heath, Decisive (2013) — practical decision-making frameworks.

• Nassim Taleb, Antifragile (2012).

• Herbert Simon, Administrative Behavior (1947).

Level 8 — Become Wise

• Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics — the foundational text on virtue and practical wisdom.

• Marcus Aurelius, Meditations.

• Epictetus, Discourses and Enchiridion.

• Seneca, Letters from a Stoic and On the Shortness of Life.

• Viktor Frankl, Man's Search for Meaning.

• Confucius, The Analects.

• Martin Seligman, Flourish (2011) — on PERMA.

• Oliver Burkeman, Four Thousand Weeks (2021) — on finitude and meaning.

• James Hollis, Living an Examined Life (2018).

• Nelson Mandela, Long Walk to Freedom (1994).

CROSS-CUTTING (referenced throughout)

• The Bible, the Analects, the Bhagavad Gita, the Quran, the Meditations, the Tao Te Ching — worth encountering, in translation, as sources of durable practical wisdom.

• Adam Smith, The Theory of Moral Sentiments (1759) — often skipped in favor of The Wealth of Nations, but arguably his greater work.

• For rapid updates on AI, security, and technology: primary vendor documentation, government agency publications (NIST, FTC, MHRA, ENISA, and country equivalents), and peer-reviewed sources. Popular reporting is a starting point, not a source.
