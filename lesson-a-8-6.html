Mastery · 8.6

Think in Decades

Most of the compounding is invisible this week

22 minThe 10-Year Test3,665 words22 sections
Skill
Choose the long game without becoming a monk
Tool
The 10-Year Test
Result
You can take a short-term loss for a long-term self
In one line
This week is a liar. Decades keep the books.
The idea

The Hook

This week is loud. Ten years from now is quiet.

The quiet one is where the books are kept.

A reputation is not made in a meeting. It is made in a hundred meetings that did not feel like a brand exercise. A body is not ruined on a dramatic Tuesday. It is ruined on ordinary Tuesdays that lost to the quarter. A relationship is not saved by a speech. It is saved by a pattern that was slightly costly at the time and invisible in the recap.

Compounding is usually taught as money. Money is the clean example. The rest of a life compounds too — trust, skill, health, the story people tell about whether you can be in a hard room. The reverse compounds as well. Small fog. Skipped sleep. A clever scalp in a thread. You will not see the interest daily. You will meet the principal later and call it luck or fate.

This lesson is the long game without the monk costume. You still live in weeks. You still might be wrong about the future. So you leave slack.

The room

The Scenario

Thursday, 2:15 p.m. Elena has a quarter problem.

"If we pull the launch forward four weeks, we make the number. Engineering says it's ugly but possible. Legal has not finished. You'd have to be the face of it."

Jonah: "Ship. We can patch. Winners ship."

Mara, quieter: "The last time we did this, support ate it for a year, and one customer still tells the story. That's a decade of a story."

Elena looks at you. "I need someone who can think past the bonus. I also need the bonus."

You can feel both clocks. One is this Friday. One is a person, ten years from now, googling whether to trust you.

What do you recommend?

This is a scenario, not a true story.

Your move

Your Move

Say the recommendation you would actually own.

Common move. Ship. The quarter is real. Ten years is a fog. You will be somewhere else by then, you tell yourself, which is how people become the story Mara mentioned.

Better move. Ship with a written list of patches and a war room. Intelligence. It can still spend reputation you will need when you are not in the mood to be impressive.

Expert move. Run the 10-Year Test on reputation, health, relationships, and the actual product. Take a short-term loss if the long book is ugly. If the long book is fine — a two-way door, a reversible mess — ship and stop pretending it is a moral crisis.

Resourceful move. Choose the long game and leave slack, because you might be wrong about the decade. Do not bet the company on a future you cannot see. Do not bet your name on a week.

Why the common move fails: this week is a liar. It overweights what can be counted by Friday. Buffett and Munger, as long-game lenses not as saints, spent careers on the idea that the market's tempo is not the same as value's tempo. You do not need their money to steal the tempo.

What can go wrong with the expert move: you can become the person who never ships, always "thinking in decades," while the shop dies this year. A long game that cannot survive the year is a speech. Leave slack. Also: you might be wrong about what the decade will reward. Humility is part of the test.

The idea

The Big Idea

The 10-Year Test. Before a costly yes, ask who you are, what is broken, and what has compounded if this choice becomes a pattern.

Most of the compounding is invisible this week. That is why you need a tool. Willpower will side with the quarter. The quarter has a meeting. The decade does not.

Explained

The Simple Explanation

Four books compound whether you watch them or not.

Reputation. What do people who are not your friends say when you are not in the room? It moves slowly, then all at once. One fogged forecast may not kill you. A pattern of fog becomes a name. Names are expensive to rename.

Skill. Ninety-minute sprints (Level 3) and practice with feedback compound. Consumption does not. Ten years of tutorials is a museum. Ten years of shipped, reviewed work is a craft.

Health. The body keeps a quieter ledger than Elena. Sleep, substances, the stress cycle you close or don't (Level 1). There is no need for a fake statistic. You have met enough forty-five-year-olds to know which patterns were free and which were loans.

Relationships. The people you will want at the table in a decade are not maintained by leftover minutes. They are maintained by costs paid when the quarter was also real.

Money compounds too. That part is well taught. If you want the investor lens: Warren Buffett's public writing and Charlie Munger's talks return to patience, not losing, and staying inside a circle of competence. Poor Charlie's Almanack (2005, later editions) is a readable pile of that lens. Steal the tempo. Do not steal a personality. They might be wrong about the future — they have been — which is why they talk about margin of safety. Your version of margin of safety is slack: time, savings, not burning the team, not making a one-way door out of a guess.

The marshmallow test is popular and easy to misuse. Walter Mischel's work on delay of gratification is real; later commentary has argued about what it measured (home environment, reliability of the adult, class) and how cleanly it predicts a life. Do not build a moral theory on a cookie. Build a habit of asking what a pattern costs in a decade.

You will use Second-Order Effects (Level 7) here at a longer horizon. And Then What, asked not three times but across a calendar you will actually live.

A warning: thinking in decades can become a way to ignore the person in front of you. The long game includes them. If your decade requires everyone else to be a stepping stone, you have misunderstood compounding. You have understood extraction.

The tool

The Practical Tool

Name: The 10-Year Test

Use it on choices that could become patterns: launches, job moves, how you treat a junior, a health bargain, a relationship you are starving, a lie that would be convenient.

Write five lines:

  1. If this becomes how I do things, who am I in 10 years? A person, not a title. One sentence.
  2. What has quietly compounded? Reputation, body, skill, the marriage, the team, the customers who tell stories.
  3. What is the short-term loss if I refuse the week? Name it without drama: money, status, Elena's mood, a bonus.
  4. What slack am I leaving because I might be wrong? Time, a reversible design, a kill switch, savings, a relationship you do not treat as guaranteed.
  5. What would Munger invert? "How does this clever week become a stupid decade?" One paragraph.

Then:

  1. Choose a pattern, not a heroics. If you ship ugly this once, write the rule that prevents it from becoming a method.

On a card:

PATTERN-ME IN 10 YEARS → WHAT COMPOUNDED → COST OF REFUSING THE WEEK → SLACK → INVERT.

Fast path

The Fastest Reliable Method

Ninety seconds. Elena wants a face for the launch.

Ask: If we did this every quarter, what story would customers tell in ten years?

If the story is ugly, you need a short-term loss or a much smaller ship. If the story is "they were messy once and then they were straight," you may have a two-way door. Name the once.

Holding sentence:

"I can make this quarter. I need to know whether we're choosing a method. If this is a method, I won't be the face. If this is once, with a kill switch and a true customer note, I'll consider it."

That sentence thinks in decades without a sermon.

The hack

The Hack

Take a small, visible short-term loss on purpose this month.

Not a grand sacrifice. A night of sleep instead of a heroic send. A delayed launch of a week you can afford. A conversation you have when it costs awkwardness instead of a later explosion. You are training the nervous system that loss-this-week is survivable. People who never practice that will always side with the liar.

Write it in the decision journal: loss taken, pattern protected.

AI move

The AI Move

AI is good at painting a decade and also at hallucinating one. Use it to invert, not to prophesy.

Use AI for:

  • A premortem at 10 years: reputation, product, body, team
  • Inversion: how this week becomes a stupid decade
  • Drafting the "once, not a method" rule

Do not use AI for:

  • A confident future ("in 2036 the market will…")
  • Permission to ignore legal, safety, or health
  • A monk speech you will not live

WHEN TO USE: A quarter is shouting and you need the quiet ledger in language.

WHAT TO PROVIDE: The choice, the pattern risk, what you might be wrong about.

WHAT GOOD OUTPUT LOOKS LIKE: Five lines of the test, a named short-term loss, slack, no prophecy.

Prompt (copy, then fill):

Context: I need a 10-Year Test, not a forecast.
Choice:
"""
[paragraph]
"""
If we repeat this, the pattern would be:
[one line]
What this week is shouting:
[number, bonus, fear]
What I might be wrong about:
[market, people, my stamina]

Do this:
1) Run: pattern-me in 10 years; what compounded; cost of refusing the week; slack; invert.
2) Separate one-time mess from method.
3) Offer two options that both leave slack.
4) List ways the long game could be wrong (I become rigid, the shop dies this year, the decade rewards something else).

Constraints: No fake numbers. No guru future. No "legacy" language.
Output: Short labeled sections.
Verification: Facts I should check this week (legal, safety, actual customer harm).

Do not paste: nonpublic financials you do not have rights to share, health records, other people's data.

Tech

The Technology Move

The job is "make the slow ledger as visible as the dashboard."

Category: long-horizon records, not more charts for the quarter.

Current examples:

  • A decision journal with a 10-year review date
  • Photos / simple health logs you actually look at twice a year, not a shrine of metrics
  • A reputation file: compliments and complaints you did not like, dated
  • Calendar: recurring time with the people who compound, treated like a board meeting

When not to use technology: a "life OS" so complex it becomes this week's project. The decade does not care about your app. It cares about the pattern.

Mistake

The Common Mistake

People think in decades only about money, and in days about everything else.

They compound the brokerage account and depreciate the body and the marriage with a clear conscience, because those do not have a login. Recovery: pick one non-money book this month — sleep, a person, a craft — and pay a short-term cost to it on a dated schedule.

The other mistake: using "the long game" to dodge a necessary loss this week that is actually a character test (8.2). Sometimes the decade is built by the expensive honest move today. Sometimes it is built by shipping. The test is how you tell. If your long game always looks like comfort, it is not a long game. It is a story.

Defense

The Defense

The 10-Year Test fails when:

  • The building is on fire. Put it out. Then ask whether your fire-making is a method.
  • You cannot predict the decade (you cannot). Then slack is the tool, not a detailed prophecy. Two-way doors, savings, reversible architecture, not being a jerk on the way through.
  • You use the decade to control other people: "you'll thank me in ten years." That is a parent speech. Do not.
  • You are young in a field that requires reps now. Thinking in decades includes a decade of practice, which looks like a lot of weeks. Do not hide from the 90-minute sprint inside a rocking chair of vision.

Limitation: this will not tell you the future. It will tell you which pattern you are voting for. That is the only part of the future you actually touch.

Council

The Masters' Table

Munger. Invert, circle of competence, patience. Application: "How does shipping ugly become a name we cannot wash?" Also: stay inside what you can actually know.

Buffett (lens, not guru). Time as the friend of a good pattern and the enemy of a bad one. Application: a slightly slower launch can be a cheaper decade. He can be wrong; so can you; slack.

Mandela. A very long clock, paid personally. Application: some games only exist if you do not take every opening (8.4) and do not sell the end for a week. Do not analogize your launch to a liberation struggle. Steal the clock, not the halo.

Lincoln. Decisions that had to survive a war, not a news cycle. Application: write the angry week, send the policy that the decade can live with.

Aristotle. A life as a whole, not a string of clever episodes. Application: eudaimonia is not a mood. It is an activity over a span. Your quarter is an episode. Name the activity.

Curie. A long inquiry. Application: some work only exists if you do not rush the result for a patron. Integrity of inquiry compounds; so does its opposite.

Synthesis. The council agrees that tempo is a moral choice, and that the week will always have a better speech.

Where the council could be wrong. Investor patience can ignore people who do not have a decade of runway. Mandela's clock is not your product calendar. Aristotle's "life as a whole" has been used to shame people whose lives were interrupted by luck, illness, or injustice. Leave slack for being wrong, and for being mortal.

Best move

The Best-Possible Response

Back to 2:15 p.m.

Calm. You do not match Jonah's "winners ship."

Observe. Quarter number vs legal unfinished vs a customer story that already exists from last time. Elena wants both clocks.

Define. The job is to choose a pattern, not to perform seriousness.

Verify. What is actually unfinished — legal, safety, a known defect? Door type: if this ships, how reversible is the harm?

Prioritize. Harm that compounds (safety, a lie, a name) over a bonus that does not.

Find resources / people. Legal, support lead, one customer who lived through last time. Not a vibe from Jonah.

Options. Full early ship. Delay. Smaller honest launch with a true limitation in the note. Ship with your name off it (this last one is a character smell).

Risks. Delay costs the number and Elena's weather. Ship costs a story that may outlive everyone's tenure. You might be wrong: maybe customers do not care. Slack: a limitation, a kill switch, a date to pull it.

Choose. Not the full ugly ship as a method. A smaller true version if one exists; otherwise a short-term loss on the date.

Communicate. "If this becomes how we launch, in ten years we are the people who ship ahead of legal and let support eat it. I won't be the face of that method. If we can launch a limited version that is true, with a customer note that names the limit, I'll own that. If the number requires the ugly full ship, that's a short-term loss I recommend we take. I'll help make the quarter another way for the next two weeks. I won't help make a decade problem for a Friday."

Act. Put the recommendation in writing. Help the other way to make the number so you are not only the person of no.

Document. The 10-Year Test, five lines, in the decision journal, review date +1 year and +10.

Adapt. If legal comes back clean tonight, update. Do not cling to the no as a personality.

Reflect. Which clock had more allies in the room? That is why the tool exists.

Practice

Practice

Ten minutes.

  1. Pick a live pattern — how you ship, sleep, speak to juniors, check a phone in a conversation.
  2. Write the five lines of the 10-Year Test.
  3. Name a short-term loss you are willing to take this month to protect the pattern. Make it smaller than a movie and larger than a slogan.
  4. Invert: how your favorite virtue becomes a stupid decade (caution becomes freeze, speed becomes a name, service becomes absence).
  5. Find the error: "I'll fix it when things calm down." Things will not. Patterns do not wait for calm.
Real life

Real-Life Challenge

Work — the ugly pull-forward. Situation: a date that makes the number and spends the name. What most people do: ship and write a retro that nobody reads. Why it fails: the retro is a week tool. The story is a decade tool. Resourceful response: 10-Year Test, once-not-method, or a short-term loss. Words to use: "I won't be the face of a method. I will own a limited true version." Words to avoid: "Legacy," "at the end of the day," "winners ship." Tool: 10-Year Test. Premortem. Door Type. AI assist: invert the week. What could go wrong: you become the shop's brake and never a motor. Offer another way to make the number.

Health — the ordinary Tuesday. Situation: sleep vs one more hour of being the person who figures it out. What most people do: one more hour. It is only today. Why it fails: today is a pattern in costume. Resourceful response: close the cycle, take the short-term loss of being finite. Words to use: "I'm done for today. I'll be more use at 8 than at 1 a.m." Words to avoid: "I'll sleep when this is over." Tool: 10-Year Test + State Check + Recovery Protocol. What could go wrong: using health as a way to never be available. The line is a pattern, not a disappearing act.

Relationships — leftover minutes. Situation: the people you want in ten years get the dregs of the person who was impressive from 9 to 7. What most people do: a nice weekend planned in theory. Why it fails: compounding needs reps, not intentions. Resourceful response: a dated cost — time that cannot be preyed on by Elena's ping, as in 8.3. Words to use: a when, on a calendar, with a name. Words to avoid: "You know you're the most important thing" as a substitute for a Thursday. Tool: 10-Year Test. Four Questions. What could go wrong: performing family as a new status. They can tell.

Bad room

Bad-Room Version

The launch is already announced. Elena told the board. Jonah is briefing sales. Legal is in the hallway saying "we are not done" with the look of a person who will be blamed later.

You are asked, in the Room, "You're good with this?"

Pause. The 10-Year Test in one breath.

"The announcement happened. I can't un-announce it. I can refuse to say we are safer or more finished than we are. I will not brief sales with a sentence I wouldn't want quoted in ten years. If we go, we go with a true limitation, a kill switch, and my name only on sentences I can defend. If that kills the number, we should have run this test before the board. I'll still help. I won't decorate."

You have not thrown Elena to the board in public. You have not become Jonah. You have taken a short-term status loss to keep a name. The decade notices names.

If they remove you from the briefing, that is a cost. Write it down. It is data about the shop, and about you.

Reflect

Reflection

  • Which book are you compounding without watching — reputation, body, skill, a relationship, a fog?
  • What short-term loss are you unwilling to take, and what does that protect?
  • Where do you use "the long game" as a story that always lets you win this week too?
  • What might you be wrong about in ten years?
  • Who already tells a story about you that you would not want compounded?
Remember

Remember This

This week will lie to you in a loud voice. Write the pattern, pay a small loss, leave slack for being wrong.

Deep dive

Optional Deep Dive

Buffett and Munger are useful because they made tempo a discipline, not a mood. They are also a culture industry: quotations, conferences, a style of sounding wise by being slow. Use the primary writing — Buffett's letters to Berkshire shareholders are public — and Munger's talks as collected. Do not become a person who cites them instead of doing the 10-Year Test on your own sleep.

On delay of gratification: Mischel, Shoda, and Rodriguez's work (including the 1989 Science paper on delay in children) started a long popular afterlife. Later analyses have argued the simple story (wait for two marshmallows, get a better life) ignores reliability of the environment and socioeconomic confounds. For this course: being able to wait is sometimes a skill and sometimes a luxury. The 10-Year Test is not a moral ranking of people who cannot wait because the week is genuinely on fire. It is a tool for people who can wait and still don't, because the quarter has better catering.

A harder variation: write a letter from ten-years-you that is not inspirational. Make it specific and a little cold. What did you spend? What compounded? Where were you wrong? Put a calendar reminder for one year, not ten. You will not wait ten years to learn if you never check.

Mission

Field Mission

In the next 24–48 hours, take one short-term loss for a long-term pattern. Sleep, a delay, a true sentence that costs a little status, time with a person, a no to a clever week.

Write five lines — the actual 10-Year Test — and the loss you paid.

If you cannot pay a loss, you have learned that the week still owns you. Try a smaller coin.

Sources

Sources to Explore

  • Charlie Munger, Poor Charlie's Almanack, 2005 (later eds.) — inversion, patience, circle of competence.
  • Warren E. Buffett, Berkshire Hathaway annual letters (public) — tempo and not losing, in ordinary language.
  • Walter Mischel, Yuichi Shoda, and Monica I. Rodriguez, "Delay of gratification in children," Science, 1989 — plus later critiques; do not overclaim.
  • Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics, Book I — a life as a whole, not a string of episodes.
  • Nelson Mandela, Long Walk to Freedom, 1994 — a long clock, used carefully, not as a product metaphor.
