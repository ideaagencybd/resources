Someone is posting lies about you online

medium The Minimum Viable Responsereputationdefamationreviewtakedown
1

What is happening

A review, a post, a thread, or a fake profile. It names you or your business and it is untrue. It is also, unhelpfully, near the top of the search results for your name.

2

What is actually going on

  • Search results care about engagement. Arguing in the replies is the most reliable way to make the post rank higher and live longer.
  • The audience that matters is small: customers, employers, clients. They need one calm, findable answer, not a war.
  • Platform policy is usually a faster lever than law. Impersonation, harassment, and fake reviews are removed under terms of service far quicker than through a court.
  • Displacement often outperforms deletion. A page you control that ranks above the lie solves the practical problem even if the lie stays up.
3

Do this, in this order

  1. Screenshot everything with dates and URLsBefore any report. Content disappears and reappears, and you will need the record.
  2. Report under the specific policy it breachesName the rule: impersonation, harassment, false information, fake review. Generic reports get generic outcomes.
  3. Post one calm public correction, once, where it livesFactual, brief, no insults, no sarcasm. Written for the reasonable third party reading later, not for the person attacking you.
  4. Then stop engaging entirelyOne response, no thread. Every reply after the first is a gift to their reach.
  5. Build what ranks above itYour own site, a professional profile, a proper listing. Search results are displaced far more often than they are erased.
  6. Tell the people who matter directlyA short private note to key clients or your manager removes the ambush effect completely.
4

Words you can borrow

The single public correction
"This is not accurate. We have no record of this order and we have asked the platform to review it. Anyone with questions is welcome to contact us directly."
To a client
"You may see a review claiming X. It is not accurate, here is the actual record, and I wanted you to hear it from me."
In the platform report
"This post contains a false factual claim about an identifiable person and breaches your policy on X. Evidence attached."
To the poster, if anything
"I have corrected the record publicly. I am not going to discuss it further."
5

What makes it worse

  • A long point-by-point rebuttal. Length reads as guilt and boosts the post.
  • Buying fake positive reviews to bury it. Detection is routine and the penalty is worse than the original problem.
  • Legal threats as an opening move. They are screenshotted and they generate sympathy for them.
  • Doxxing or exposing them in return.
  • Refreshing it hourly. It damages your judgement and changes nothing.
6

Once the pressure drops

  • Set a search alert for your name and your business so you find things early.
  • Ask satisfied clients for genuine reviews steadily over time. Volume is the real defence.
  • Keep records for anything that might become a legal matter later — dates, harm caused, revenue effect.
  • Accept some residue. Total cleanliness is not achievable; dominance of the accurate story is.
Where this stops being yours to handle

If it includes your home address, threats, sexual content, or content about your children, stop treating it as reputation management. Report it as harassment to the police with your evidence and get a reference number.

A note on this library

These are general principles written to be useful under pressure, not legal, medical, or financial advice. Laws and emergency procedures differ by country. If life is at risk, contact your emergency services first and read this afterwards.
