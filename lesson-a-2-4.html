The Bias Field Guide

Your brain is a brilliant liar with good intentions

28 minBias Check of Three946 words22 sections
Skill
Catch the usual distortions in the wild
Tool
Bias Check of Three
Result
You can name the trap you are in
In one line
You don't need fifty biases. You need three questions that catch most of them.
The idea

The Hook

There is a cottage industry of bias posters. Forty-two colorful hexagons. People share them, feel scientific, and then walk into a meeting and do every one.

You do not need a zoo. You need a field guide: a few animals you will actually recognize in the wild, and a check that works when you are the animal.

Kahneman and Tversky's research program — decades of work on how people judge under uncertainty — is the well. Thinking, Fast and Slow (2011) is the popular map, with the usual popular-map distortions. Later psychologists have argued about replication, about how sharp the "two systems" metaphor is, and about when a "bias" is just a shortcut that usually works. Good. This course will not pretend the science is a finished cathedral.

The operational claim is modest: under pressure you will over-weight what is vivid, what confirms you, what you have already spent, and what makes your team look coherent. You can catch a lot of that with three questions.

The room

The Scenario

The team has been building a feature for four months. The demo is next week. Usage data from a cousin feature is poor. Jonah says, "We've come too far to stop." Elena says, "Leadership already told the board." A designer says, "I saw a tweet that a competitor's version is crushing it." Nobody has a base rate for features like this in your own product.

What trap are you in, and which question would you ask out loud?

Your move

Your Move

Common: Debate the tweet. Better: "Let's look at the data." Still vague. Expert: Bias Check of Three, spoken as ordinary language, not as a dunk. Resourceful: Expert, plus a reversible test instead of a religious ship/kill.

The idea

The Big Idea

A bias named as an insult changes nobody. A bias caught as a question sometimes does.

Explained

The Simple Explanation

Bias Check of Three

  1. What would change my mind? If the answer is "nothing," you are not reasoning. You are defending.
  2. What am I protecting? Status, sunk time, a relationship, a story about being the kind of person who finishes.
  3. What's the base rate? How often do things like this work, in a boring sample, not in a vivid anecdote?

Five animals worth recognizing:

  • Confirmation. You hunt evidence that you were already right. Disconfirm on purpose.
  • Availability. The vivid story (tweet, outage, that one customer) crowds out the quiet base rate.
  • Sunk cost. Four months is gone either way. The only question is the next month.
  • Fundamental attribution. They failed because they are careless; I failed because of the system. Invert it once.
  • Groupthink / agreement trap. Everyone nods. Ask who disagrees, and make it safe.

Do not turn this into a personality diagnosis of other people. The only bias you can reliably work on in the next five minutes is yours.

On replication: some famous effects are smaller or shakier than the TED version. Ego depletion is mixed. Social priming took hits. Loss aversion and many probability-judgment effects remain useful if you do not treat them as magic. When evidence is mixed, say so, and keep the question anyway if the question is cheap and wise.

The tool

The Practical Tool

Print the three questions. Before a decision, write one line each. If you cannot answer (3), you are deciding on a story.

Fast path

The Fastest Reliable Method

Ask only: What would change my mind? If you smirk, you found the trap.

The hack

The Hack

Appoint a rotating dissenter with a time-box, not a personality. "Your job for twelve minutes is to try to kill this." Then they go back to being a colleague. Permanent devil's advocates become ignored mascots.

AI move

The AI Move

Context: My current belief: [belief]
Evidence I like: [list]
Goal: Answer the Bias Check of Three for me. Then give the strongest case I am wrong. Do not flatter.
Verification: What you invented.
Tech

The Technology Move

A dashboard with base rates beats a thread with anecdotes. Pre-register a kill criterion in the ticket. Not a bias-app.

Mistake

The Common Mistake

Calling other people biased as a status move. They will bring you a poster of your own.

Defense

The Defense

Sometimes you should be stubborn — values, safety, a promise. The check still helps: you can say "this is a value, not a forecast."

Council

The Masters' Table

Kahneman/Tversky. Substitution of easy questions for hard ones. Tetlock. Foxes update; hedgehogs defend a line. Janis. Groupthink as a popular model with limits — not every agreement is a pathology. Munger. Invert. Where wrong. Over-correction into indecision. After the check, choose.

Best move

The Best-Possible Response

You say: "Four months is gone. I'm protecting not looking wasteful. Base rate of cousin features is poor. What would change my mind is a two-week test with a number we agree now." The room is less cinematic. The next month might be saved.

Practice

Practice

Take a live opinion. Write the three answers. Change one behavior.

Real life

Real-Life Challenge

Money. A stock or a course you already paid for. Sunk cost vs next dollar. Work. "Leadership already told the board." What are you protecting? News. Vivid crime story vs base rate.

Bad room

Bad-Room Version

The board is in an hour. Everyone agrees. Your job is one sentence: "The base rate is ugly. Here is a reversible test. Here is what would change my mind after." Then stop.

Reflect

Reflection

  • Which animal is your house pet?
  • Who is allowed to change your mind?
Remember

Remember This

If nothing could change your mind, you are not thinking. You are representing a team.

Deep dive

Optional Deep Dive

Read Kahneman for the map, then a skeptical methods piece so you do not become a bias-poster person. The point is humility with a procedure, not a new identity as The Rational One.

Mission

Field Mission

In one meeting, ask "what would change our mind?" and mean it.
