Medical & Mental Health

Someone is dangerously drunk or has taken something

high The Recovery Positionalcoholdrugsfirst aidsafety
1

What is happening

At a party, in a bar, or at home. They are barely responsive, or vomiting, or their breathing has changed. People around you are laughing or have drifted away, and someone is suggesting putting them to bed.

2

What is actually going on

  • The classic fatal error is letting someone sleep it off on their back. Unconscious people vomit and cannot protect their airway.
  • Alcohol continues to be absorbed after the last drink, so someone who is merely very drunk can deteriorate for another hour or more.
  • Reduced or noisy breathing, cold clammy skin, blue lips, and unresponsiveness are emergency signs, not drunk signs.
  • Fear of getting someone in trouble kills people. Many jurisdictions have specific protections for calling for medical help.
3

Do this, in this order

  1. Try to rouse them properlyLoud voice, shake the shoulders. If they cannot be woken, treat it as a medical emergency now.
  2. Put them on their side, not their backRecovery position: on their side, head tilted slightly back and down, top leg bent forward to stop them rolling.
  3. Stay with them and keep watching breathingDo not leave them in a room alone "to sleep." Monitoring is the entire job.
  4. Call emergency services if any warning sign is presentCannot be woken, slow or irregular breathing, blue or grey lips, seizure, cold clammy skin, repeated vomiting, or a head injury.
  5. Keep them warm and do not give food or drinkNo coffee, no water forced down, no walking them about, no cold shower.
  6. Find out what they took, honestlyAsk friends without judgement and keep any packaging. Paramedics need this and nobody is in trouble.
4

Words you can borrow

To the group
"Nobody is putting them to bed. They stay on their side and someone stays with them."
Calling for help
"Unresponsive adult, has been drinking and possibly took something, breathing is slow and noisy." Give the address first and clearly.
To their friends
"I am not judging anyone and nobody is getting in trouble. I need to know what they took so the paramedics can help."
If they resist help
"You can be angry with me tomorrow. Right now I am staying with you."
5

What makes it worse

  • Letting them lie on their back or face down on a sofa.
  • Coffee, cold showers, or walking them around — none of these help and all waste time.
  • Making them vomit deliberately.
  • Leaving them with someone who is also heavily intoxicated.
  • Not calling because of fear of police, parents, or embarrassment. This is the decision people regret permanently.
6

Once the pressure drops

  • Stay until they are genuinely awake and oriented, or until they are handed to medics.
  • Tell them plainly the next day what happened. Not a lecture — the facts.
  • If it was a mixture, and especially if it involved opioids or sedatives, raise it seriously. Combination is what kills.
  • If you are around this repeatedly, learn where naloxone is available and how to use it.
Where this stops being yours to handle

If breathing stops or you cannot feel a pulse, start CPR immediately and get someone to call emergency services and find a defibrillator. Push hard and fast in the centre of the chest and do not stop until help arrives.

A note on this library

These are general principles written to be useful under pressure, not legal, medical, or financial advice. Laws and emergency procedures differ by country. If life is at risk, contact your emergency services first and read this afterwards.
