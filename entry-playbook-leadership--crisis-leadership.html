Leadership

Crisis leadership

427 words

A crisis is a leader's most visible moment. Handled well, it is when reputations are made. Handled poorly, it is when they are lost.

Note

THE TOOL — The crisis leader's checklist

Note

First hour: • Acknowledge the crisis publicly to the team. Do not minimize. Do not catastrophize. • Identify the top three questions that need answers. Assign an owner to each. • Establish a communication cadence. 'We'll update at [time].' • Do not commit to a resolution timeline you can't verify. First day: • Own the narrative. To customers, to leadership, to the team. In your own words, in the order you want them to hear them. • Protect the team's ability to actually work on the crisis. Fewer meetings, not more. Fewer reporters, not more. First week: • Structure the response. Who's on it, who's off it. Rotations if it will run more than three days. • Manage upward. Your leadership needs to know what's happening and what you're doing about it. Silence during a crisis is career-damaging. After the crisis: • Write it up honestly. What happened, what we did well, what we did badly, what we're changing. • Take real time off. Crises are exhausting. Recovering is a leadership discipline, not a weakness.

Note

THE GREAT MINDS COUNCIL — On leadership, from the Council

Note

Through Marcus Aurelius's lens: 'What we do in life echoes in eternity.' A leader's small choices — the tone in a meeting, the response to a mistake, the recognition of a quiet contribution — are compounded by everyone watching. Through Nelson Mandela's lens: 'It is better to lead from behind and to put others in front, especially when you celebrate victory when nice things occur. You take the front line when there is danger.' Through Lincoln's lens: 'I don't like that man. I must get to know him better.' Especially in leadership. The people you dismiss are the people whose views you most need. Through Andy Grove's lens: 'Success breeds complacency. Complacency breeds failure. Only the paranoid survive.' Through Frances Frei's lens: Trust = authenticity + logic + empathy. Weakness in any one dimension undermines the whole. Through Chris Hadfield's lens: The astronaut's rule: 'sweat the small stuff' in training, so you don't have to sweat it in the moment. Where the Council might be wrong: Leadership frameworks are often written by people who succeeded — a survivor-biased sample. The cemetery of failed leaders who followed the same advice is invisible. Take frameworks as prompts, not laws.

Remember This

“Leadership is producing results through people. The rest is scaffolding for that.”
