Better Questions

The quality of your questions is the quality of your mind

24 minThe Question Ladder845 words22 sections
Skill
Upgrade the question before you hunt the answer
Tool
The Question Ladder
Result
You ask things that actually move the problem
In one line
A better question is a cheaper strategy.
The idea

The Hook

People do not fail because they cannot find answers.

They fail because they spend three days answering a question that was never going to matter, while the question that would have unlocked the room sits unasked, because it felt too simple, too political, or too likely to make them look like they did not already know.

Socrates made a career of this and was executed for it, which is a useful reminder that good questions are not always loved. You are not being asked to become a public menace. You are being asked to stop hunting with a closed fist.

The room

The Scenario

Elena: "Can we be done by Friday?"

Jonah hears a yes/no. He says yes, because no feels like incompetence.

The better ladder:

  • Clarify: What does "done" mean in a sentence a customer could read?
  • Cause: What is making Friday hard?
  • Option: If Friday is real, what gets dropped?
  • Risk: What breaks if we hit Friday ugly?
  • Decision: Who is allowed to change the date?

Same four seconds of mouth. Different life.

Your move

Your Move

Common: Answer the question as asked, even if it is a trap. Better: "It depends." True, and often useless. Expert: Climb one rung: "When you say done, do you mean in the customer's hands or internally signed off?" Resourceful: Expert, plus you already know your BATNA if Friday is fake.

The idea

The Big Idea

Questions are tools with jobs. Use a clarify question to share a picture. Use a cause question to find a lever. Use an option question to invent. Use a risk question to stay adult. Use a decision question to end the meeting.

Explained

The Simple Explanation

The Question Ladder

  1. Clarify — What are we talking about? Done, soon, urgent, simple, they, always.
  2. Cause — What is producing this? (Not who is evil.)
  3. Option — What else could we do?
  4. Risk — What could go wrong? What happens next?
  5. Decision — What are we choosing, who owns it, by when?

Closed questions (yes/no) are for confirmation at the end, not for exploration at the start. Open questions are not automatically good — "What are your thoughts?" in a status-heavy room is an invitation to fog.

Peter Drucker's useful bias: the important and difficult job is not to find the right answer, it is to find the right question. Treat that as a lens, not a poster.

The tool

The Practical Tool

Write the ladder on the top of your meeting note. Force at least one question per rung before you offer a plan.

Words that usually need a clarify rung: soon, just, simple, they, always, strategic, alignment, done, quick, ownerless "we."

Fast path

The Fastest Reliable Method

When someone asks a binary, answer with a define: "What would good look like?"

The hack

The Hack

Ask the question you are afraid looks stupid. It is often the one everyone else is also afraid of. Status is expensive. Clarity is cheaper.

AI move

The AI Move

Context: The question I was asked: "[question]"
The real situation: [facts]
Goal: Rewrite using the Question Ladder — one question per rung. Mark which one I should ask first given politics: [brief].
Constraints: Short enough to say out loud. No jargon.
Tech

The Technology Move

A shared doc with the five rungs as headings. Meeting recordings you actually timestamp. Not a question bot that interrogates humans for sport.

Mistake

The Common Mistake

Using questions as a weapon — the fake-curious cross-examination. People feel it. They stop telling you the truth.

Defense

The Defense

Some questions are not yours to ask in that room. Read the Room (4.1). A brilliant question at the wrong status moment is just a career story.

Council

The Masters' Table

Socrates. Not-knowing as a method. Drucker. Right question over right answer. Voss. Calibrated questions ("How am I supposed to do that?") as negotiation, with ethical limits. Where wrong. Endless questions as stalling. After the ladder, choose.

Best move

The Best-Possible Response

Elena's Friday. You ask what done means. It means "customer can invoice." That is not the same as "code merged." You put the drop-list on the table. Friday survives as a smaller animal. You look less heroic than Jonah's yes. You also do not lie.

Practice

Practice

Take five questions from your last two days. Rewrite each one rung higher. Say one of them tomorrow.

Real life

Real-Life Challenge

Salary. Not "can I have more" first. Clarify the range, the job, the BATNA. Then ask. Family. "Why don't you ever…" → "When you said X, what did you need?" Research. "Is this true?" → "What would count as a primary source here?"

Bad room

Bad-Room Version

A VP asks a yes/no in public. You are not allowed a seminar. One clarify, then a time: "If done means in their hands, I need two hours to answer honestly."

Reflect

Reflection

  • Which rung do you live on?
  • Which question are you avoiding because of status?
Remember

Remember This

Answer a bad question brilliantly and you still lose. Climb first.

Deep dive

Optional Deep Dive

In Superforecasting, Tetlock's better questioners unpack vague terms and hunt base rates. That is the clarify and cause rungs with numbers. You will do this fully in Level 7. For now, refuse to hunt an undefined animal.

Mission

Field Mission

In one real conversation today, ask "What would good look like?" and then shut up for five seconds.
