Family, Love & Social

Someone close has died and you have to function

medium The Two-Week Listgriefdeathadminfamily
1

What is happening

It has happened. There are calls to make, a service to arrange, relatives arriving with opinions, and an administrative machine that expects you to produce documents while you cannot remember your own address.

2

What is actually going on

  • Grief and admin arrive simultaneously and the admin has deadlines. This is one of the cruellest designs in modern life and it is survivable in list form.
  • You will function oddly — competent one hour, unable to make a sandwich the next. That is normal and it is not a sign you are handling it badly.
  • Family conflict at funerals is about grief looking for a target and about old hierarchies resurfacing. Almost none of it is about the flowers.
  • Decisions made to avoid conflict in week one — money, possessions, promises — tend to be regretted for years.
3

Do this, in this order

  1. Do only what has a legal deadline this weekRegistering the death, the certificate, informing the necessary institutions. Everything else can wait, including thank-you notes.
  2. Get multiple copies of the death certificateYou will need far more than you expect and getting them later is slower and dearer.
  3. Let people do specific tasksNot "let me know if you need anything." Assign: collect Aunt June, make the sandwiches, ring the pension office.
  4. Delay every optional decision by a monthClearing the house, selling the car, giving away clothes. There is no prize for speed and there is real regret in it.
  5. Eat, drink water, and sleep when you canGrief is physically taxing and dehydration and low blood sugar mimic collapse.
  6. Write down what you have done and what is leftYou will not remember. One page on the fridge prevents doing things twice.
4

Words you can borrow

Delegating
"Yes — can you do the order of service and ring the caterers? That would genuinely help."
Deferring decisions
"I am not deciding about the house this month. Ask me in November."
Managing a difficult relative
"I know you would have done it differently. This is how we are doing it, and I would like you there."
When you cannot talk
"I cannot do this conversation today. I am not upset with you. Text me instead."
5

What makes it worse

  • Making financial or property decisions in the first month.
  • Promising possessions to relatives in the emotion of the day.
  • Drinking through it. It delays grief by months and adds problems.
  • Trying to hold the whole family's emotions as well as your own.
  • Returning to work immediately to avoid feeling it. The bill arrives with interest.
6

Once the pressure drops

  • Expect the hard part around six to twelve weeks, when everyone else has moved on and the admin stops.
  • Mark the anniversaries deliberately rather than being ambushed by them.
  • Sort your own paperwork as a result — will, wishes, passwords. Almost everyone who goes through this does, and it is a real act of love.
  • Consider bereavement support if it is not shifting after some months, especially if the death was sudden or traumatic.
A note on this library

These are general principles written to be useful under pressure, not legal, medical, or financial advice. Laws and emergency procedures differ by country. If life is at risk, contact your emergency services first and read this afterwards.
