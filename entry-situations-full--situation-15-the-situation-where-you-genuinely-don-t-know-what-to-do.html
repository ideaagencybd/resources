Situation 15 — The Situation Where You Genuinely Don't Know What to Do

384 words

The Situation

You've done the Level 1 pause. You've defined the problem. You've run CODA-R. You've listed options. And you still genuinely don't know what to do. All of the options look bad, or unclear, or unknowable.

What Most People Do

Freeze. Or: pick an option randomly to escape the discomfort. Or: pretend to know what to do.

Why It Often Fails

None of those produce good outcomes. And you're not actually alone here — the belief that you should already know is often the biggest obstacle.

The Resourceful Response

Ask for help. Not with the answer — with the thinking. The right question, asked to the right person, transforms uncertainty into clarity faster than any amount of solo pondering.

Words To Use

"I'm stuck on this in a genuine way. Could I have 20 minutes with you to walk through my thinking? I don't need you to solve it — I need help spotting what I'm missing."

"Here's my current thinking. Here's where I'm getting stuck. What would you ask me that I haven't asked myself?"

"You've handled situations like this before. If you were me, what would be the first thing you'd do?"

Words To Avoid

"I don't know what to do. [as a closing statement]"

"Just tell me what to do. [skipping the thinking]"

"I've got it under control. [when you don't]"

What Can Still Go Wrong

Sometimes the honest answer is that the situation has no good option. Then the question becomes: which bad option is least bad, most reversible, and consistent with the person you want to be? Even 'no good option' is a decidable frame. But you'll get to it faster with help than alone.

· Final Note On The Playbooks ·

The playbooks in this section are compressions. Every one of them could be its own book. Some are (see the Further Reading appendix).

Their purpose here is not to be exhaustive — it is to be close at hand when a real situation arises. The reader who returns to the situation cards on a Tuesday morning before a difficult call gets more value from this book than the reader who finishes it once and puts it away.

Come back often. That is the design.
