Mastery · 6.B

Boss Fight: Digital Chaos

Hacked-looking email, broken sheet, hallucinated brief

40 minSecurity Floor + Verify + Clean Table3,197 words23 sections
Skill
Stay calm inside a digital mess
Tool
Security Floor + Verify + Clean Table
Result
You contain the damage and restore a working system
In one line
Contain first. Then verify. Then repair the sheet. Not the other way around.
The idea

The Hook

It will not arrive as one problem.

It will arrive as a morning: a message that might be a compromise, a sheet that will not add up, a brief that already went upstairs with a number a model invented, and a room that wants you to fix the visible thing because the visible thing has a meeting attached.

If you repair the sheet while an inbox is forwarding to a stranger, you are tidying a burning kitchen.

This boss is not a puzzle about who is cleverest with a formula. It is a test of order: contain, verify, repair. Security Floor first. Then Claim-Source-Date-Math-Human. Then a Clean Table. Judgment stays human. Brands do not matter. The sequence does.

The room

The Scenario

Monday, 8:07 a.m. You are Mara's stand-in. Elena's customer review is at 9:30.

Object 1 — the mail. Your work inbox shows a message from "IT Security" at 7:41: "We detected a sign-in from Moldova. Reset now to keep access." A link. A logo. Overnight you also got a "you have a new forwarding rule" notice you have not opened. Jonah texted: "Did you send a weird invoice to North region? They pinged me."

Object 2 — the sheet. The quarter file — the one Elena will project — has two header rows, three spellings of West, a handwritten total in row 200 that finance already distrusts, and a filter still on from Thursday. Jonah's tab "DASHBOARD" shows West down 40%. A note in cell A1: =40% // from AI brief, don't touch.

Object 3 — the brief. A one-pager in the shared drive, generated Friday night with a model, already sent to Elena. It says West revenue is $47,000 (down 40% vs last quarter) and cites "internal analytics, Q2 board pack, p. 12" and a paper, "Chen & Alvarez, Regional Demand Decay, 2024." You cannot find p. 12. You cannot find the paper. The $47,000 does not match a quick filter of the sheet, which, if you squint at visible cells, looks more like $112,000 before you trust anything.

Object 4 — possible compromise. The invoice Jonah mentioned is a PDF "from you" asking North to wire a "correction." You did not send it. Your phone is on the same work identity.

Elena pings: "I forwarded the 47k brief to the customer. Don't make me look stupid. Also fix the West slide. See you at 9:30."

Jonah: "I'll have the agent resend a corrected brief to everyone on the thread, including the customer. Faster."

You have 83 minutes, one body, and four objects that all want to be first.

What do you do?

Your move

Your Move

Do not keep reading. Pick A, B, or C. Write it down. Then read the consequence for that letter only.

Option A — Fix what Elena can see

You dive the sheet. You fight the 40%. You ask a model to "rewrite the brief with the right number" and let Jonah's agent send it to the thread, customer included, so Elena is covered. You leave the IT mail and the forwarding notice for after the meeting. The invoice is "probably a mix-up."

Option B — Prove the 47k is fake, in public, now

You reply-all to Elena and the customer: the paper is hallucinated, the 40% is a lie, Jonah's dashboard is garbage, and IT's email is probably a phish. You attach a half-filtered screenshot. You click the IT link in another tab "just to see if it's fake." You will clean the sheet after you have told the truth.

Option C — Contain, then verify, then repair

You do not click the IT link. You do not let the agent send. You treat the identity as possibly compromised until the known front door says otherwise. Then you retract or hold the number without a new fake number. Then you build a Clean Table for one defensible sentence at 9:30.

Consequences

Consequence Engine

If you chose A

What happened: Jonah's agent sent a "correction" at 8:22. It still used $47,000 because the model read the brief as source. The customer now has two mails. At 8:31 North replies that they almost wired the invoice; their finance wants a call. At 8:40 you notice the forwarding rule is real — a copy of your mail, including Elena's thread, is leaving the building. The sheet is prettier. The kitchen is on fire.

Elena, 8:41: "Stop sending. IT is on the other line. What did you do?"

Retry. The meeting is not the boss. The identity is. Go back and pick a different letter. Contain first.

If you chose B

What happened: The customer now has a public accusation and a screenshot with a filter still on. Their counsel is on the thread asking whether your company fabricates analytics. The IT link was a phish; you typed your password on it at 8:16 because you were already hot. The forwarding rule, which was real, is now the least of it — a session is open in a country you are not in. Jonah's 40% is still the slide in Elena's deck because you never built a table. You were right about the ghost paper and wrong about the order.

Elena, 8:50: "You may be correct and I still cannot put you in front of the customer."

Retry. Truth without contain is a leak with a speech attached. Pick again.

If you chose C

What happened: You did not click. You messaged Jonah: do not send. You opened the real identity provider by typing the URL (or a bookmark you already trust). You followed the official compromised-account steps: sessions, forwarding, password, MFA. You called IT through a known number, not the mail. You told Elena, privately: "Possible account issue. I am containing. Do not use 47k. I will not put a replacement number on the customer thread until I have a source. I will have a defensible West sentence by 9:15 if the identity is stable."

At 8:35 IT confirms a forwarding rule you did not set and kills it. The invoice is a fake; North is told through a known channel not to pay. The ghost paper dies in a folder, not on letterhead a second time. You copy RAW, build DATA, SUMIFS West, walk three rows. West is not down 40%. Jonah compared a partial window to a full one (6.6). You have a from-to, a denominator, and an assumptions box.

9:22. Elena has a three-line brief that is ugly and true. The customer has a hold, not a second hallucination. You look less heroic than Option B. You still have a job and a room that can be used.

This is the path. Read on for the tools, the meeting, and the after-action. If you landed here on the second try, that is the lesson, not a shame.

The idea

The Big Idea

Contain first. Verify second. Repair third.

A digital mess will beg you to fix the artifact that has a meeting. The artifact is rarely the blast radius. An identity that is leaking, a number that is traveling, a robot that is still allowed to send — those are the fires. The Pause still comes first. Then the Security Floor. Then CSDMH. Then the Clean Table. Then, if you have time, a chart that does not lie.

Explained

The Simple Explanation

Four objects, one sequence.

Contain (Security Floor). Surprise link = hostile until the known front door says otherwise. Possible forwarding, fake invoice, Jonah's agent: these are actions in the world. Stop actions. Official platform guidance, not a model, not a hero video. Kick sessions. Kill forwarding. Revoke the agent's send. Tell North, through a known channel, do not pay. Tell Elena, privately, hold the number.

Verify (Claim, Source, Date, Math, Human). $47,000, 40%, p. 12, Chen & Alvarez 2024. Each is a claim. Open or delete. You never fake a citation a model invented. You never let an agent "correct" a brief by reading the brief. High-stakes customer numbers get a Human owner: you, with a table, or you say you do not know yet.

Repair (Clean Table). RAW copy. One header. Normalize West. No handwritten total. SUMIFS or a pivot. Check row count. Assumptions: window, definition, what you excluded. BDC on Jonah's 40%: from-to, denominator, causation (here, a bad comparison window — not a tragedy, a trick).

If time dies, a true incomplete sentence beats a fluent complete one: "West, comparable window, is $X vs $Y last quarter, n = Z deals. The 47k and 40% are retracted. Source: this export, timestamp."

The tool

The Practical Tool

Name: Contain → Verify → Repair

Under heat, write the three words. Then:

  1. Hands off surprise links and the send button. Pause. Tell any agent no.
  2. Known front door for identity. Official hijack steps. IT via known channel. Sessions, forwarding, MFA, password.
  3. Stop the money and the mail. Fake invoice: warn recipients on a channel you already use. Retract or hold the traveling number. Do not replace it with a new unverified number.
  4. Split claims. Brief vs sheet vs mail. CSDMH on the brief. Do not use the brief as a source for the sheet.
  5. Clean Table for one question: West, this quarter vs a comparable window.
  6. One true artifact for 9:30: three lines, from-to, n, what is retracted.
  7. Log. What you touched, what you did not click, who you told, when the forwarding died.
Fast path

The Fastest Reliable Method

If you have ten minutes, not eighty-three:

  1. Do not click. Do not send.
  2. Known door: sessions + forwarding.
  3. Private message to Elena: "Hold 47k. Possible account issue. No new number yet."
  4. Known channel to North: "Do not pay that invoice."
  5. If identity is stable, one filter-off SUM of West on a copy, written as "preliminary, not the 40%."

That is not mastery. That is not dying in the first ten minutes.

The hack

The Hack

Put the customer thread on hold, not on correct.

A correction is a new claim. A hold is a governor (6.8). "Do not use the Friday number; replacement by 9:15 from a human-checked table" is a professional sentence. Two competing numbers in a customer's inbox is how counsel gets involved.

AI move

The AI Move

You may use a model after contain, on redacted data, as analyst or critic — not as sender, not as incident responder, not as a source.

Do not paste: the live invoice, tokens, recovery codes, customer personal data, the contents of the possibly forwarded mail.

Prompt (only once identity is stable):

Context: I am repairing a brief that must not invent numbers. Role: hostile reviewer, then analyst.
Claims in the old brief: [list, no customer names]
From my Clean Table (redacted): West comparable = [n], amount = [x], prior window = [y]
Goal: 1) Mark each old claim SOURCE / UNSOURCED / CONTRADICTED. 2) Draft a 5-line retraction-and-replace for an internal manager, BLUF, no new citations. 3) Challenge my window definition.
Constraints: NOT IN SOURCE if I didn't give it. No send-instructions. No legal advice.
Verification: Every number in the draft must appear in my table lines above.

What to verify: The five lines against the sheet, by hand.

Tech

The Technology Move

Jobs, not brands: identity recovery (your provider's official page), a spreadsheet (Excel or Sheets), a human mail client with send still in human hands. An agent with send permission is a loaded tool. Revoke first.

What's changing. The phish will look more like IT. The agent will be more eager to "help" by sending. The sequence does not change.

Mistake

The Common Mistake

Fixing the slide because the slide has a meeting.

The second mistake: a public unmasking of a hallucination before the leak is stopped. Being right is not contain.

Recovery from A or B, if this were real: stop sending, known door, official IT, hold the number, then the table. You do not "add a better agent." You turn the agent off.

Defense

The Defense

This sequence fails when:

  • You are locked out of the known door. Then a second device, a phone call to known IT, a colleague in the building. Do not use the phish as the door.
  • Elena refuses the hold. You still do not put your name on 47k. Facts vs Stories. Offer a true sentence. Do not launder hers.
  • The sheet cannot be cleaned by 9:30. Then 9:30 is a hold plus a time, not a fiction.
  • Physical safety or a crime in progress. This is a digital boss. If money is already gone, the bank's official fraud path, not a course.
Council

The Masters' Table

Shannon. Stop the leaky channel before you encode a prettier message.

Hopper. Debug the procedure: forwarding, send permission, handwritten total. The bug is often the step everyone skipped because a meeting was near.

Feynman. Do not fool yourself that a second fluent brief is a source.

Gates. Multiply only the clean step. An agent on a dirty number multiplies the error.

Taleb. The tail is the forwarding rule you did not open because the slide was louder.

Turing. The machine will keep imitating competence. You are the one who can stop it.

Synthesis. Contain the channel, verify the claims, repair the table. Heroics are a failure mode.

Where the council could be wrong. Sometimes the mail is fake and the identity is fine, and you "wasted" twenty minutes on sessions. Those twenty minutes are cheap. A single forwarded customer thread is not.

Best move

The Best-Possible Response

Calm. Phone face down for one breath. You will not click to make the feeling stop.

Observe. Four objects. Traveling 47k. Possible forwarding. Fake invoice. Dirty 40%. Agent offering to send.

Define. 8:07–8:40 is contain. 8:40–9:15 is one true number or a hold. 9:30 is a usable room.

Verify. Identity first, via known door — you do not yet know if Moldova is real.

Prioritize. Stop send and leaks. Then money (North). Then retract 47k internally. Then table. Then Elena's face.

Find resources / people. Official IT. North's known contact. Elena private. Not Jonah's agent. Not the link.

Options. A, B, C. You already saw the tails.

Risks. Elena looks bad if you hold. Elena looks worse if a second fake goes out. You look slow. You look like the adult.

Choose. C.

Communicate. Jonah: "Do not send. Off switch." Elena: "Hold 47k. Containing an account issue. True West by 9:15 or we go in with a hold." North: "Do not pay. Official channel only."

Act. Sessions, forwarding, password, MFA. Revoke agent send. RAW → DATA → SUMIFS → check. BDC on 40%. Delete Chen & Alvarez from anything that might travel.

Document. Timestamped log. What you clicked (nothing). What IT confirmed. The comparable window.

Adapt. If IT says the forwarding was a false notice, you still do not click surprise links next time. If West is down, you say the true from-to, not Jonah's axis.

Reflect. What pulled you toward the slide?

That is a resourceful human in digital chaos. Not a hacker. A person with a sequence.

Practice

Practice

Fifteen minutes.

  1. Write your actual 8:07 order of operations in eight lines, no tools named, only jobs.
  2. Mark each line Contain, Verify, or Repair.
  3. If Repair appears before Contain, rewrite.
  4. Draft Elena's three-line 9:22 brief from this toy table:
RegionThis quarterPrior (same length)
West112108
East9095
  1. Write the retraction sentence for 47k and for 40%.
  2. Write the one sentence to Jonah that stops the agent.
Real life

Real-Life Challenge

Work — mixed incident. Situation: phish + bad number + helpful bot. What most people do: the slide. Why it fails: the leak is the blast radius. Resourceful response: C. Words to use: "Hold. Known door. No send." Words to avoid: "The AI will fix it," reply-all truth bombs, the surprise link. Tool: Contain → Verify → Repair. What could go wrong: Elena wants a number more than a hold. Offer a time, not a fiction.

Digital — family version. Situation: a parent got the IT mail, a shared photo album, a "repair" person on the phone. Resourceful response: hang up, known door, official recovery, then the photos' backup. Do not give the caller a code. What could go wrong: shame. Sit with them. Do not outsource to a model.

Bad room

Bad-Room Version

Worse: you already clicked at 7:50. The agent already sent a "correction." The customer paid a small test wire to see if the invoice was real. Elena is in a taxi to the customer.

Contain is still first. It is just later.

Different device if you can. Official hijack steps. Bank/fraud official path for the wire — not a hero. Call Elena: facts, not a novel. "A fake invoice got a test payment. I am with official fraud and IT. Do not discuss 47k. The Friday brief is retracted." Then the table, if you are still the person for 9:30, or you hand the sheet to finance and you stay on the identity.

You do not fly to Moldova. You do not pay a recovery scammer. You do not have the agent apologize.

Reflect

Reflection

  • What object in the scenario pulled your hands first?
  • Where do you still want to be the person who "tells the truth" before the leak is stopped?
  • Who can turn off your automations if you are the one compromised?
  • Which number in your work is one fluent paragraph away from a customer?
  • If this happened tomorrow, where is the known IT door — URL, phone, human?
Remember

Remember This

Contain the leak. Hold the number. Then build a table. Heroics are how the second incident starts.

Deep dive

Optional Deep Dive

Why the order is the lesson.

Incident response, even at a personal scale, has always had a contain-then-eradicate-then-recover shape. You do not need a SOC to steal the idea. A forwarding rule is an open channel. A send-capable agent is an open channel. A customer thread is an amplifier. Repairing a pivot while those are open is aesthetic.

Feynman's cargo cult shows up as a second brief with nicer formatting. Hopper's moth shows up as the handwritten 40% in A1. Taleb's tail is the invoice North almost paid while you formatted a chart. Shannon's noisy channel is the identity you did not lock.

Gates would still automate Monday's filing — after this week, with a Check, on a Clean Table, with the token that cannot send.

If you want a harder drill, write a one-page playbook for your stack: known IT door, how to list forwarding, how to revoke the glue tool, who to call at the bank, a hold sentence for Elena. That page is the boss fight, pre-lost.

Mission

Field Mission

In the next 24–48 hours, do the unglamorous version while nothing is on fire:

  1. Find your known identity door (type it, bookmark it).
  2. Open forwarding / filters / sessions. Confirm they are yours.
  3. Find the off switch for any send-capable automation (6.8).
  4. Write a three-line "hold the number" sentence in your notes.
  5. Pick one sheet you trust too much. Make a RAW copy.

Send yourself a four-line log: door, forwarding checked, off switch, hold sentence. No secrets in the log.
