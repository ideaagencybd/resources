Negotiate the Interests

Positions are the weather. Interests are the climate.

28 minBATNA + Trades4,021 words22 sections
Skill
Prepare a real negotiation
Tool
BATNA + Trades
Result
You can ask for more without blowing up the relationship
In one line
Know your walk-away. Trade interests, not speeches.
The idea

The Hook

You walk in asking for a number.

They say no to the number. You hear no to you. You either fold, because you need the job, or you harden, because you have already told two friends the number. The conversation becomes a tug on a single rope. Tug-of-war is not negotiation. It is a sport with one winner and a strained shoulder.

Under the number is a climate: they need to keep a band, you need rent and respect, they need you not to leave in October, you need a title that will travel, both of you need a story you can tell without wincing. Positions are what people say. Interests are why they say it. If you only fight positions, you will miss the trades.

This lesson is the salary ask, and also scope, deadline, and who picks up the child on Thursdays. Same tool. BATNA first — what you will do if there is no deal — then interests, then trades. Chris Voss is a tactical complement, not a replacement. Silence and labels will not save you if you have no walk-away and no map.

The room

The Scenario

Thursday, 9:00 a.m. A glass room. You have been in the role eighteen months. The market has moved. You have a written offer from elsewhere: 12% above current, slightly worse title, longer commute. You would prefer to stay.

Elena says, before you sit: "I can probably do a small bump. I cannot do a title this cycle. Please don't make this weird."

You had a script: the number, the market, the migration you ran. You can feel the script dying. Finance will need a story. Elena's interest is not your rent. It is her band, her VP, and not losing you in October.

She waits. The first person who names a number in a panic often names the wrong one.

What do you do?

Your move

Your Move

Do not keep reading yet. Write your BATNA in one sentence — what you will actually do if this room yields nothing. If you cannot, you are not ready to negotiate. You are ready to plead.

Common move. A position fight. "I need 15% and a title or I can't stay." Maybe true as a feeling. As a first move it corners her. She will protect the band and you will both lose the stay.

Better move. Hand her the other offer like a weapon. "I have 12% elsewhere." Sometimes necessary. As a first move it can feel like a threat even when it is a fact. Threats change the weather. They also end some relationships.

Expert move. Interests and trades, with a real BATNA in your pocket, not on the table yet. "I want to stay. I'm not asking you to be a market magician in one meeting. My interests are pay that doesn't fall behind, a title that will travel in two years, and a scope that matches what I'm already doing. What are the constraints you actually have — money, title, scope, timing, a written path? I'll trade on timing if the path is real."

Resourceful move. Prepare the BATNA worksheet before the glass room. Pause when she says "don't make this weird" — that is her identity talking. Accurate Mirror: she needs this not to become a scene, and she has a band. Then expert. Put the other offer on the table only as proof, not as a hostage note, and only if you would actually leave.

Why the common move fails: a single-rope tug ignores her constraints, so she cannot help you even if she wants to.

What can go wrong with the expert move: "interests" can become a fog that never names a number. You still need a number, a range, and a walk-away. Soft people skip the number. Hard people skip the climate. Resourceful people have both.

The idea

The Big Idea

Positions are the weather. Interests are the climate.

Prepare your BATNA — Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement — so you are not begging. Then trade. Fisher and Ury named this in Getting to Yes (1981). It is a popular, durable map, not a laboratory result for your salary band. Steal it anyway. Then use Voss for the moments in the room: labels, calibrated questions, silence. Tactics without a BATNA are theater.

Explained

The Simple Explanation

Positions: "15%." "No title this cycle." "I need you to pick up Thursdays."

Interests: security, fairness, face, a story for Finance, a story for your family, sleep, status that will travel, not being the villain, not being the fool who stayed cheap.

Two people can fight positions and both lose a deal that would have met interests. A title later plus money now plus a written scope might beat a title now with no money, depending on your climate. You will not know until you ask what they can actually move.

BATNA. What you will do if this negotiation yields nothing. Another offer. Stay and wait a cycle. Leave without an offer (usually a worse BATNA than people admit). A cheaper apartment. A sibling who can do Thursdays. A BATNA is not a fantasy. It is a plan you would actually run. The better your BATNA, the less you need to slime (4.6). The worse your BATNA, the more you need trades, patience, and a smaller ask — not a louder speech.

Fisher, Ury, and Patton also push: separate the people from the problem; invent options before locking a number; insist on using some external standard (market data, a band, a precedent) so it is not only will. Those are useful. They fail when the other side has all the power and no need to invent. Then your BATNA is the point, and so is knowing when to stop talking.

Voss as complement. Never Split the Difference (2016) is tactical: "no" as the start of information, labels ("It seems like you have a band you can't break"), calibrated questions ("How am I supposed to do that?" used carefully), mirrors, silence. It is not a replacement for interests, and "How am I supposed to do that?" can sound like a game if you do not have a real constraint. Use it when you need information and time. Do not use it as a personality.

Trades, concessions, silence, questions.

  • Trades: I move on timing, you move on money. I take scope, you take a title path.
  • Concessions: they should cost you, be named, and get something back. A free concession trains them to wait.
  • Silence: after a number, after a question. Let them fill it. If you fill it, you are negotiating with yourself.
  • Questions: "What can you actually move?" "What would Finance need to see?" "If not a title, what would make this fair in six months?"

Salary, scope, deadline, family logistics — same skeleton. The Thursday pickup is a negotiation of interests (sleep, fairness, a career night, a child's bedtime), not a moral exam of who loves the child more.

The tool

The Practical Tool

Name: BATNA + Trades

The BATNA worksheet (fill before the room):

FieldYour notes
The negotiationWhat deal, with whom, by when
My position (opening)The first number / ask I can say without wincing
My targetWhat I actually want
My walk-awayThe line below which I run the BATNA
My BATNAThe plan I will actually run if there is no deal
BATNA qualityStrong / medium / weak — be honest
Their likely positionWhat they will say first
Their likely interestsBand, face, retention, fairness, VP story, time
Their likely BATNAReplace me, wait me out, a cheaper hire, a sibling, a vendor
External standardsMarket data, bands, precedents, dates, legal minimums
Trades I can offerTiming, scope, title path, location, hours, on-call, a project
Trades I wantMoney, title, scope, deadline, help, a written path
ProofFacts they can check (4.6)
QuestionsThree calibrated questions, in my mouth
If they say noNext step, not a sulk
Residue testCan I stay in this relationship after my tactics?

In the room:

  1. Pause. Mirror their constraint. "You have a band this cycle, and you don't want this to be a scene."
  2. State the stay (or the relationship) if it is true. "I want to stay."
  3. Name your interests, then one or two trades, then a number range if this is money.
  4. Ask what they can move. Then silence.
  5. Package, do not nibble. "If we do X money now, Y written title review in March, Z scope on paper, I can live with no title today."
  6. Write it down before you leave. A deal that lives only in the air is not a deal.

Pocket card:

WALK-AWAY → THEIR INTERESTS → PACKAGE OF TRADES → WRITE IT DOWN.

A salary package example:

If they cannotYou might trade
Title nowWritten criteria and a date, copied to the VP
15%8% now + a six-month review + scope that matches the work
RemoteMoney, or a two-day office deal that is real
Anything this quarterA stay bonus, a course, a hiring slot, a project that will travel on a CV

If the other offer is real and you would take it, it can be named as a fact: "I have another offer at 12% more, worse title, worse commute. I would rather stay for a package that doesn't make me a fool in October." That is BLUF plus BATNA. It is not a tantrum.

Fast path

The Fastest Reliable Method

When the meeting has already started and your worksheet is in a drawer:

Three questions. One silence.

  1. "What can you actually move — money, title, scope, timing?"
  2. "What would Finance / the other person need to see?"
  3. "If we can't do it today, what would a real path look like on paper?"

Then stop talking. If you cannot take notes, you are not in a negotiation. You are in a vibe. Ask for twenty-four hours: "I want to stay, and I don't want to invent a number in a panic. Let me come back tomorrow with a package." Buying a night is often the most resourceful move in the glass room.

The hack

The Hack

Decide the walk-away in writing, in a calm hour, with a person who is not impressed by your bravado.

The walk-away you invent in the room is not a walk-away. It is a mood. The hack is a sentence in your notes app: "If they cannot do X by Y, I run Z." Look at it before you enter. If Z is a fantasy, you do not have a BATNA. You have a wish.

AI move

The AI Move

AI is useful for the worksheet, for market language, and for spotting when you are about to threaten by accident. It does not know your band, your offer letter, or whether you would actually leave. It will happily inflate your BATNA. That inflation is how people resign in their heads and then fold in the room.

Use AI for:

  • Filling the worksheet from messy notes
  • Packaging trades
  • "Rewrite this so it is not a threat"
  • Practice: you be Elena, keep going until I use interests

Do not use AI for:

  • Fake competing offers
  • A script to trap HR
  • Pasting other people's salaries from a leaked sheet if that would get someone fired (and it might)

[numbered]

Constraints: No fake offers. No new numbers. No "win-win" as a slogan. Label guesses.
Verification: What would make my BATNA weaker than I think?

What to verify: The other offer's real net (commute, title, vesting, health). The "market" number you quoted. Whether Elena can actually move a title.

Do not paste: the full offer letter with ID numbers, colleagues' pay, anything legally privileged.

Tech

The Technology Move

The job is "see the package, not only the number."

Current examples of the category:

  • A two-column sheet: their constraints, your trades
  • Salary bands and levels from a real internal doc, not a tweet
  • A follow-up email that restates the package the same day
  • Calendar: the March title review, actually booked

When not to use technology: do not let a compensation website become a personality. Those sites are noisy. Use them as one input, not as a weaponized "fact."

Mistake

The Common Mistake

Falling in love with a position. "It has to be 15%." Maybe it does. Often you wanted 15% because it was the story that would make you feel caught up. A package might do the same job. If you cannot name the job, you will worship the number.

The other mistake: revealing a fake BATNA. If you would not leave, do not play leave. They can call it. Then you have no face and no raise. Machiavelli would call this a misuse of appearances. He would be right, and you still should not become him.

Recovery: if you already blurted a number you cannot stand on, do not invent a second number in shame. "I put a number on the table too fast. I want to come back tomorrow with a package. I still want to stay." Then go do the worksheet.

Defense

The Defense

BATNA + Trades fails when:

  • You have no alternative and they know it. Then smaller asks, more proof, more time, a coalition — or acceptance, without self-contempt, while you build a BATNA for the next cycle.
  • They will not invent options. Some managers only say no. Then you run the BATNA or you stay with eyes open.
  • Family negotiations where "trade" sounds like a market in a place that should be a marriage. You can still name interests. You may need 4.4 and 4.8 more than a package. Do not become a consultant at your own table.
  • Voss-tactics in a culture that reads them as games. Then drop the cadence. Keep the questions.

Limitation: Getting to Yes can sound like two reasonable lawyers. Many rooms are not that. Power exists. So does spite. A good BATNA is how you survive both.

Council

The Masters' Table

Fisher and Ury. Through interests vs positions, BATNA, options, external standards. Application: Elena's "no title this cycle" is a position. Her interest is a band and a VP. Invent a path.

Chris Voss. Through labels, questions, "no" as information, silence. Application: "It seems like you have a constraint you can't break today." Then a question. Limit: not a replacement for a worksheet, not a hostage cadence in a glass room.

Machiavelli. Through their BATNA and yours as power. Application: if they can replace you cheaply, your speech about fairness is entertainment. Build a real alternative. Do not perform one.

Carnegie. Through not making Elena the villain — with caution. Application: "I want to stay" if true. Not as butter. As climate.

Lincoln. Through patience and a written outcome. Application: a title in March that the VP has seen is better than a heroic no in September.

Synthesis. The council agrees that walk-aways make you honest, and that trades beat tugs. They split on how much steel to show. Show only the steel you would actually use.

Where the council could be wrong. "Win-win" can become a way to talk yourself out of money you could have had. Some counterparties move only when they feel a real BATNA. If you always package and never walk, you are not principled. You are cheap to keep.

Best move

The Best-Possible Response

Back to the glass room.

Calm. "Don't make this weird" landed. Pause. That is her identity, not a gag order.

Observe. She can maybe do money. Not title this cycle. You have a real offer. You prefer to stay.

Define. The job of this meeting is a package or a scheduled second meeting, not a vibe.

Verify. Is the other offer still live? Is 12% net of commute? Would you take it? If not, it is not a BATNA.

Prioritize. Stay + pay + a written path. Title now is a want, not a walk-away, unless it is.

Find resources / people. Finance's story. The VP. A market number you can actually show.

Options. Tug 15%. Threaten with the offer. Interests and a package. Ask for a night.

Risks. Naming the offer poisons the stay. Not naming it leaves money on the table. A fake walk-away is the worst of both.

Choose. Mirror, interests, ask what she can move, package, write down.

Communicate. "I don't want this to be weird either. I want to stay. I also can't pretend the market didn't move. You have a band and no title this cycle — have I got the constraint right? My interests are pay that doesn't fall behind, a title that will travel, and scope that matches the work I'm already doing. What can you actually move, and what would a written path to title look like by March? I can trade on timing. I have another offer at 12% with a worse commute; I'd rather not use it. I want a package I can say yes to this week."

Then silence.

Act. Take notes. If she needs Finance, book the follow-up before you stand up.

Document. Same-day recap: numbers, dates, who will talk to whom.

Adapt. If the package misses the walk-away, run the BATNA. Do not run a sulk.

Reflect. Did you negotiate with yourself in the silence? Did you believe your own walk-away?

Practice

Practice

Do this now. Fifteen minutes.

  1. Pick a live negotiation: salary, scope, deadline, or family logistics.
  2. Fill the entire BATNA worksheet. No empty rows.
  3. Circle the BATNA. Would you actually run it this month? If no, rewrite until it is real or admit it is weak.
  4. Write a 90-second package out loud.
  5. Write three questions. Remove any that are just "How am I supposed to do that?" unless that is truly your voice and constraint.
  6. Write the recap email now, as if you already had the meeting. You are teaching yourself to leave with paper.

Then a drill: list five trades that are not money. Most people can only think of money. That poverty is expensive.

Real life

Real-Life Challenge

Work — the salary ask. Situation: you are under market, you would prefer to stay, there may or may not be a real other offer. What most people do: a number, a threat, or a hint for six months. Why it fails: a tug, a poisoned well, or a hope. Resourceful response: worksheet, interests, package, written path, true BATNA. Words to use: "What can you actually move?" "I want to stay." "Written criteria by March." Words to avoid: a fake offer, "I deserve," a comparison to a named peer's pay if that will torch a person. Tool: BATNA + Trades. BLUF the stay. Mirror her constraint. AI assist: the worksheet prompt; then strip threats. What could go wrong: you win money and lose the relationship because you enjoyed the steel too much. Residue test.

Family — logistics as climate. Situation: who picks up the child on Thursdays, or who flies for the holiday. What most people do: a moral exam. "If you cared, you would…" Why it fails: identity fight. Nobody can trade in a moral exam. Resourceful response: interests (sleep, a career night, fairness, the child's actual evening), options (a paid pickup, a swap, a four-week trial), a walk-away that is not a divorce threat unless you mean it. Words to use: "I need Thursdays this month for X. I can take Mondays. If we can't, the paid option is Y." Words to avoid: a scoreboard of who loves more. Tool: the same worksheet, in softer words. Purpose / Opening / Ask / Close if it is also a postponed talk. What could go wrong: you become a consultant in your own kitchen. Warmth is not the opposite of a plan.

Work — deadline and scope. Situation: they want the date and the extra feature. What most people do: a heroic yes. Why it fails: you sold a fake BATNA (you will "make it work"). Resourceful response: "You can have the date or the feature. If you need both, we cut Z or we add a person. Which trade?" Words to use: the triangle, on paper. Words to avoid: "I'll try."

Bad room

Bad-Room Version

Elena says: "If you even mention another offer, I will have to tell the VP you're a flight risk, and the bump goes away. So. Are you loyal, or not?"

This is not principled negotiation. This is a loyalty test as a club.

Pause. You do not pass a loyalty exam to earn a market.

"I am not here to threaten you, and I am not going to sit a loyalty test. I want to stay. I also have a decision to make about pay and path. If naming a fact about the market makes me a flight risk, I need to know that, because that's information about whether this is a place I can grow. I can come back tomorrow with a package that doesn't mention another employer. I cannot come back as someone who never asks."

Then you leave on time. You run the worksheet again with a colder eye on their BATNA. If they punish the ask, your BATNA just got more valuable, even if it is uncomfortable.

Do not bluff a resignation in this weather unless you will sign it.

Reflect

Reflection

  • What is your walk-away, in a sentence you would run this month?
  • Which position are you worshipping because it would feel like respect?
  • What is their interest, in words they would sign?
  • Where do you fill silences, and what number lives in that fear?
  • If they saw your tactics, would the relationship survive?
Remember

Remember This

If you cannot name the walk-away, you are not negotiating. You are hoping in a nicer chair.

Deep dive

Optional Deep Dive

A short map of two traditions.

Fisher/Ury is "principled negotiation": interests, options, standards, people separate from problems. It came out of a world of deals, diplomacy, and law. It is at its best when both sides can invent and both sides will live together afterward.

Voss is "tactical empathy" from crisis negotiation: the other side is often not inventing, they are armed, and "splitting the difference" can get someone hurt. Labels and "no" as safety have real uses in a salary meeting. They also become a TikTok personality. If you catch yourself collecting tactics like Pokémon, you have missed the worksheet.

On salary specifically: pay is lumpy, gendered, and path-dependent in ways a one-hour lesson cannot fix. This tool will not close a structural gap by itself. It will stop you from making the gap worse with a panic number, a fake offer, or a six-month hint. Use real data. Use a written recap. Use a person who has done this in your industry, not only a book.

If you want a practice beyond this lesson, fill a BATNA worksheet for a negotiation you already lost. What was the real walk-away? What trade was available that you could not see because you were tugging a rope? That autopsy is worth more than a second read of Getting to Yes.

Mission

Field Mission

In the next 48 hours, fill the BATNA worksheet for one live negotiation — even if the conversation is next week.

If a conversation is already booked, use the 90-second package and send a written recap after.

Afterward, four lines:

  1. Walk-away
  2. Their interest
  3. The package
  4. Whether you would actually run the BATNA

A worksheet you will not look at in the room does not count. Print it or put it on one phone screen.
