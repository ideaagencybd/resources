Case 9 — The Team Member Who Wants to Leave

404 words

The Situation

A high-performer on your team tells you privately: 'I think I'm going to start looking. Not urgent. But I wanted to be honest with you.'

Your Move — Decide Before Reading On

Option A

Panic-counter. Immediately promise a promotion, a raise, and a new project.

What likely happens:

They stay for six months, then leave anyway because your response confirmed you hadn't been serious about their development before the exit signal. Colleagues see that the way to get a raise is to threaten to leave. Culture damaged.

What you gained:

Six months. Short-term retention.

What you missed / what could go wrong:

The person still leaves. Your credibility with them and with your team is damaged.

What you should have noticed:

Counter-offers usually fail. Retention through panic is not retention.

Option B

Thank them for telling you. Genuinely explore what would make them stay — including the answer 'nothing.' Have the conversation without pressure.

What likely happens:

One of three: (1) you discover a concrete problem you can solve, and they stay — for real reasons; (2) they leave, but on good terms, and they refer future talent to you; (3) you learn something you can use to prevent the next such conversation.

What you gained:

Trust. Honest information. Even in loss, a preserved relationship.

What you missed / what could go wrong:

Some emotional intensity. Real risk of losing the person.

What you should have noticed:

The person told you HONESTLY. Honor that. Match their honesty with yours.

Option C

Immediately mark them as untrusted. Reassign their key work. Prepare for their exit.

What likely happens:

They notice the change within a week. They accelerate their departure. They tell their network. Your future hires ask questions in reverse-reference calls that make it clear they were warned about you.

What you gained:

Operational continuity if you can get the reassignment right.

What you missed / what could go wrong:

The trust of every remaining team member, who watched. Your reputation.

What you should have noticed:

Punishing honesty is the fastest way to end all future honesty from the people reporting to you.

The Wrap

B, always. The team member did something rare and professional — gave you warning. Treat that gift with the seriousness it deserves. Have the real conversation. Sometimes you'll retain them for real reasons. Sometimes you'll lose them, but keep the relationship. Both outcomes are better than the alternatives.
