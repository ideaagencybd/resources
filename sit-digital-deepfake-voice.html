A call from a family member asking urgently for money

high The Callback Rulefraudaifamilyimpersonation
1

What is happening

Your daughter, your son, your mother — the voice is right. They are in trouble: an accident, an arrest, a phone lost abroad, a hospital. They need money now and they cannot talk long.

2

What is actually going on

  • Voices can now be cloned convincingly from a few seconds of public audio. The voice sounding right is no longer evidence of anything.
  • The script always contains the same three elements: distress, urgency, and a reason you cannot verify — new number, broken phone, cannot talk, do not tell dad.
  • Distress bypasses verification. Parents and grandparents are targeted precisely because love makes deliberation feel like betrayal.
  • The payment method is the tell: transfer to a new account, crypto, vouchers, or a courier collecting cash. Real emergencies do not need these.
3

Do this, in this order

  1. Hang up and call them on their known numberThe number in your phone, not the one that called you. This single step defeats nearly every version of this scam.
  2. Ask a question no clone can answerNot a birthday — something only they would know: what we called the old car, what happened at the caravan. Agree such a phrase with your family in advance.
  3. Send no money on this callWhatever the story. If it is genuine, ten minutes changes nothing; if it is not, ten minutes saves everything.
  4. Break the secrecy instruction immediatelyCall another family member. "Do not tell anyone" exists solely to stop this call happening.
  5. If they claim to be police, lawyer, or hospital, verify independentlyLook up the institution and ring the main switchboard. Never a number they supply.
  6. Keep the evidenceNumber, time, account details, any recording. Needed for the bank and the report.
4

Words you can borrow

The safe deflection
"I am going to hang up and call you back on your usual number. If you are really in trouble, you will still be there."
The verification question
"Before we go on — what did we call Grandad's dog?"
Against secrecy
"I am going to tell your mother. If that is a problem, I know what this is."
Refusing the method
"I do not transfer money to new accounts on a phone call. Not ever."
5

What makes it worse

  • Confirming names or details they fish for. "Is that you, Sarah?" hands them the script.
  • Transfers, crypto, vouchers, or handing cash to a courier.
  • Staying on the line "so it can be sorted." Keeping you on the line is a technique.
  • Feeling ashamed for hesitating. Verification is love, not doubt.
6

Once the pressure drops

  • Agree a family code word today, out loud, with everyone. It costs one minute and it ends this entire category of attack.
  • If money moved, call the bank immediately and ask for a recall attempt.
  • Report it — these operations run at industrial scale and reports drive disruption.
  • Warn older relatives with the actual script, not a general warning. Specifics are what stick.
A note on this library

These are general principles written to be useful under pressure, not legal, medical, or financial advice. Laws and emergency procedures differ by country. If life is at risk, contact your emergency services first and read this afterwards.
