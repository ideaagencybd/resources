Situation 6 — The Vendor Who Overpromises and Underdelivers

263 words

The Situation

A vendor sold you on aggressive delivery. Three months in, they're missing dates and providing thin explanations. Your leadership is asking why the project is late.

What Most People Do

Advocate for the vendor internally ('they're working on it'). Give them one more chance, then another. Blame yourself for not being clearer.

Why It Often Fails

You inherit the failure of a supplier you're defending. Your leadership loses confidence in your judgment.

The Resourceful Response

Escalate professionally with the vendor. Document. Force a decision. Report cleanly upward.

Words To Use

"I want to be direct about where we are. When we signed, you committed to X. The current pace suggests we won't hit X or Y. I need to understand — is that a resource issue, a technical issue, or a scoping issue?"

"Based on your answer, we have three options: [1] we restructure the timeline realistically; [2] you add the resources needed to hit the original date; [3] we part ways. Which conversation do you want to have?"

"For our internal leadership, I need to know your answer by [specific date] so I can plan honestly."

Words To Avoid

"I'm sure it'll work out."

"Let's give them another month."

"It's not really that bad."

What Can Still Go Wrong

The vendor may respond well and course-correct. They may also stall and hope you'll continue to accept excuses. The moment you sense the second, act on option 3 — even though switching vendors mid-project is painful. The sunk-cost fallacy has probably kept more bad relationships alive than any other business error.
