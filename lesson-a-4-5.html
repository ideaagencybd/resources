Feedback That Lands

Specific, behavioral, usable

22 minSBI3,805 words22 sections
Skill
Give and receive feedback without theater
Tool
SBI
Result
You can say the true thing without cruelty
In one line
Situation, behavior, impact. Then a question.
The idea

The Hook

"You need to be more professional."

That sentence has ruined more Tuesdays than actual incompetence. It is a verdict wearing the mask of help. The person who hears it cannot use it. They can only defend a self. The person who said it feels brave. They have not been brave. They have been vague.

The other failure is the opposite: you have the true thing, and you sand it until it is a compliment with a tiny pebble in it. They hear the compliment. The pebble goes home with you and comes back as resentment in a performance review.

Feedback that lands is specific, behavioral, and usable. It is not a personality transplant. It is not a license to be rude and call the rudeness "candor." It is a small, factual gift with a cost: you have to have seen something, and you have to be willing to be wrong about the impact.

The room

The Scenario

Monday, 2:10 p.m. You are Jonah's lead on a client call. Jonah is technically right. He is also socially blunt.

The client says the timeline is tight. Jonah replies, on the live call:

"That's because your legal team sat on the contract for three weeks. We can't magic that back."

Silence. The client laughs a laugh that is not a laugh. After the call, Elena pings you: "Handle Jonah. I am tired of being the adult."

You have handled Jonah with hints. You have said "maybe tone it down." You have laughed with him afterward, which he took as permission. The promotion you want (4.2) will include "can you develop people" as a silent test.

Jonah is at your desk, already explaining why he was right.

What do you say that he can actually use?

Your move

Your Move

Do not keep reading yet. Write the sentence you would say to Jonah. Then write the sentence you would want, if the client had just called you unprofessional.

Common move. Character. "You're being a jerk on client calls. You have to be more professional." He will hear "you are a jerk." He will produce a brief on why legal did sit on the contract. You will both be right and the next call will be the same.

Better move. The sandwich. Compliment, pebble, compliment. "Great technical point, maybe watch the tone, but we really need you." He will eat the bread. The meat is gone.

Expert move. SBI, then a question. "On the call just now, when the client said the timeline was tight, you said their legal team sat on the contract and we can't magic it back. The impact: they went tight, Elena is now in a repair, and I can't put you on the next call in that mode. I need you to name the constraint without assigning blame in the room. How do you want to handle the next one?"

Resourceful move. Room Sheet first (is this the room? desk vs later). Pause, because you liked his sentence too, in a private way. Then SBI. Then you let him correct the facts. Then you do not collapse if he says you are over-sensitive. Receiving his counter-feedback is the other half of this lesson.

Why the common move fails: people cannot work on a trait in the next hour. They can work on a sentence.

What can go wrong with the expert move: SBI can become a prosecutor's stack of "impacts" that are actually your stories. "You made us look like children" is a story. "Elena is in a repair with the client" is closer to a fact. Keep the impact checkable.

The idea

The Big Idea

Say the situation, the behavior, the impact. Then ask a question that gives them their dignity back.

SBI is the tool. Receiving is the other skill: do not collapse, and do not lawyer. Radical Candor is a popular frame with a known failure mode — people use it as a license to be rude. We will steal the useful part and leave the swagger.

Explained

The Simple Explanation

The Center for Creative Leadership taught Situation–Behavior–Impact as a way to keep feedback in the observable world. The idea is older than the acronym: describe a time, a thing a camera could have seen, and a consequence, rather than a soul.

  • Situation. When and where. "On the 2:00 client call."
  • Behavior. What they said or did, in words a recording would confirm. Not "you were hostile." The sentence.
  • Impact. On the work, the client, the team, you. Prefer checkable impacts. Label feelings as yours: "I don't trust the next call yet" is more honest than "everyone was horrified," unless you actually know everyone was horrified.

Then a question, because feedback without a door is a verdict. "How do you see it?" is a real door if you will actually listen (4.3). "Don't you agree?" is a trap.

Receiving without collapsing or lawyering.

When it is your turn in the chair, two cheap moves show up:

  • Collapse. "You're right, I'm a disaster." This looks humble. It is often a way to make the other person comfort you, so the usable piece dies.
  • Lawyering. Point-by-point cross-examination of their memory, their tone, their right to speak. You may win the transcript. You will lose the information.

Stone and Heen's Thanks for the Feedback (2014) is a useful popular map: separate the switch (is there a true piece?), the volume (how it was said), and the relationship (what this person means). You can reject the volume and still take the switch. You can reject a false verdict ("sloppy," 4.3) and still take a process miss.

A decent receiving sequence:

  1. Pause.
  2. Accurate Mirror of their SBI.
  3. "Give me a minute to sort what's true."
  4. Own the piece that is true. Be specific.
  5. If you need facts, book a time. Do not hold a trial in the doorway.
  6. Ask for one example if they were vague. You are allowed to need SBI from them too.

Radical Candor. Kim Scott's popular frame (2017) says care personally, challenge directly. The useful part: do not use "nice" as a way to abandon people, and do not use "direct" as a way to not care. The failure mode is well known in the wild: people skip the care, keep the challenge, and call the result a brand. If your SBI would embarrass you if posted, it is not candor. It is a status move. Machiavelli would recognize the incentive. Do not take him as a coach.

Unfair criticism belongs here too. SBI is how you give. The Accurate Mirror is how you receive the unfair part long enough to find the fair piece. You do not have to accept a character assassination to accept a bug.

The tool

The Practical Tool

Name: SBI

Giving

  1. Ask if it is a usable moment. "I have a specific note from the call. Is now all right?" If they are shaking, later.
  2. Situation. One time. Not "you always."
  3. Behavior. Camera words. Their sentence, their action.
  4. Impact. Work first, feelings labeled as yours, stories labeled as stories.
  5. Ask. "How do you see it?" Then actually mirror.
  6. Need / Next. One change, observable, soon. "On the next call, name the delay without blaming legal in the room. I'll back you if they push."

Receiving

  1. Pause. Shoulders.
  2. Mirror. "The sentence on the call, and the impact on the client and Elena."
  3. Sort: true / unsure / false.
  4. Own the true piece in a sentence that could be quoted.
  5. If unfair: "I can own X. I don't accept Y. I want an example of Y if we're going to work on it."

Pocket card:

When / What A Camera Saw / What It Did / Question.

A worked rewrite:

VagueSBI
"Be more professional.""On the 2:00 call, when they said the timeline was tight, you said their legal team sat on the contract and we can't magic it back. They went quiet. I now have a repair to do, and I need a different sentence next time."
"You're not leadership material."Too big. Split or don't say it. If you must: "In the last two steering meetings you didn't speak until the last two minutes, and the VP left without your risk. The impact is they don't have you as an owner. I want you to take the risk slide next Thursday."
"Great job, just a small thing, but you're amazing."Drop the bread. Say the small thing. If there is also genuine praise, give it at another hour so it can be believed.
Fast path

The Fastest Reliable Method

When you have ninety seconds in a hallway:

One SBI, one need, stop.

"On the call, that sentence about their legal team. It landed as blame. I need you to name the delay without the villain. I'll take the repair. We'll debrief at 4:00."

If you are receiving in a hallway: "I want to get this right. The sentence on the call — yes. 'Not at the level' — I need a proper time, not a doorway. 4:00?"

The hack

The Hack

Write the SBI down before you speak, in three lines. If you cannot write the behavior without an adjective, you do not have it yet.

If you are about to give feedback by chat, the hack is to ask for a voice call unless the note is tiny and logistical. Chat strips face. Face-stripping turns SBI into a prosecution exhibit.

AI move

The AI Move

AI is good at stripping adjectives and finding the camera sentence. It is bad at knowing what actually happened. If you feed it your anger, it will write a fluent SBI of your story.

Use AI for:

  • "Rewrite this as SBI. Flag every word a camera could not see."
  • A receiving draft: mirror, own, reject, ask
  • A check: "Where is this rude and calling itself candor?"

Do not use AI for:

  • A pile-on document about a colleague
  • Generating "everyone thinks" claims
  • HR-sensitive content you would not put in an email

What to verify: The impact. Models love "this damaged trust" as a universal spice. Maybe it did. Maybe the client was fine and Elena is tired. Check.

Do not paste: performance ratings, compensation, medical, a recording without consent.

Tech

The Technology Move

The job is "keep it specific and recoverable."

Current examples of the category:

  • A private note with three lines of SBI before the meeting
  • A shared working agreement for calls ("no villains in the client room")
  • Afterward, a two-line recap so the feedback is not only air
  • 360 tools and anonymous forms: use with suspicion. Anonymity increases honesty and also cruelty. If you lead, do not outsource a relationship to a portal.

When not to use technology: a first hard piece of feedback about a human's dignity. Voice. Face if you can. The exception is written culture where written is respect — then write SBI, not a novel.

Mistake

The Common Mistake

Radical Candor as a mood. You skip care, you keep the edge, you tell yourself the problem is that other people are snowflakes. Sometimes other people are snowflakes. More often you wanted the status of being "the honest one."

The receiving mistake: lawyering the example because if this example falls, the identity stays intact. One example can be wrong and the pattern can still be true. Ask for a second example if you need it. Do not make the first example a murder trial.

Recovery: if you already said "unprofessional," go back. "That word is too big. Let me do it properly. Situation, sentence, impact…" If you already collapsed, stand up: "I went to a verdict on myself. I want the usable piece. Give me the example again."

Defense

The Defense

SBI fails when:

  • You do not have a situation. Then you do not have feedback. You have a vibe. Wait until you have a camera sentence, or say "I only have a vibe, and I don't trust it yet."
  • The power gap is huge and they cannot safely answer your question. Then you still give SBI, but you own more of the next step, and you do not pretend it was a dialogue of equals.
  • The person uses your question to filibuster. Then: "I can hear your view at 4:00. The need for the next call is still X."
  • You are in a culture that will punish written SBI. Then you speak, and you document for yourself.

Limitation: this will not make Jonah warm. It will make the next call a place you can put him, or a place you cannot, based on behavior. That is a management decision, not a feeling.

Council

The Masters' Table

Kim Scott. Through care + challenge. Application: the Jonah note should come from someone who will still back him on the technical point. Failure mode: challenge without care, branded as virtue.

Stone and Heen. Through the receiving side: identity, switch, relationship. Application: Jonah will hear "you are a jerk" unless you keep the camera. You, receiving Elena, will hear "you cannot lead" unless you sort the pieces.

Aristotle. Through ethos: your feedback only lands if you are a credible, non-random source. If you laugh at his joke and then prosecute it, your ethos is mush. Application: stop sending mixed rewards.

Carnegie. Through not beginning with a character attack — with the caution. Application: SBI is respect. Flattery as a sandwich is not.

Machiavelli. Through incentives: if bluntness is rewarded as "authenticity," you will get more of it. If you are Elena, what you laugh at in the hallway is the real policy. Application: change the reward, not only the speech.

Synthesis. The council agrees that usable feedback is specific, and that identity is the landmine. They split on how much heat is allowed. You will have to choose a heat you can stand behind tomorrow.

Where the council could be wrong. Some people need a stronger identity challenge than SBI offers — a pattern, a career truth. SBI can become a way to nibble around a firing you are avoiding. If the true thing is "I cannot put you in front of clients," that can still be said as situation, behavior, impact, and a decision. Do not hide a decision in coaching theater.

Best move

The Best-Possible Response

Back to the desk.

Calm. You thought his sentence was a little bit funny. That is part of why you did not handle it. Pause.

Observe. Camera fact: the sentence. Client laugh. Elena ping. Jonah already defending.

Define. The job is one usable change for the next call, not a personality review, not a friendship trial.

Verify. You heard the sentence. You do not know the client's private reaction. Do not fake "the client is furious" if you mean "Elena is tired and I am worried."

Prioritize. SBI, question, need. Repair with the client is a separate act, maybe yours today.

Find resources / people. Elena, briefly, so she knows you handled it. A working agreement for calls.

Options. Hint. Sandwich. Character. SBI. Email so you can be "precise" (usually colder).

Risks. He lawyers legal's delay (partly true). You fold. He tells the team you "can't take honesty."

Choose. SBI at the desk, short, then a time if he wants the trial.

Communicate. "I want to keep you on these calls. On the 2:00, when they said the timeline was tight, you said their legal sat on the contract and we can't magic it back. Impact: they went tight, Elena is in a repair, and I don't want that sentence in the room. The delay can be true and still not be a villain in front of them. I need you to name the constraint without blame. How do you see it?"

Act. Listen. Mirror. If he is right about the delay, own that you will help get legal out of the critical path. Do not trade that for keeping the villain sentence.

Document. Two lines to yourself. One line to Elena: handled, the need, the next call as the test.

Adapt. If he repeats it next time, this is no longer feedback. It is a staffing decision.

Reflect. Did you use a trait word? Did you laugh in the hallway last month?

Practice

Practice

Do this now. Twelve minutes.

  1. Write the last piece of feedback you gave or avoided. If it has a trait word, circle it.
  2. Rewrite as SBI. Three lines. A question.
  3. Write the last piece you received. Sort: true / unsure / false. Write a receiving response that does not collapse or lawyer.
  4. Rewrite "be more professional" for a real person in your life. If you cannot find a camera sentence, you are not ready to say it.
  5. Say the Jonah SBI out loud until it is not a speech.

Then the receiving drill: have a willing person give you a slightly unfair note. Practice the mirror and the sort out loud. This is a gym.

Real life

Real-Life Challenge

Work — unfair criticism, receiving. Situation: "You're not leadership material" or "that was sloppy," with one thin example or none. What most people do: collapse in the room, lawyer in the car. Why it fails: you either swallow a false identity or you fight the volume and miss the switch. Resourceful response: mirror, sort, own the true piece, ask for SBI on the rest, book a time if it is a career sentence. Words to use: "Give me the situation and the behavior. I can own X. I don't accept Y without an example." Words to avoid: "That's not fair" as the whole reply; a fake thank-you that you do not mean. Tool: SBI on the way in; Accurate Mirror on the way in; Pause. AI assist: draft the receiving reply, then strip groveling. What could go wrong: you ask for examples as a stall. If they give one, work it.

Work — giving it to the blunt expert. Situation: Jonah, or anyone whose truth-telling is useful and whose delivery is expensive. What most people do: protect them because they are "the talent." Why it fails: the room trains the behavior. Then you are surprised at a firing. Resourceful response: SBI, a working rule, a next-call test. Words to use: the sentence, the impact, the next behavior. Words to avoid: "jerk," "always," a joke that undoes it. Tool: SBI. Room Sheet for public vs private. What could go wrong: you do it in front of the team to show Elena you are tough. That is status, not feedback.

Family — a specific complaint. Situation: a partner's comment in front of friends. What most people do: "you're so embarrassing" at 11:00 p.m. Why it fails: trait, no situation, no usable next. Resourceful response: "At dinner, when you told the story about my boss, I went small. I need that story to stay home. Can we do that?" Words to avoid: a personality profile, a list from 2019.

Bad room

Bad-Room Version

Elena says, with Jonah present: "Tell him. He was unprofessional. I want to hear you say it."

This is no longer only Jonah's feedback. It is a status scene.

Pause. You will not humiliate him to perform loyalty.

"I'll handle the specific with Jonah. The camera version: on the call, the sentence about their legal team. That's the behavior. Impact is a client repair, which I'll take today. Jonah, I'll do the usable version with you at 3:00, not as a show. Elena, you'll have the next-call rule by end of day."

You have not refused Elena. You have refused a hanging. At 3:00 you do real SBI. If Elena needed a show, you have information about her, which is a different difficult conversation (4.4).

If Jonah smirks, you still do not pile on. The smirk can be an SBI later: "When I set the 3:00, you smirked in front of Elena. Impact: she now thinks you are not taking it. I need that not to happen."

Reflect

Reflection

  • Which trait words do you use when you are afraid to be specific?
  • When you receive, do you collapse or lawyer — and who taught you that?
  • Where have you used "candor" as a license?
  • What feedback have you been hinting, and what is the camera sentence?
  • What would it mean to thank someone for a usable piece you did not want?
Remember

Remember This

If a camera could not see it, it is not ready to say. If you cannot own any piece of it, you are not yet receiving.

Deep dive

Optional Deep Dive

A short caution about feedback culture.

After Jack Welch, after "radical candor," after every company that posted values on a wall, there is a repeating failure: systems that reward the appearance of honesty produce cruelty with a glossary. Research on feedback and performance is mixed, large, and context-heavy; this course will not pretend a single meta-analysis tells you how to talk to Jonah. The operational claim is modest: specific beats vague, behavior beats trait, a question beats a hanging, and the receiver's identity is the landmine.

On 360 reviews: they can surface patterns a manager cannot see. They can also become a gossip machine with a login. If you use them, require examples. If you receive them, sort switch / volume / relationship. Do not let an anonymous adjective become your autobiography.

On "feedback is a gift": sometimes it is. Sometimes it is a status move. The Room Sheet (4.1) and incentives (Machiavelli as map) help you tell. You can take a gift from a person you do not fully trust if the camera sentence is true. You do not have to take their story about your soul.

If you want a practice beyond this lesson, for two weeks, refuse to give any feedback that you have not written as SBI first, even if you then speak it. Count how many times the writing deletes the trait word. That deletion is the work.

Mission

Field Mission

In the next 48 hours, do one of these, for real:

  • Give one piece of SBI you have been hinting
  • Or receive one piece without collapsing or lawyering, out loud

Afterward, four lines:

  1. Situation
  2. Behavior (camera)
  3. Impact (checkable)
  4. What you asked, or what you owned

Hints and sandwiches do not count.
